FAQ Hip Pain

Frequently Asked Questions About Hip Pain

Hip pain is a common complaint among active adults, athletes, and individuals simply trying to maintain mobility and independence.

Because the hip is involved in walking, exercising, climbing stairs, golfing, pickleball, travel, and countless daily activities, even mild hip pain can become frustrating.

Below are some of the most common questions patients ask about hip pain.

Why Does My Hip Hurt?

Hip pain may originate from several different structures, including:

  • The hip joint
  • Cartilage
  • Labrum
  • Tendons
  • Muscles
  • Bursa
  • Ligaments
  • The lower back
  • The sacroiliac joint

An accurate diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

Where Is Hip Pain Usually Felt?

The location of symptoms often provides important clues.

Groin Pain

May be associated with the hip joint itself.

Outer Hip Pain

Often involves structures surrounding the greater trochanter.

Buttock Pain

May originate from the hip, pelvis, sacroiliac joint, or spine.

Thigh Pain

Some hip conditions may create symptoms extending into the thigh.

Because symptoms overlap, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

Why Does My Hip Hurt When I Walk?

Walking places force through the hip joint and surrounding structures.

Several conditions may contribute, including:

  • Hip arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Hip bursitis
  • Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome
  • Labral injuries

The specific cause varies from patient to patient.

Why Does My Hip Hurt At Night?

Night pain is a common complaint among patients with hip conditions.

Many patients report:

  • Difficulty sleeping on one side
  • Pain when rolling over
  • Disrupted sleep

The location of symptoms often provides important diagnostic clues.

Why Does My Hip Hurt When I Sleep On My Side?

Pain while side sleeping is commonly associated with conditions affecting the outer hip, including:

  • Hip bursitis
  • Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (GTPS)
  • Tendon-related conditions

Direct pressure on irritated structures may aggravate symptoms.

Why Does My Hip Feel Stiff?

Hip stiffness is commonly associated with:

  • Hip arthritis
  • Mobility restrictions
  • Joint conditions
  • Previous injuries

Patients often notice stiffness while:

  • Getting out of a car
  • Putting on shoes
  • Crossing their legs
  • Squatting

Why Does My Hip Hurt When I Get Out Of A Car?

Many hip conditions may cause discomfort during:

  • Hip flexion
  • Rotation
  • Weight transfer

This is a common complaint among patients with hip arthritis and other joint-related conditions.

Is My Pain Coming From My Hip Or My Back?

This is one of the most common questions patients ask.

Hip conditions and spine conditions frequently create similar symptoms.

Pain may originate from:

  • The hip joint
  • The lumbar spine
  • The sacroiliac joint
  • Pelvic structures

A comprehensive evaluation often helps clarify the source.

What Is Hip Arthritis?

Hip arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the hip.

Common symptoms include:

  • Groin pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Difficulty walking

Not every patient with arthritis experiences severe symptoms.

Does Hip Arthritis Mean I Need A Hip Replacement?

No.

Many patients successfully manage hip arthritis without surgery.

Treatment recommendations should always be individualized.

Can I Exercise With Hip Arthritis?

Many individuals remain active despite hip arthritis.

Exercise recommendations should be tailored to:

  • Symptoms
  • Function
  • Activity goals
  • Overall health

Remaining active is often an important part of maintaining long-term mobility.

What Is Hip Bursitis?

Hip bursitis refers to irritation involving a bursa near the hip.

Patients commonly experience:

  • Outer hip pain
  • Tenderness
  • Pain while sleeping on the affected side
  • Pain during walking

What Is Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (GTPS)?

GTPS is a broader diagnosis involving structures around the outer hip.

Many patients diagnosed with "hip bursitis" actually have conditions that fall under the umbrella of GTPS.

What Is A Hip Labral Tear?

A hip labral tear is an injury involving the cartilage ring that surrounds the socket of the hip.

Common symptoms may include:

  • Groin pain
  • Clicking
  • Catching
  • Hip stiffness
  • Activity-related discomfort

Can I Have A Labral Tear Without Pain?

Yes.

MRI studies frequently identify labral tears in individuals who have minimal symptoms or no symptoms at all.

Imaging findings must always be interpreted within the context of the patient.

Does Every Labral Tear Require Surgery?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Overall hip health

Every patient deserves an individualized discussion regarding available options.

Why Does My Hip Click Or Pop?

Clicking and popping are common.

Many individuals experience these sensations without significant injury.

However, clicking associated with:

  • Pain
  • Catching
  • Locking
  • Functional limitations

may warrant evaluation.

Is Hip Popping Normal?

In many cases, yes.

Occasional popping without symptoms is often not concerning.

When popping is accompanied by discomfort or dysfunction, further evaluation may be appropriate.

Can Running Cause Hip Pain?

Running places repetitive forces through the hip.

Several conditions may contribute to symptoms in runners, including:

  • Tendon disorders
  • Labral pathology
  • GTPS
  • Arthritis
  • Mobility restrictions

Many runners remain active with appropriate evaluation and treatment strategies.

Can Pickleball Cause Hip Pain?

Yes.

Pickleball involves:

  • Rotational movements
  • Quick directional changes
  • Repetitive loading

which may contribute to symptoms in some individuals.

Can Golf Cause Hip Pain?

Yes.

The golf swing places rotational stress on the hips and surrounding structures.

Pain may develop from several different conditions depending on the individual.

Do I Need An MRI?

Not always.

Many hip conditions can be evaluated effectively through:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • X-rays

MRI studies may be appropriate in selected situations.

What Is The Difference Between An MRI And X-Ray?

X-Ray

Primarily evaluates:

  • Bone
  • Joint space
  • Arthritis

MRI

Provides additional information regarding:

  • Labrum
  • Tendons
  • Cartilage
  • Muscles
  • Soft tissues

Both studies may play important roles in the diagnostic process.

Can PRP Help Hip Pain?

PRP is one of several treatment options that may be discussed depending on the diagnosis and individual patient circumstances.

Treatment decisions should always be based on a comprehensive evaluation.

When Should I See A Specialist For Hip Pain?

You should consider evaluation if:

  • Pain persists.
  • Walking becomes difficult.
  • Sleep is affected.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Mobility decreases.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.
