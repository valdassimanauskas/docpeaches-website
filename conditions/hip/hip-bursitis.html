Hip Bursitis

Understanding Hip Bursitis: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Hip bursitis is one of the most common causes of pain on the outside of the hip.

Many patients describe symptoms such as:

  • Pain when lying on one side
  • Pain walking long distances
  • Discomfort climbing stairs
  • Tenderness over the outer hip
  • Pain during exercise

The condition can affect athletes, active adults, and individuals who are simply trying to stay mobile and independent.

While the term hip bursitis is commonly used, it is important to understand that outer hip pain may involve multiple structures around the hip.

This is one reason why obtaining an accurate diagnosis is so important.

What Is A Bursa?

A bursa is a small fluid-filled structure that helps reduce friction between tissues.

Bursae are located throughout the body and are commonly found near:

  • Tendons
  • Muscles
  • Bones
  • Joints

Their purpose is to help structures move smoothly during activity.

What Is Hip Bursitis?

Hip bursitis refers to irritation involving one of the bursae located near the hip.

The most commonly discussed bursa is located near the outer aspect of the hip around the greater trochanter.

When this area becomes irritated, patients may develop:

  • Pain
  • Tenderness
  • Activity-related discomfort
  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side

Symptoms can range from mild to severe.

What Causes Hip Bursitis?

Several factors may contribute, including:

Repetitive Activity

Walking, running, and sports participation may increase stress on the hip.

Prolonged Standing

Certain occupations require extended periods on the feet.

Sports Participation

Activities such as:

  • Running
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Golf
  • Hiking

may place additional stress on the hip.

Biomechanical Factors

Movement patterns and muscle imbalances may contribute.

Previous Injuries

Past injuries may alter how forces are distributed through the hip.

Often, multiple factors are involved.

Common Symptoms Of Hip Bursitis

Patients may experience:

  • Outer hip pain
  • Tenderness when touching the hip
  • Pain walking
  • Pain climbing stairs
  • Pain exercising
  • Pain after prolonged activity
  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side

Symptoms are often aggravated by direct pressure over the outer hip.

Why Does My Hip Hurt When I Sleep On My Side?

One of the most common complaints associated with hip bursitis is:

"I can't sleep on that side anymore."

Pressure placed directly over the irritated structures may increase discomfort and make side sleeping difficult.

This symptom frequently brings patients into the office for evaluation.

Is Hip Bursitis The Same As GTPS?

Not exactly.

Many patients use the terms interchangeably, but there is an important distinction.

Hip Bursitis

Refers specifically to irritation involving a bursa.

Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome (GTPS)

Refers to a broader group of conditions affecting the outer hip.

Many patients diagnosed with hip bursitis may actually have a combination of tendon-related and soft tissue conditions that fall under the umbrella of GTPS.

Hip Bursitis vs Hip Arthritis

Patients often confuse these conditions because both may cause hip pain.

Hip Bursitis

Usually causes pain along the outside of the hip.

Hip Arthritis

Typically causes pain within the joint and is often felt in the groin.

Because symptoms can overlap, proper evaluation is important.

Who Gets Hip Bursitis?

Hip bursitis may affect:

  • Active adults
  • Athletes
  • Runners
  • Walkers
  • Pickleball players
  • Golfers
  • Individuals with physically demanding jobs

Women are commonly affected, although the condition can occur in anyone.

How Is Hip Bursitis Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to determine which structures are contributing to symptoms.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Strengthening programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Exercise progression strategies
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

Can You Continue Exercising?

Many patients remain active while managing hip bursitis.

The appropriate approach depends on:

  • Symptoms
  • Activity type
  • Functional limitations
  • Individual goals

The objective is often to help patients remain active while addressing the factors contributing to symptoms.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Outer hip pain persists.
  • Sleeping on your side becomes difficult.
  • Walking aggravates symptoms.
  • Exercise participation is limited.
  • Stairs become painful.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward improvement.
