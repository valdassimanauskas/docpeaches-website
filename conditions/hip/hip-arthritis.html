Hip Arthritis

Understanding Hip Arthritis: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Hip arthritis is one of the most common causes of hip pain and stiffness in adults.

Many patients first learn they have arthritis after an X-ray, MRI, or evaluation for persistent groin pain, hip stiffness, or difficulty walking.

For some, the diagnosis provides relief because they finally understand why their hip hurts.

For others, the diagnosis creates anxiety because they immediately think:

"Does this mean I need a hip replacement?"

The answer is not necessarily.

One of the most important things patients should understand is:

Arthritis is common. Arthritis does not automatically mean surgery.

Many individuals with hip arthritis continue to live active, healthy, and productive lives for years.

The key is understanding the diagnosis and developing an individualized treatment strategy.

What Is Hip Arthritis?

Hip arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the hip.

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint designed to provide:

  • Stability
  • Strength
  • Mobility
  • Shock absorption

Healthy cartilage allows the hip to move smoothly.

As joint changes occur over time, patients may develop:

  • Pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Functional limitations

The severity of symptoms varies considerably from person to person.

What Causes Hip Arthritis?

Several factors may contribute to hip arthritis, including:

Age-Related Changes

Joint changes become more common over time.

Previous Injuries

Past injuries may affect the long-term health of the hip.

Sports Participation

Years of repetitive loading may contribute to joint wear.

Genetics

Some individuals may have a greater predisposition to developing arthritis.

Biomechanical Factors

Joint structure and movement patterns may influence stress on the hip.

Occupational Demands

Certain activities may place repeated stress on the joint.

Most patients develop arthritis from a combination of factors rather than a single cause.

Common Symptoms Of Hip Arthritis

Patients may experience:

  • Groin pain
  • Hip stiffness
  • Difficulty walking
  • Pain during exercise
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Difficulty putting on shoes or socks
  • Pain getting in and out of a car
  • Difficulty climbing stairs
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms often develop gradually over time.

Where Is Hip Arthritis Pain Usually Felt?

One of the most common locations is:

The Groin

Many patients point directly to the front of the hip or groin region when describing their symptoms.

Pain may also be felt in:

  • The front of the thigh
  • The buttock
  • The side of the hip
  • The knee

This is one reason hip arthritis is sometimes confused with other conditions.

Why Does My Hip Feel Stiff?

Stiffness is one of the hallmark symptoms of hip arthritis.

Patients frequently notice difficulty:

  • Crossing their legs
  • Tying their shoes
  • Getting into a car
  • Squatting
  • Rotating the hip

As mobility decreases, daily activities may become increasingly challenging.

Does Arthritis Always Cause Pain?

No.

This is one of the most important concepts patients should understand.

Many people have arthritis visible on imaging studies and experience relatively few symptoms.

Others experience substantial discomfort despite modest imaging findings.

This is why physicians evaluate:

  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Physical examination findings
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging studies

together.

The X-ray is important.

The patient is more important.

How Is Hip Arthritis Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Movement assessment
  • Functional evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to understand how imaging findings relate to symptoms and quality of life.

Hip Arthritis vs Hip Bursitis

Patients frequently confuse these conditions.

Hip Arthritis

Typically involves the hip joint itself.

Hip Bursitis

Often involves structures located on the outer aspect of the hip.

Although symptoms may overlap, they are different diagnoses and may require different treatment approaches.

Can You Exercise With Hip Arthritis?

In many cases, yes.

Remaining active is often an important component of maintaining mobility, strength, and overall health.

Exercise recommendations should be individualized based on:

  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Overall health

The objective is often to help patients remain active whenever possible.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings
  • Overall health

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Strength training
  • Weight management strategies
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

Does Hip Arthritis Always Lead To Hip Replacement?

No.

Many patients assume arthritis automatically means surgery.

The reality is more complex.

Many individuals successfully manage arthritis for years while maintaining active lifestyles.

When surgery becomes appropriate, it should be considered within the context of the patient's symptoms, goals, and overall health.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Hip pain is becoming more frequent.
  • Walking becomes difficult.
  • Stiffness is worsening.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Daily activities are becoming more challenging.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is hip arthritis?

Hip arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the hip joint.

Where is hip arthritis pain usually felt?

Most commonly in the groin, although pain may also be felt in the thigh, buttock, or even the knee.

Why does my hip feel stiff?

Can I continue exercising with hip arthritis?

Many individuals remain active, although exercise recommendations should be individualized.

No.
