Hip Pain

Understanding Hip Pain: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Hip pain is one of the most common musculoskeletal complaints affecting active adults, athletes, and individuals simply trying to stay mobile and independent.

Whether you're walking the golf course, playing pickleball, training in the gym, hiking with family, or simply getting in and out of a car, the hip plays a critical role in nearly every movement you make.

Because the hip is such an important weight-bearing joint, pain can significantly impact quality of life, mobility, and overall function.

The good news is that not all hip pain is the same.

Understanding the source of your symptoms is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Causes Hip Pain?

Hip pain is a symptom, not a diagnosis.

Several different structures may contribute to symptoms, including:

Hip Arthritis

One of the most common causes of hip pain in adults.

Hip Bursitis

Irritation involving structures on the outside of the hip.

Greater Trochanteric Pain Syndrome

A common cause of pain along the outer hip.

Hip Labral Tears

Injuries involving cartilage structures within the hip joint.

Tendon Conditions

Irritation or injury involving muscles and tendons surrounding the hip.

Sports Injuries

Acute and repetitive stress injuries affecting athletes and active individuals.

Referred Pain

Some symptoms that feel like hip pain may actually originate from the lower back, pelvis, or sacroiliac joint.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the true source of symptoms.

Where Is Your Hip Pain Located?

The location of pain often provides important clues.

Pain In The Groin

May be associated with conditions involving the hip joint itself.

Pain On The Outside Of The Hip

Often associated with structures near the greater trochanter.

Pain In The Buttock

May originate from the hip, sacroiliac joint, or lower back.

Pain Traveling Down The Leg

May involve the hip, nerves, or other structures.

Because symptoms frequently overlap, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

Common Symptoms Of Hip Pain

Patients may experience:

  • Groin pain
  • Outer hip pain
  • Buttock pain
  • Stiffness
  • Difficulty walking
  • Pain during exercise
  • Pain getting in and out of a car
  • Difficulty climbing stairs
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms vary depending on the underlying condition.

Why Does My Hip Hurt When I Walk?

Walking places significant force through the hip joint and surrounding structures.

Pain during walking may be associated with several conditions, including:

  • Arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Bursitis
  • Labral injuries
  • Biomechanical issues

A thorough evaluation helps determine the underlying cause.

Hip Pain In Athletes

Athletes place unique demands on the hip.

Sports commonly associated with hip injuries include:

  • Golf
  • Tennis
  • Pickleball
  • Running
  • Soccer
  • Hockey
  • CrossFit
  • Weightlifting

Athletic hip pain may result from:

  • Acute injuries
  • Repetitive stress
  • Mobility limitations
  • Overuse
  • Movement pattern dysfunction

Understanding the specific demands of the athlete is an important part of the evaluation process.

Is It My Hip Or My Back?

One of the most common questions patients ask is:

"How do I know whether my pain is coming from my hip or my back?"

This can be surprisingly difficult to determine.

Conditions involving the:

  • Hip joint
  • Lower back
  • Sacroiliac joint
  • Pelvis

may create similar symptoms.

This is why a comprehensive evaluation is often necessary before deciding on a treatment strategy.

How Is Hip Pain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and understand how they relate to the patient's goals and lifestyle.

Why Accurate Diagnosis Matters

The hip is surrounded by multiple structures capable of producing pain, including:

  • Cartilage
  • Labrum
  • Tendons
  • Muscles
  • Bursa
  • Bone
  • Joint surfaces

Because multiple conditions may create similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step in the treatment process.

The diagnosis guides the treatment strategy.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Strength training
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

No single treatment is appropriate for every patient.

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Hip pain persists.
  • Walking becomes uncomfortable.
  • Exercise is limited.
  • Stiffness is worsening.
  • Sports participation is affected.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward improvement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common cause of hip pain?

Several conditions may contribute, including arthritis, bursitis, tendon disorders, labral tears, and sports-related injuries.

How do I know if my pain is coming from my hip or my back?

Many hip and spine conditions create similar symptoms. A comprehensive evaluation often helps clarify the source.

Why does my hip hurt when I walk?

Walking places stress on the hip joint and surrounding structures. Several different conditions may contribute.

Do I need an MRI for hip pain?

Not always.

Imaging recommendations depend on symptoms, examination findings, and the suspected diagnosis.

Does hip pain always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.

Should I bring prior imaging to my consultation?

Yes.

Imaging studies and treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
