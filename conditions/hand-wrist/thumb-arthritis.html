Thumb Arthritis

Understanding Thumb Arthritis: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Thumb pain can make even the simplest daily activities difficult.

Patients frequently report discomfort while:

  • Opening jars
  • Turning doorknobs
  • Writing
  • Gripping objects
  • Using tools
  • Golfing
  • Playing pickleball

One of the most common causes of thumb pain in adults is thumb arthritis.

Because the thumb plays a critical role in grip strength and hand function, symptoms can significantly impact quality of life.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Is Thumb Arthritis?

Thumb arthritis refers to changes involving the joint located at the base of the thumb.

This joint helps allow:

  • Pinching
  • Gripping
  • Twisting
  • Lifting
  • Fine motor movements

Because the thumb is used throughout the day, this joint experiences substantial repetitive loading over time.

As changes occur within the joint, patients may develop symptoms that interfere with hand function.

What Causes Thumb Arthritis?

Several factors may contribute, including:

Age-Related Changes

Joint changes become more common as we age.

Repetitive Hand Use

Years of gripping, pinching, and hand-intensive activities may contribute.

Previous Injuries

Past injuries may affect long-term joint health.

Genetics

Some individuals may be predisposed to developing arthritis.

Occupational Demands

Certain occupations place substantial repetitive stress on the thumb joint.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of Thumb Arthritis

Patients may experience:

  • Pain at the base of the thumb
  • Stiffness
  • Weakness
  • Difficulty gripping
  • Difficulty opening jars
  • Difficulty turning keys
  • Swelling
  • Reduced hand function

Symptoms often develop gradually over time.

Where Is Thumb Arthritis Pain Usually Felt?

Most patients experience pain:

At The Base Of The Thumb

This is the most common location.

Pain may also extend into:

  • The hand
  • The wrist
  • The thumb itself

Because several conditions may cause thumb pain, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

Why Does It Hurt To Open Jars?

Opening jars requires substantial force through the thumb joint.

Patients with thumb arthritis frequently notice discomfort during:

  • Twisting motions
  • Pinching activities
  • Strong gripping tasks

This symptom is one of the most common reasons patients seek evaluation.

Why Does My Thumb Feel Weak?

The thumb plays a major role in grip strength.

As symptoms develop, patients may notice:

  • Difficulty gripping objects
  • Reduced pinch strength
  • Trouble opening containers
  • Fatigue during hand-intensive activities

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the source of weakness.

Can Thumb Arthritis Affect Sports?

Yes.

Athletes frequently rely on strong hand and thumb function.

Activities commonly affected include:

  • Golf
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Weightlifting
  • Cycling

Understanding the athlete's goals is an important part of treatment planning.

Thumb Arthritis vs Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Patients sometimes confuse these conditions because both affect the hand.

Thumb Arthritis

Primarily involves the joint at the base of the thumb.

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Involves irritation of the median nerve at the wrist.

The symptoms, causes, and treatment considerations are different.

A comprehensive evaluation helps establish the diagnosis.

How Is Thumb Arthritis Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to understand how imaging findings relate to symptoms and hand function.

Does Arthritis On An X-Ray Always Cause Symptoms?

No.

This is one of the most important concepts patients should understand.

Many individuals have arthritis visible on imaging studies without significant symptoms.

Others experience substantial discomfort despite relatively modest imaging findings.

Treatment decisions should always be based on the patient, not the X-ray alone.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings
  • Overall hand health

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Activity modifications
  • Splinting
  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Interventional procedures
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Thumb pain persists.
  • Gripping becomes difficult.
  • Daily activities are affected.
  • Weakness develops.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Dr. Peaches believes that arthritis should never define what a patient can or cannot do.

The objective is not simply to identify arthritis on an X-ray.

The objective is to understand how symptoms affect function, quality of life, and the activities that matter most.

Whether the goal is golfing, pickleball, exercise, work, hobbies, travel, or simply performing daily tasks comfortably, treatment should be individualized.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is thumb arthritis?

Thumb arthritis refers to changes involving the joint at the base of the thumb.

Where is thumb arthritis pain usually felt?

Most commonly at the base of the thumb near the wrist.

Why does it hurt to open jars?

Opening jars requires gripping and twisting forces that place stress on the thumb joint.

Can thumb arthritis affect sports?

Yes.

Golf, pickleball, tennis, cycling, and other activities may be affected.

Does thumb arthritis always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their symptoms and goals.

Should I bring prior imaging to my consultation?

Yes.

Imaging studies and treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
