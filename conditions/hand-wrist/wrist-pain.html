Wrist Pain

Understanding Wrist Pain: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Wrist pain is a common complaint affecting athletes, active adults, office workers, golfers, pickleball players, fitness enthusiasts, and individuals performing everyday activities.

Because the wrist is involved in nearly every hand movement, pain can interfere with:

  • Working
  • Typing
  • Exercising
  • Lifting
  • Golfing
  • Pickleball
  • Household activities
  • Daily function

The wrist is a complex structure composed of multiple bones, ligaments, tendons, joints, cartilage structures, and nerves.

As a result, many different conditions can contribute to wrist pain.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Causes Wrist Pain?

Wrist pain is a symptom, not a diagnosis.

Several different structures may contribute to symptoms.

Common causes include:

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

A common nerve-related condition affecting the wrist and hand.

TFCC Injuries

Injuries involving cartilage and stabilizing structures on the pinky side of the wrist.

Wrist Arthritis

Joint-related changes that may contribute to pain and stiffness.

Tendon Disorders

Irritation or injury involving tendons around the wrist.

De Quervain's Tenosynovitis

A common cause of pain near the thumb side of the wrist.

Sports Injuries

Acute and repetitive stress injuries affecting athletes and active individuals.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the true source of symptoms.

Where Is Your Wrist Pain Located?

The location of symptoms often provides important diagnostic clues.

Pain On The Thumb Side Of The Wrist

May involve tendons, joints, or soft tissues near the thumb.

Pain On The Pinky Side Of The Wrist

May involve structures such as the TFCC or surrounding tissues.

Pain In The Front Of The Wrist

May involve tendons, nerves, or other structures.

Pain In The Back Of The Wrist

May be associated with tendons, joints, or sports-related injuries.

Because symptoms often overlap, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

Common Symptoms Of Wrist Pain

Patients may experience:

  • Wrist pain
  • Stiffness
  • Weakness
  • Swelling
  • Clicking sensations
  • Numbness
  • Tingling
  • Difficulty gripping objects
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms vary depending on the underlying condition.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Lift Things?

Lifting places force through the wrist joint and surrounding structures.

Pain during lifting may be associated with:

  • Tendon disorders
  • Ligament injuries
  • TFCC injuries
  • Arthritis
  • Other wrist conditions

The specific cause depends on the individual patient.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Exercise?

Many patients notice symptoms during:

  • Weightlifting
  • Push-ups
  • Golf
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Fitness classes

Athletic and exercise-related wrist pain may result from repetitive loading, overuse, or acute injury.

Wrist Pain In Athletes

Athletes place substantial demands on the wrist.

Sports commonly associated with wrist pain include:

  • Golf
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Weightlifting
  • Gymnastics
  • CrossFit
  • Baseball

Athletic wrist pain may result from:

  • Repetitive stress
  • Acute injuries
  • Overuse
  • Biomechanical factors

Understanding the athlete's goals is an important part of treatment planning.

Why Does My Wrist Click Or Pop?

Many patients report:

  • Clicking
  • Popping
  • Catching sensations

These symptoms may occur with a variety of wrist conditions.

Some clicking is harmless.

However, clicking associated with pain or functional limitations may warrant evaluation.

Why Accurate Diagnosis Matters

The wrist contains many structures capable of producing pain, including:

  • Bones
  • Joints
  • Ligaments
  • Tendons
  • Cartilage
  • Nerves

Because multiple conditions may create similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step in the treatment process.

The diagnosis guides the treatment strategy.

How Is Wrist Pain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and understand how they relate to the patient's goals and lifestyle.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Activity modifications
  • Bracing
  • Exercise programs
  • Interventional procedures
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Wrist pain persists.
  • Gripping becomes difficult.
  • Numbness develops.
  • Exercise is limited.
  • Weakness develops.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward improvement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common cause of wrist pain?

Several conditions may contribute, including carpal tunnel syndrome, tendon disorders, TFCC injuries, arthritis, and sports-related injuries.

Why does my wrist hurt when I lift objects?

Pain during lifting may involve tendons, ligaments, cartilage structures, joints, or other wrist tissues.

Why does my wrist click?

Clicking may occur with a variety of wrist conditions. When clicking is associated with pain, evaluation may be appropriate.

Can sports cause wrist pain?

Yes.

Golf, pickleball, tennis, weightlifting, and other activities may contribute to wrist symptoms.

Do I need an MRI?

Not always.

Many wrist conditions can be evaluated effectively through history, physical examination, and X-rays.

Does wrist pain always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.
