FAQ Wrist Pain

Frequently Asked Questions About Wrist Pain

Wrist pain is a common reason patients seek orthopedic and sports medicine care.

Because the wrist is involved in nearly every hand movement, symptoms can interfere with:

  • Work
  • Typing
  • Exercise
  • Sports
  • Golf
  • Pickleball
  • Household activities
  • Daily life

Many different conditions can cause wrist pain, which is why obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options.

Below are some of the most common questions patients ask about wrist pain.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt?

Wrist pain may originate from several different structures, including:

  • Joints
  • Tendons
  • Ligaments
  • Cartilage
  • Nerves
  • Bones

Common causes include:

  • Carpal Tunnel Syndrome
  • TFCC injuries
  • Wrist arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • De Quervain's Tenosynovitis
  • Sports injuries

The specific cause depends on the individual patient.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Lift Things?

Lifting places force through the wrist joint and surrounding structures.

Pain while lifting may be associated with:

  • Tendon disorders
  • Ligament injuries
  • TFCC injuries
  • Arthritis
  • Other wrist conditions

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the source of symptoms.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Do Push-Ups?

Push-ups place significant load through the wrist.

Patients commonly notice symptoms because the wrist is positioned in extension while bearing weight.

Several wrist conditions may contribute to discomfort during this activity.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Golf?

The golf swing places substantial forces through the wrists.

Pain may be associated with:

  • Tendon disorders
  • TFCC injuries
  • Arthritis
  • Overuse conditions

Golfers frequently seek evaluation when symptoms begin affecting performance or enjoyment of the game.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Play Pickleball?

Pickleball involves:

  • Repetitive gripping
  • Quick wrist movements
  • Repetitive impact forces

Several wrist conditions may contribute to symptoms in pickleball players.

Why Does My Wrist Click?

Many patients report:

  • Clicking
  • Popping
  • Catching sensations

Some clicking occurs without injury or disease.

However, clicking associated with:

  • Pain
  • Weakness
  • Instability
  • Functional limitations

may warrant evaluation.

Why Does My Wrist Feel Weak?

Weakness may occur because of:

  • Pain-related compensation
  • Tendon disorders
  • Nerve conditions
  • Arthritis
  • Previous injuries

The underlying cause varies from patient to patient.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt When I Rotate It?

Rotational activities may aggravate certain wrist conditions.

Patients often notice symptoms while:

  • Opening jars
  • Turning doorknobs
  • Using tools
  • Swinging a golf club

Pain during rotation may provide important diagnostic clues.

What Is A TFCC Injury?

A TFCC injury involves structures located on the pinky side of the wrist that help stabilize and support wrist movement.

Common symptoms may include:

  • Pinky-side wrist pain
  • Clicking
  • Weakness
  • Pain with rotation

Why Does The Pinky Side Of My Wrist Hurt?

Pain on the pinky side of the wrist may be associated with:

  • TFCC injuries
  • Tendon disorders
  • Joint-related conditions
  • Sports injuries

The location of symptoms often helps guide diagnosis.

What Is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome?

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome involves irritation of the median nerve as it travels through the wrist.

Common symptoms include:

  • Hand numbness
  • Tingling
  • Nighttime symptoms
  • Hand weakness

Why Do My Hands Fall Asleep At Night?

Nighttime numbness is commonly associated with Carpal Tunnel Syndrome.

Patients frequently report waking up and shaking out their hands to relieve symptoms.

What Is De Quervain's Tenosynovitis?

De Quervain's Tenosynovitis is a tendon-related condition affecting the thumb side of the wrist.

Common symptoms include:

  • Thumb-side wrist pain
  • Pain with gripping
  • Pain with lifting
  • Pain with twisting motions

Why Does The Thumb Side Of My Wrist Hurt?

Several conditions may contribute, including:

  • De Quervain's Tenosynovitis
  • Thumb arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Sports-related conditions

A comprehensive evaluation can help identify the cause.

What Is Wrist Arthritis?

Wrist arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the wrist.

Common symptoms include:

  • Pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Difficulty gripping

Why Does My Wrist Feel Stiff?

Stiffness may occur because of:

  • Arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Previous injuries
  • Joint-related conditions

The timing and pattern of symptoms often help guide diagnosis.

Can I Exercise With Wrist Pain?

Many patients remain active while managing wrist conditions.

Exercise recommendations should be individualized based on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals

Do I Need An MRI?

Not always.

Many wrist conditions can be evaluated effectively through:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • X-rays

MRI studies may be appropriate in selected situations.

What Is The Difference Between An MRI And X-Ray?

X-Ray

Primarily evaluates:

  • Bone
  • Joint alignment
  • Arthritis

MRI

Provides additional information regarding:

  • Ligaments
  • Tendons
  • Cartilage
  • Soft tissues

Both studies may play important roles in the diagnostic process.

Does Wrist Pain Always Require Surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals

Treatment recommendations should always be individualized.

When Should I See A Specialist For Wrist Pain?

You should consider evaluation if:

  • Pain persists.
  • Gripping becomes difficult.
  • Weakness develops.
  • Numbness develops.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Wrist pain is common.

The challenge is determining why the wrist hurts.

Because many different structures can create similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step in the treatment process.

Whether the goal is golfing, pickleball, fitness, work, travel, hobbies, or simply using the hand comfortably, treatment should be individualized and centered around helping patients maintain the activities that matter most.
