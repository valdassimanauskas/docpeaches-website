SI Joint Dysfunction

Understanding SI Joint Dysfunction: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Many patients assume that all lower back pain originates from a disc, nerve, or muscle within the spine.

However, another structure that may contribute to pain is the sacroiliac joint, commonly referred to as the SI joint.

Because SI joint symptoms often mimic other conditions, patients are frequently misdiagnosed or spend months searching for answers before the source of pain is identified.

Understanding SI joint dysfunction can help patients better understand their symptoms and the role the sacroiliac joint may play in overall movement and function.

What Is The SI Joint?

The sacroiliac joints connect the spine to the pelvis.

Every person has:

  • A right SI joint
  • A left SI joint

These joints help transfer forces between the upper and lower body during activities such as:

  • Walking
  • Running
  • Standing
  • Lifting
  • Climbing stairs

Although the SI joints have limited motion, they play an important role in stability and movement.

What Is SI Joint Dysfunction?

SI Joint Dysfunction is a term used to describe symptoms believed to originate from one or both sacroiliac joints.

Because the SI joint sits between the spine and pelvis, pain may be felt in several different areas, making diagnosis challenging.

Many patients are surprised to learn that symptoms they assumed were coming from a disc or hip may actually involve the SI joint.

What Causes SI Joint Dysfunction?

Several factors may contribute to SI joint-related symptoms, including:

  • Previous injuries
  • Repetitive stress
  • Altered movement patterns
  • Degenerative changes
  • Sports participation
  • Occupational demands
  • Changes in spinal or pelvic mechanics

In many cases, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of SI Joint Dysfunction

Patients may experience:

  • Lower back pain
  • Buttock pain
  • Pelvic pain
  • Hip-region discomfort
  • Pain while standing
  • Pain while walking
  • Pain when transitioning from sitting to standing
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms may occur on one side or both sides depending on the individual situation.

Where Is SI Joint Pain Usually Felt?

One of the most common locations is:

  • Just below the beltline
  • Near the buttock
  • Along the back portion of the pelvis

Some patients also experience discomfort that extends into the upper leg.

Because symptoms overlap with many other conditions, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

SI Joint Dysfunction vs Herniated Disc

Patients frequently confuse SI joint dysfunction with disc-related conditions.

Both may contribute to:

  • Lower back pain
  • Buttock pain
  • Activity limitations

However, they involve different anatomical structures and often require different treatment considerations.

This is why proper diagnosis is essential.

SI Joint Dysfunction vs Hip Pain

The SI joint and hip are located close together and can produce similar symptoms.

Many patients initially believe they have:

  • Hip arthritis
  • Hip bursitis
  • Hip injuries

when the SI joint may be contributing to discomfort.

Likewise, true hip conditions may mimic SI joint dysfunction.

A comprehensive evaluation helps distinguish between these possibilities.

Why SI Joint Dysfunction Is Often Misdiagnosed

The SI joint exists at the intersection of several important structures.

Pain may resemble symptoms originating from:

  • The lumbar spine
  • The hips
  • Muscles
  • Ligaments
  • Nerves

Because multiple conditions can produce similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis often requires careful evaluation.

How Is SI Joint Dysfunction Evaluated?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Imaging review when appropriate

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • MRI
  • X-rays
  • CT imaging
  • Diagnostic procedures when appropriate

The goal is to determine whether the SI joint is contributing to symptoms.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Patient goals

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Lifestyle changes
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Lower back pain persists.
  • Pain is concentrated near the buttock or pelvis.
  • Walking becomes uncomfortable.
  • Symptoms limit exercise or activity.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.
  • You are unsure whether symptoms are coming from your back, hip, or pelvis.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SI Joint Dysfunction?

SI Joint Dysfunction refers to symptoms believed to originate from one or both sacroiliac joints.

Where is SI joint pain usually felt?

Pain is commonly felt near the lower back, buttock, pelvis, or hip region.

Can SI joint pain feel like a herniated disc?

Yes.

Symptoms may overlap, which is why proper diagnosis is important.

Can SI joint pain feel like hip pain?

Yes.

Many patients have difficulty distinguishing between hip pain and SI joint-related symptoms.

Is SI Joint Dysfunction diagnosed with MRI alone?

No.

Diagnosis typically involves a combination of medical history, physical examination findings, functional assessment, and imaging when appropriate.

Should I bring my imaging studies to my consultation?

Yes.

MRI studies, imaging reports, and prior treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
