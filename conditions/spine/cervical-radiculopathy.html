Cervical Radiculopathy

Understanding Cervical Radiculopathy: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Many patients describe it as:

"Pain that starts in my neck and shoots down my arm."

Others notice numbness in their fingers, tingling in their hand, weakness when lifting objects, or discomfort that seems to travel from the shoulder all the way into the fingertips.

These symptoms may be associated with a condition known as Cervical Radiculopathy.

Cervical radiculopathy occurs when a nerve in the neck becomes irritated, inflamed, or compressed. Because the nerves of the cervical spine travel into the shoulders, arms, and hands, symptoms may be felt far away from the neck itself.

Understanding the cause of cervical radiculopathy is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment strategy.

What Is Cervical Radiculopathy?

The cervical spine contains nerves that exit the spinal canal and travel into the:

  • Shoulders
  • Arms
  • Hands
  • Fingers

When one of these nerve roots becomes irritated or compressed, symptoms may develop along the pathway of that nerve.

This condition is known as cervical radiculopathy.

The specific symptoms often depend on which nerve is involved.

What Causes Cervical Radiculopathy?

Several conditions may contribute to nerve irritation in the neck, including:

Herniated Disc

One of the most common causes of cervical radiculopathy.

Degenerative Disc Disease

Age-related changes that may affect nearby nerves.

Cervical Stenosis

Narrowing around the spinal nerves or spinal canal.

Foraminal Stenosis

Narrowing of the spaces where nerves exit the spine.

Degenerative Spine Conditions

Various structural changes that may contribute to nerve irritation.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the underlying cause.

Common Symptoms Of Cervical Radiculopathy

Symptoms vary depending on the affected nerve and the severity of irritation.

Patients may experience:

  • Neck pain
  • Shoulder pain
  • Arm pain
  • Tingling
  • Numbness
  • Burning sensations
  • Hand symptoms
  • Finger symptoms
  • Weakness
  • Difficulty lifting objects

Some patients experience significant arm symptoms with very little neck pain.

Why Does The Pain Travel Into The Arm?

Many patients are surprised that a problem in the neck can create symptoms in the hand or fingers.

This occurs because cervical nerves travel from the neck into the upper extremity.

When a nerve becomes irritated near the spine, symptoms may be felt anywhere along that nerve's pathway.

This is known as radicular pain.

Can Cervical Radiculopathy Feel Like A Shoulder Problem?

Yes.

Patients frequently believe they have:

  • Shoulder injuries
  • Rotator cuff problems
  • Muscle strains

when the actual source of symptoms may involve a nerve in the cervical spine.

Likewise, some shoulder conditions may mimic cervical radiculopathy.

This is one reason why obtaining an accurate diagnosis is so important.

How Is Cervical Radiculopathy Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Neurological assessment
  • Functional evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • MRI
  • X-rays
  • CT imaging
  • Diagnostic procedures when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of nerve irritation and determine how it relates to the patient's symptoms.

Why MRI Findings Must Be Interpreted Carefully

MRI studies frequently reveal:

  • Disc abnormalities
  • Degenerative changes
  • Foraminal narrowing
  • Stenosis

However, imaging findings do not always correlate perfectly with symptoms.

Many individuals have MRI abnormalities without experiencing pain.

This is why physicians evaluate:

  • Symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Neurological findings
  • Imaging studies

together.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • The diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Imaging findings
  • Patient goals

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Medications
  • Interventional spine procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Arm pain persists.
  • Numbness or tingling develops.
  • Weakness is present.
  • Symptoms interfere with work or daily activities.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.
  • You are unsure whether symptoms are coming from your neck or shoulder.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward determining your options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is cervical radiculopathy?

Cervical radiculopathy occurs when a nerve root in the neck becomes irritated, inflamed, or compressed.

Can cervical radiculopathy cause arm pain?

Yes.

Arm pain is one of the most common symptoms associated with cervical radiculopathy.

Why do my fingers feel numb?

Certain cervical nerves travel into the hand and fingers. Irritation of these nerves may contribute to numbness or tingling sensations.

Is cervical radiculopathy the same as a shoulder injury?

No.

Although symptoms may overlap, cervical radiculopathy and shoulder conditions are different diagnoses.

Does cervical radiculopathy always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and symptoms.

Should I bring my MRI to my consultation?

Yes.

MRI studies, imaging reports, and prior treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
