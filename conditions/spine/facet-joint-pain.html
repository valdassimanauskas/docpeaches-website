Facet Joint Pain

Understanding Facet Joint Pain: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

When patients review an MRI report, they often encounter terms such as:

  • Facet arthropathy
  • Facet degeneration
  • Facet hypertrophy
  • Facet joint arthritis

For many people, these terms create confusion and uncertainty.

What exactly are facet joints?

Can they cause pain?

And if they do, what should patients know about their treatment options?

Facet joints are an important part of normal spinal function and may contribute to symptoms in certain individuals. Understanding their role within the spine can help patients better understand their diagnosis and make more informed healthcare decisions.

What Are Facet Joints?

Facet joints are small joints located throughout the spine.

These joints connect neighboring vertebrae and help:

  • Guide movement
  • Support stability
  • Control spinal motion
  • Maintain flexibility

Facet joints are found throughout the:

Cervical Spine

The neck.

Thoracic Spine

The mid-back.

Lumbar Spine

The lower back.

Like other joints throughout the body, facet joints may undergo age-related changes over time.

What Is Facet Joint Pain?

Facet joint pain refers to symptoms that may originate from one or more facet joints within the spine.

Because many spinal structures are capable of producing similar symptoms, diagnosing facet-mediated pain often requires a comprehensive evaluation.

The goal is to determine whether facet joints are actually contributing to the patient's symptoms rather than simply identifying age-related changes on imaging.

What Causes Facet Joint Pain?

Several factors may contribute to facet joint changes, including:

  • Aging
  • Degenerative changes
  • Repetitive stress
  • Prior injuries
  • Sports participation
  • Occupational demands
  • Altered spinal mechanics

In many cases, multiple factors may contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of Facet Joint Pain

Symptoms vary depending on the region of the spine involved.

Patients may experience:

  • Neck pain
  • Lower back pain
  • Stiffness
  • Pain with certain movements
  • Activity-related discomfort
  • Reduced flexibility
  • Difficulty standing for prolonged periods

Symptoms often overlap with other spine conditions, which is why proper diagnosis is important.

Facet Joint Pain In The Neck

When facet joints in the cervical spine are involved, symptoms may include:

  • Neck pain
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Stiffness
  • Headaches in some individuals
  • Shoulder-region discomfort

The specific pattern depends on the structures involved.

Facet Joint Pain In The Lower Back

When lumbar facet joints are involved, patients may experience:

  • Lower back pain
  • Stiffness
  • Pain while standing
  • Pain with extension movements
  • Activity limitations

Many patients assume their symptoms are coming from a disc when the source may be elsewhere.

This is why a comprehensive evaluation is essential.

What Is Facet Arthropathy?

Facet arthropathy is a term commonly used to describe degenerative changes involving the facet joints.

It is one of the most common MRI findings seen in adults.

However, not every patient with facet arthropathy experiences symptoms.

Imaging findings alone do not establish a diagnosis.

Why MRI Findings Don't Always Match Symptoms

This is one of the most important concepts in spine care.

Many patients have:

  • Facet degeneration
  • Disc degeneration
  • Disc bulges
  • Other MRI abnormalities

without experiencing pain.

Likewise, some patients experience significant symptoms despite relatively modest imaging findings.

This is why physicians evaluate:

  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging studies
  • Clinical history

together.

How Is Facet Joint Pain Evaluated?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Imaging review
  • Review of prior treatments

In some situations, physicians may discuss specialized diagnostic procedures when evaluating potential facet-mediated pain.

The objective is to determine whether facet joints are contributing to symptoms.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Patient goals

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Lifestyle changes
  • Interventional spine procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The most appropriate treatment strategy is individualized.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are facet joints?

Facet joints are small joints located throughout the spine that help guide movement and provide stability.

Can facet joints cause lower back pain?

Yes.

Facet joints may contribute to lower back pain in certain individuals.

Can facet joints cause neck pain?

Yes.

Facet joints in the cervical spine may contribute to neck pain and stiffness.

What is facet arthropathy?

Facet arthropathy refers to degenerative changes involving the facet joints.

Does facet arthropathy always cause symptoms?

No.

Many people have facet degeneration on MRI studies without experiencing pain.

Should I bring my MRI to my consultation?

Yes.

MRI studies, imaging reports, and previous treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
