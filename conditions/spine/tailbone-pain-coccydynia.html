Tailbone Pain (Coccydynia)

Understanding Tailbone Pain (Coccydynia): Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Tailbone pain can make everyday activities surprisingly difficult.

Many patients describe symptoms such as:

"It hurts every time I sit down."

"Driving is uncomfortable."

"Getting up from a chair causes pain."

The tailbone, also known as the coccyx, sits at the very bottom of the spine and serves as an attachment point for several muscles, tendons, and ligaments.

When pain develops in this area, even simple activities can become frustrating.

Understanding the cause of tailbone pain is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options.

What Is Tailbone Pain?

Tailbone pain, also known as coccydynia, refers to pain originating from the coccyx or surrounding structures.

Symptoms may occur:

  • While sitting
  • When leaning backward
  • During position changes
  • During prolonged driving
  • During certain daily activities

The severity of symptoms varies significantly from person to person.

Where Is The Tailbone?

The coccyx is located at the bottom of the spine beneath the sacrum.

Although relatively small, the coccyx plays a role in:

  • Weight distribution while sitting
  • Muscle attachment
  • Ligament attachment
  • Pelvic floor support

Because of its location, the coccyx may become symptomatic when subjected to stress or injury.

What Causes Tailbone Pain?

Several factors may contribute.

Falls

A fall directly onto the buttocks is one of the most common causes.

Repetitive Pressure

Prolonged sitting may aggravate symptoms in some individuals.

Sports And Recreation

Certain activities may place repetitive stress on the coccyx.

Childbirth

Some individuals develop tailbone pain following childbirth.

No Obvious Injury

In some cases, symptoms develop without a clearly identifiable cause.

A comprehensive evaluation helps identify potential contributing factors.

Common Symptoms Of Tailbone Pain

Patients may experience:

  • Pain while sitting
  • Pain when rising from a seated position
  • Pain during prolonged driving
  • Tenderness over the tailbone
  • Pain with leaning backward
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms are often most noticeable while sitting.

Why Does My Tailbone Hurt When I Sit?

This is one of the hallmark symptoms of coccydynia.

Sitting places pressure through the coccyx and surrounding structures.

Patients frequently report:

"The longer I sit, the worse it gets."

The specific cause varies depending on the individual and underlying diagnosis.

Why Does It Hurt When I Stand Up?

Many patients notice discomfort during transitions.

Symptoms may occur:

  • Standing from a chair
  • Rising from a car seat
  • Getting up after prolonged sitting

This pattern is commonly associated with tailbone-related pain.

Can A Fall Cause Tailbone Pain?

Yes.

A fall directly onto the buttocks is one of the most common causes of coccyx injuries.

Even when imaging does not reveal a significant injury, symptoms may persist and warrant evaluation.

Tailbone Pain And Driving

Driving is a frequent aggravating activity.

Many patients report:

  • Increased pain during long drives
  • Difficulty remaining seated
  • Needing frequent position changes

Because prolonged sitting places pressure on the coccyx, symptoms may become more noticeable during travel.

How Is Tailbone Pain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and rule out other causes of pain.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Duration of symptoms
  • Imaging findings
  • Individual goals

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Activity modifications
  • Cushion recommendations
  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Interventional procedures when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Tailbone pain persists.
  • Sitting becomes difficult.
  • Driving is uncomfortable.
  • Symptoms continue to worsen.
  • Daily activities become limited.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.
