Mid Back Pain

Understanding Mid Back Pain: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Mid back pain can make everyday activities uncomfortable and frustrating.

Patients commonly describe symptoms such as:

"I have pain between my shoulder blades."

"My upper back feels tight and stiff."

"Sitting at my desk all day makes it worse."

The mid back, also known as the thoracic spine, helps support posture, movement, and everyday function.

When pain develops in this region, it may affect work, exercise, sleep, travel, and quality of life.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options.

What Is Mid Back Pain?

Mid back pain refers to discomfort occurring between the base of the neck and the lower back.

This region includes:

  • Thoracic vertebrae
  • Facet joints
  • Muscles
  • Ligaments
  • Ribs
  • Soft tissues

Several different structures may contribute to symptoms.

Where Is Mid Back Pain Usually Felt?

Patients commonly describe pain:

Between The Shoulder Blades

One of the most frequent locations.

Along The Spine

Pain may occur directly over the thoracic spine.

Around The Ribs

Some patients notice symptoms extending around the chest wall or rib cage.

Across The Upper Back

Pain may be broad rather than localized.

The location of symptoms often provides important diagnostic clues.

What Causes Mid Back Pain?

Several factors may contribute.

Muscle Strain

One of the most common causes.

Postural Stress

Prolonged sitting and desk work may contribute to symptoms.

Sports And Exercise

Athletic activities may place repetitive stress on the thoracic spine.

Joint-Related Conditions

Facet joints and surrounding structures may become symptomatic.

Degenerative Changes

Age-related changes may contribute in some individuals.

Previous Injuries

Prior trauma may affect the thoracic region.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of Mid Back Pain

Patients may experience:

  • Pain between the shoulder blades
  • Stiffness
  • Muscle tightness
  • Difficulty twisting
  • Pain with prolonged sitting
  • Pain with exercise
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms vary depending on the underlying cause.

Why Does My Back Hurt Between My Shoulder Blades?

This is one of the most common questions patients ask.

Pain between the shoulder blades may be associated with:

  • Muscle strain
  • Postural stress
  • Joint-related conditions
  • Sports injuries
  • Other thoracic spine conditions

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the source of symptoms.

Why Does Sitting Make My Mid Back Hurt?

Many patients report increased symptoms during:

  • Desk work
  • Computer use
  • Driving
  • Travel

Prolonged sitting may place stress on the muscles and joints of the thoracic spine.

Why Does My Upper Back Feel Tight?

Muscle tightness is a common complaint.

Patients often describe:

"My back feels knotted."

or

"I constantly feel tight between my shoulders."

Several musculoskeletal conditions may contribute to these symptoms.

Mid Back Pain In Athletes

Athletes place substantial demands on the spine.

Sports commonly associated with thoracic symptoms include:

  • Golf
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Weightlifting
  • Running
  • Cycling

Rotational and repetitive movements may contribute to symptoms in some individuals.

Mid Back Pain And Golf

Golf requires repeated rotational movements through the thoracic spine.

Players may notice symptoms during:

  • Swinging
  • Practice sessions
  • Tournament play

Thoracic mobility often plays an important role in golf performance.

How Is Mid Back Pain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and understand how they affect daily activities.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Duration of symptoms
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Postural strategies
  • Interventional procedures when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Pain persists.
  • Sitting becomes uncomfortable.
  • Exercise is limited.
  • Symptoms continue to worsen.
  • Daily activities become difficult.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes pain between the shoulder blades?

Several conditions may contribute, including muscle strain, postural stress, sports injuries, and joint-related conditions.

Why does my mid back hurt when I sit?

Prolonged sitting may increase stress on the muscles and joints of the thoracic spine.

Can golf cause mid back pain?

Yes. The golf swing places rotational demands on the thoracic spine and surrounding muscles.

Is mid back pain serious?

Most cases are musculoskeletal, but persistent or worsening symptoms should be evaluated.

Do I need an MRI?

Imaging recommendations depend on symptoms, examination findings, and the suspected diagnosis.

Can I continue exercising?

Many patients remain active, although activity recommendations should be individualized.
