Chronic Low Back Pain

Understanding Chronic Low Back Pain

Low back pain is one of the most common health concerns affecting adults throughout the world.

For some individuals, symptoms resolve quickly. For others, discomfort persists for months or even years, affecting work, exercise, sleep, travel, and overall quality of life.

When symptoms continue beyond the expected healing period, physicians may refer to the condition as chronic low back pain.

Understanding the potential causes of chronic low back pain is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options and developing a personalized care plan.

What Is Chronic Low Back Pain?

Chronic low back pain generally refers to lower back symptoms that persist for an extended period of time.

Unlike acute injuries that may improve within weeks, chronic symptoms often involve more complex factors that require comprehensive evaluation.

Every patient's experience is unique.

Some individuals experience intermittent discomfort while others struggle with daily limitations that affect work, recreation, and everyday activities.

Why Does Chronic Low Back Pain Occur?

The lower back contains many structures capable of generating pain.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Intervertebral discs
  • Facet joints
  • Sacroiliac joints
  • Muscles
  • Ligaments
  • Nerves
  • Degenerative changes
  • Previous injuries

In many cases, multiple factors may contribute simultaneously.

This is why accurate diagnosis is so important.

Common Conditions Associated With Chronic Low Back Pain

Several conditions may be associated with persistent low back symptoms, including:

Herniated Disc

Changes involving the disc that may contribute to back pain or leg symptoms.

Degenerative Disc Disease

Age-related or degenerative changes involving the spinal discs.

Annular Tear

A disruption involving the outer portion of an intervertebral disc.

Lumbar Radiculopathy

Symptoms involving irritation of spinal nerves.

Sciatica

Pain that travels from the lower back into the leg.

Facet Joint Pain

Pain originating from the small joints located in the spine.

SI Joint Dysfunction

Pain associated with the sacroiliac joints connecting the spine and pelvis.

Common Symptoms

Patients with chronic low back pain may experience:

  • Persistent lower back discomfort
  • Stiffness
  • Pain with prolonged sitting
  • Pain with standing
  • Difficulty exercising
  • Difficulty lifting
  • Reduced mobility
  • Leg symptoms
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms vary depending on the underlying diagnosis.

How Is Chronic Low Back Pain Evaluated?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Review of prior treatments
  • Imaging review when appropriate

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • MRI
  • X-rays
  • CT imaging
  • Diagnostic procedures in selected situations

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and develop an individualized treatment strategy.

Why MRI Findings Don't Always Tell The Whole Story

Many patients become concerned after reading an MRI report.

However, imaging findings do not always correlate with symptoms.

Some people have significant MRI abnormalities and minimal symptoms.

Others experience severe symptoms despite relatively modest imaging findings.

This is why physicians evaluate the entire patient rather than relying solely on imaging studies.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on the diagnosis, severity of symptoms, and patient goals.

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modification
  • Lifestyle changes
  • Interventional spine procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

No single treatment is right for every patient.

The best strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek An Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Symptoms have persisted for several weeks or months.
  • Daily activities are becoming difficult.
  • Exercise is limited by pain.
  • Leg symptoms are present.
  • Previous treatments have not provided sufficient improvement.
  • You are unsure about the source of your symptoms.

Obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important first step.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is considered chronic low back pain?

Chronic low back pain generally refers to symptoms that persist beyond the expected healing period and continue affecting daily function.

Is chronic low back pain always caused by a herniated disc?

No.

Many structures within the spine and surrounding tissues can contribute to chronic symptoms.

Can MRI findings explain all back pain?

Not always.

Imaging findings must be interpreted alongside symptoms, physical examination findings, and clinical history.

Does chronic low back pain always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and individual circumstances.

Should I bring my MRI to my consultation?

Yes.

MRI studies, imaging reports, and previous treatment records can provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
