Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS)

Understanding Pain After Spinal Surgery

Many patients undergo spinal surgery hoping to improve pain, restore function, and return to the activities they enjoy. While surgery can be an important treatment option for certain spinal conditions, some individuals continue to experience symptoms after surgery or develop new symptoms over time.

Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS) is a term used to describe persistent or recurrent pain that continues following spinal surgery.

Historically, this condition was often referred to as Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS). More recently, terms such as Persistent Spinal Pain Syndrome (PSPS) and Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS) have become increasingly common because they more accurately describe the patient's experience without implying that a procedure or surgeon has failed.

At Dr. Peaches, evaluation focuses on identifying potential causes of persistent symptoms, understanding how symptoms impact daily life, and discussing appropriate treatment options based on each patient's diagnosis and goals.

What Is Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS)?

Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS) describes persistent or recurrent pain following a spinal procedure.

Symptoms may occur:

  • Immediately after surgery
  • Months after surgery
  • Years after surgery

The severity and location of symptoms vary from person to person.

Some patients experience ongoing symptoms similar to those they had before surgery, while others develop new symptoms during the recovery process or later in life.

What Causes Pain After Spinal Surgery?

There is no single cause of persistent pain after spinal surgery.

Potential contributing factors may include:

Scar Tissue Formation

Scar tissue may develop around nerves and surrounding structures following surgery.

Recurrent Disc Herniation

In some cases, a disc herniation may recur after previous treatment.

Degenerative Changes

The spine continues to age over time, and additional changes may occur in discs, joints, and supporting structures.

Adjacent Segment Degeneration

Changes may develop above or below a previously treated spinal segment.

Nerve-Related Pain

Persistent nerve irritation or nerve-related symptoms may contribute to ongoing discomfort.

Facet Joint Pain

The small joints of the spine may become a source of symptoms following surgery.

Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction

The sacroiliac (SI) joint may contribute to lower back, buttock, or pelvic symptoms in some patients following lumbar surgery.

Spinal Stenosis

Narrowing within the spine may contribute to persistent or recurrent symptoms.

Common Symptoms

Symptoms vary depending on the individual and the underlying cause.

Patients may report:

Low Back Pain

Persistent discomfort involving the lumbar spine.

Leg Pain

Pain that travels into one or both legs.

Sciatica

Symptoms involving irritation of spinal nerves.

Numbness Or Tingling

Altered sensation in the lower extremities.

Weakness

Changes in strength or physical function.

Difficulty Standing Or Walking

Some individuals experience increased symptoms with prolonged activity.

Persistent Neck Pain

For patients who have undergone cervical spine procedures.

Common Procedures Associated With PASS

Pain After Spinal Surgery may occur following various spinal procedures, including:

Lumbar Discectomy

Procedures performed to address disc herniations.

Lumbar Fusion

Stabilization procedures involving one or more spinal levels.

Cervical Spine Surgery

Procedures involving the neck.

Laminectomy

Procedures performed to address spinal stenosis.

Decompression Procedures

Various surgeries designed to relieve pressure on nerves.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

Pain After Spinal Surgery is not a diagnosis itself.

It is a description of persistent symptoms following surgery.

The most important step is determining:

  • Where symptoms are coming from
  • Whether symptoms are related to the original condition
  • Whether new conditions have developed
  • Which structures may be contributing to pain

An accurate diagnosis helps guide treatment recommendations.

How Is PASS Evaluated?

A comprehensive evaluation may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, prior procedures, and treatment history.

Physical Examination

Assessment of movement, strength, sensation, and functional limitations.

Imaging Studies

Review of:

  • MRI
  • CT scans
  • X-rays
  • Other diagnostic studies when appropriate

Diagnostic Procedures

Selected patients may undergo image-guided diagnostic procedures to help identify potential pain generators.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations vary depending on the diagnosis and individual patient circumstances.

Discussions may include:

Physical Therapy

Movement-based rehabilitation and strengthening strategies.

Activity Modification

Adjustments designed to support recovery and function.

Medications

When appropriate as part of a comprehensive treatment plan.

Epidural Steroid Injections

Image-guided procedures commonly used in selected patients.

Facet Joint Procedures

Evaluation and treatment of facet-mediated pain.

Sacroiliac Joint Procedures

Assessment of potential SI joint involvement.

Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

May be discussed for selected patients depending on the diagnosis.

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation (PNS)

An advanced treatment option that may be considered in certain situations.

Additional Surgical Evaluation

For some patients, consultation with a spine surgeon may be appropriate.

PASS And Quality Of Life

Persistent symptoms following spinal surgery may affect:

  • Work
  • Exercise
  • Sports participation
  • Travel
  • Sleep
  • Daily activities

Because every patient's experience is unique, treatment planning should focus on both diagnosis and functional goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS)?

PASS describes persistent or recurrent pain that continues after spinal surgery.

Is PASS the same as Failed Back Surgery Syndrome?

Yes.

Pain After Spinal Surgery (PASS) is a more modern and patient-friendly term that is often used instead of Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS).

Can pain return years after back surgery?

Yes.

Symptoms may develop months or years after a previous spinal procedure.

Does persistent pain mean the surgery failed?

Not necessarily.

Many factors may contribute to symptoms following spinal surgery, and a comprehensive evaluation is important.

Can scar tissue cause pain after surgery?

Scar tissue may be one of several factors considered during evaluation.

Should I have persistent pain after surgery evaluated?

If symptoms continue to affect your quality of life, a comprehensive evaluation may help identify contributing factors and appropriate treatment options.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Persistent pain following spinal surgery can be frustrating and often leaves patients searching for answers.

The most important step is understanding why symptoms continue.

Because multiple structures can contribute to pain after surgery, successful treatment begins with an accurate diagnosis and a personalized treatment strategy.

Whether the goal is returning to work, staying active, improving mobility, participating in sports, or maintaining quality of life, treatment recommendations should be individualized and focused on helping patients continue doing the things that matter most.
