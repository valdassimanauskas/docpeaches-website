Insulin Resistance

Understanding Insulin Resistance

Insulin resistance is one of the most common metabolic health concerns affecting adults today.

Many people have insulin resistance without realizing it.

Over time, insulin resistance may be associated with:

  • Weight gain
  • Difficulty losing weight
  • Increased belly fat
  • Low energy
  • Food cravings
  • Metabolic dysfunction
  • Changes in body composition

Because symptoms often develop gradually, many patients do not recognize insulin resistance until they begin experiencing challenges with weight management, energy levels, or overall health.

At Dr. Peaches, insulin resistance is evaluated as part of a comprehensive approach to metabolic health, weight management, healthy aging, and performance medicine.

What Is Insulin?

Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas.

Its primary role is to help the body regulate blood sugar and move glucose from the bloodstream into cells where it can be used for energy.

When insulin functions efficiently, the body is able to maintain healthy energy regulation.

What Is Insulin Resistance?

Insulin resistance occurs when the body's cells become less responsive to insulin.

As a result, the body may produce increasing amounts of insulin in an effort to maintain normal blood sugar regulation.

Over time, this process may affect:

  • Metabolic health
  • Energy levels
  • Body composition
  • Weight management

Insulin resistance often develops gradually and may be present for years before it is recognized.

Why Is Insulin Resistance Important?

Insulin resistance is closely connected to metabolic health.

Many patients who struggle with:

  • Weight gain
  • Difficulty losing weight
  • Increased abdominal fat
  • Fatigue
  • Low energy

may also have underlying metabolic factors contributing to these symptoms.

Understanding insulin resistance can be an important step toward improving overall health and wellness.

Common Signs Of Insulin Resistance

Many individuals with insulin resistance report:

Difficulty Losing Weight

Weight loss may become increasingly challenging despite lifestyle modifications.

Weight Gain

Especially around the abdomen and midsection.

Increased Belly Fat

Changes in fat distribution are a common concern.

Low Energy

Persistent fatigue and reduced stamina throughout the day.

Increased Hunger

Some individuals report feeling hungry more frequently.

Food Cravings

Particularly cravings for highly processed or carbohydrate-rich foods.

Changes In Body Composition

Loss of muscle mass combined with increased body fat.

What Causes Insulin Resistance?

Multiple factors may contribute to insulin resistance.

Genetics

Family history may influence metabolic health.

Body Composition

Excess body fat, particularly abdominal fat, may affect insulin sensitivity.

Physical Inactivity

Regular movement and exercise support overall metabolic function.

Aging

Metabolic changes may occur over time.

Sleep Disruption

Poor sleep may influence insulin sensitivity and metabolic health.

Stress

Chronic stress may affect multiple systems involved in energy regulation.

Insulin Resistance And Weight Gain

One of the most common reasons patients seek evaluation is unexplained weight gain.

Many individuals report:

  • Gaining weight despite healthy habits
  • Difficulty losing belly fat
  • Weight loss plateaus
  • Increased abdominal fat accumulation

Insulin resistance may contribute to these challenges.

For this reason, insulin resistance and weight management are often discussed together.

Insulin Resistance And Belly Fat

Many patients specifically notice increased abdominal fat.

This may be frustrating because:

  • Clothing fits differently
  • Exercise becomes more difficult
  • Energy levels decline
  • Confidence may be affected

Body composition changes involving abdominal fat frequently overlap with metabolic health concerns.

Insulin Resistance And Metabolic Health

Insulin resistance is one component of overall metabolic health.

Other factors may include:

  • Body composition
  • Physical activity
  • Hormonal health
  • Nutrition
  • Sleep
  • Recovery

A comprehensive approach often produces the best long-term outcomes.

Insulin Resistance And Healthy Aging

Healthy aging involves more than avoiding disease.

Many patients want to:

  • Maintain energy
  • Preserve muscle mass

  • Improve body composition
  • Support longevity

Optimizing metabolic health may help support these goals.

Physician-guided treatment strategies when appropriate.

Insulin resistance is often associated with changes in body composition and weight management challenges.

Many patients with insulin resistance report difficulty losing weight despite lifestyle modifications.

What are common signs of insulin resistance?

Common concerns include weight gain, increased belly fat, fatigue, food cravings, and difficulty losing weight.

Is insulin resistance related to metabolic health?

Insulin resistance is closely connected to overall metabolic health.

Can exercise improve metabolic health?
