Pickleball Elbow

Understanding Pickleball Elbow: Why Your Elbow Hurts and What You Should Know

Pickleball has become one of the fastest-growing sports in the United States. Its combination of competition, exercise, and social interaction has made it popular among athletes, active adults, and retirees alike.

As participation has grown, so has the number of players experiencing elbow pain.

Many people describe this condition as "pickleball elbow," a term commonly used to describe pain that develops from repetitive swinging, gripping, and impact forces associated with the sport.

If elbow pain is affecting your ability to play, understanding the cause is often the first step toward finding the right path forward.

What Is Pickleball Elbow?

Pickleball elbow is not a separate medical diagnosis.

Instead, it is a term commonly used to describe elbow pain that develops while playing pickleball.

In many cases, symptoms are related to irritation of the tendons that help control wrist and forearm movement. This often occurs on the outside of the elbow and may resemble what physicians commonly refer to as tennis elbow.

Depending on the individual, symptoms may also involve muscles, tendons, ligaments, joints, or surrounding soft tissues.

Because several conditions can produce similar symptoms, a proper evaluation is important.

Why Does Pickleball Cause Elbow Pain?

Pickleball involves repetitive motions that place stress on the elbow and forearm.

Potential contributing factors include:

  • Repetitive paddle swings
  • Frequent gripping
  • Poor swing mechanics
  • Sudden increases in activity
  • Inadequate recovery
  • Muscle imbalances
  • Previous injuries
  • Equipment factors

For many players, symptoms develop gradually over time rather than from a single injury.

Common Symptoms

People experiencing pickleball-related elbow pain may notice:

  • Pain on the outside of the elbow
  • Tenderness around the elbow joint
  • Weakness when gripping objects
  • Discomfort during backhand shots
  • Pain while lifting items
  • Reduced performance on the court
  • Stiffness after playing

Symptoms may be mild initially but can become more noticeable if repetitive stress continues.

Who Is Most Commonly Affected?

Pickleball elbow can affect:

  • Recreational players
  • Competitive players
  • New players
  • Former tennis players
  • Active adults
  • Retirees
  • Individuals returning to sports after a long break

Interestingly, many players develop symptoms shortly after increasing playing frequency or intensity.

How Is Pickleball Elbow Diagnosed?

A diagnosis begins with a thorough history and physical examination.

Important considerations may include:

  • Location of symptoms
  • Playing habits
  • Training volume
  • Previous injuries
  • Occupational demands
  • Grip strength
  • Movement patterns

In some situations, imaging studies may be helpful to evaluate other causes of elbow pain.

Because multiple conditions can create similar symptoms, accurate diagnosis is important before considering treatment options.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on the individual patient and underlying diagnosis.

Potential management strategies may include:

  • Activity modification
  • Technique adjustments
  • Physical therapy
  • Strengthening programs
  • Mobility exercises
  • Sports medicine evaluation
  • Image-guided procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions when appropriate
  • Surgical consultation when necessary

The goal is not simply to reduce symptoms but to help people continue participating in the activities they enjoy.

Can You Continue Playing Pickleball?

The answer depends on several factors.

Some individuals may be able to continue playing with modifications, while others may benefit from temporarily reducing activity to allow symptoms to improve.

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the most appropriate strategy based on the severity of symptoms and the underlying cause.

Preventing Pickleball Elbow

While no strategy can completely eliminate risk, potential considerations include:

  • Proper warm-ups
  • Strength training
  • Gradual increases in activity
  • Appropriate paddle selection
  • Technique improvement
  • Adequate recovery
  • Listening to early warning signs

Many overuse injuries become easier to manage when addressed early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is pickleball elbow the same as tennis elbow?

In many cases, pickleball elbow involves similar tendon-related irritation commonly associated with tennis elbow, but a proper evaluation is important because multiple conditions can produce similar symptoms.

Can beginners develop pickleball elbow?

Yes. New players sometimes develop symptoms when activity levels increase rapidly or when muscles and tendons are not yet conditioned for repetitive play.

Should I stop playing if my elbow hurts?

The answer depends on the severity of symptoms and the underlying cause. An evaluation can help determine the most appropriate approach.

Can pickleball cause permanent damage?

Many causes of pickleball-related elbow pain can be addressed when identified early. Delaying evaluation may allow symptoms to persist longer than necessary.

Should I bring imaging to my consultation?

If you have previous imaging studies, reports, or medical records, bringing them may provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
