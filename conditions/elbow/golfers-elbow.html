Golfer's Elbow

Understanding Golfer's Elbow: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Pain on the inside of the elbow can make everyday activities uncomfortable and frustrating. Whether you're swinging a golf club, lifting weights, working with tools, or simply carrying groceries, persistent elbow pain can interfere with both performance and quality of life.

One common cause of pain on the inside of the elbow is a condition known as golfer's elbow.

Despite its name, golfer's elbow affects far more than golfers. Many active adults, athletes, and working professionals develop this condition through repetitive stress placed on the muscles and tendons of the forearm.

Understanding why golfer's elbow develops is often the first step toward finding the right treatment approach.

What Is Golfer's Elbow?

Golfer's elbow, also known as medial epicondylitis, is a condition that affects the tendons attached to the inside portion of the elbow.

These tendons help control movements of the wrist, hand, and forearm. Over time, repetitive stress may contribute to irritation and discomfort in this area.

Although golfer's elbow is commonly associated with golf, it can develop from many different activities that require repeated gripping, lifting, or wrist motion.

What Causes Golfer's Elbow?

Golfer's elbow is often related to repetitive movements that place strain on the muscles and tendons of the forearm.

Common contributing activities may include:

  • Golf
  • Weight training
  • Baseball
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Carpentry
  • Plumbing
  • Construction work
  • Gardening
  • Repetitive lifting
  • Occupational overuse

Symptoms often develop gradually rather than from a single injury.

Common Symptoms

People with golfer's elbow may experience:

  • Pain on the inside of the elbow
  • Tenderness near the bony prominence of the elbow
  • Pain when gripping objects
  • Weakness in the hand or forearm
  • Discomfort when lifting
  • Symptoms during sports activities
  • Stiffness around the elbow

Symptoms may range from mild discomfort to significant limitations in activity.

Golf and Elbow Pain

Although golfer's elbow can affect anyone, golfers may be particularly susceptible because of the repetitive forces generated during the golf swing.

Factors that may contribute include:

  • Swing mechanics
  • Practice volume
  • Equipment selection
  • Conditioning level
  • Previous injuries

A proper evaluation can help determine whether symptoms are related to golfer's elbow or another condition affecting the elbow.

How Is Golfer's Elbow Diagnosed?

A diagnosis typically begins with a comprehensive history and physical examination.

Important factors may include:

  • Location of symptoms
  • Activity level
  • Sports participation
  • Occupational demands
  • Previous injuries
  • Grip strength
  • Range of motion

In some cases, imaging studies may be considered to help evaluate the elbow and surrounding structures.

Because multiple conditions can produce similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is important before making treatment decisions.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment should be individualized based on the patient's symptoms, goals, and overall condition.

Management strategies may include:

  • Activity modification
  • Physical therapy
  • Stretching programs
  • Strengthening exercises
  • Sports medicine evaluation
  • Image-guided procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The goal is to improve function, support recovery, and help patients return to the activities they enjoy.

Can Golfer's Elbow Occur Without Playing Golf?

Absolutely.

Many people diagnosed with golfer's elbow have never played golf.

The condition is often related to repetitive stress placed on the forearm and elbow through work, exercise, sports, or daily activities.

The name reflects one common association, not the only cause.

When Should You Seek An Evaluation?

You may want to seek an evaluation if:

  • Symptoms are limiting daily activities.
  • Pain persists despite rest.
  • Grip strength is declining.
  • Sports participation is affected.
  • You are unsure about the cause of your elbow pain.

A proper diagnosis can help identify the source of symptoms and guide future treatment decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is golfer's elbow the same as tennis elbow?

No. Golfer's elbow typically affects the tendons on the inside of the elbow, while tennis elbow usually affects the outside of the elbow.

Do you have to play golf to develop golfer's elbow?

No. Many people who develop golfer's elbow have never played golf. Repetitive activities involving the wrist and forearm may contribute to symptoms.

Can weightlifting cause golfer's elbow?

Certain lifting movements may place stress on the forearm tendons and may contribute to symptoms in some individuals.

Does golfer's elbow always require surgery?

No. Many patients explore non-surgical treatment pathways depending on their diagnosis and individual circumstances.

Should I bring imaging to my consultation?

If you have previous MRI studies, X-rays, ultrasound reports, or medical records, bringing them may provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
