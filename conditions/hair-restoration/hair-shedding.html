Hair Restoration

Hair Shedding

Why Is My Hair Falling Out?

Finding hair in the shower, on your pillow, in your brush, or on your clothing can be alarming.

Many people become concerned when they notice what appears to be more hair shedding than usual and begin asking:

  • Why is my hair falling out?
  • Why am I shedding so much hair?
  • Is this normal?
  • Am I going bald?
  • Will my hair grow back?

Hair shedding is one of the most common reasons patients seek evaluation for hair loss.

Because several different factors may contribute, understanding the cause is often the first step toward developing an appropriate treatment plan.

At Dream Regen, consultations focus on identifying contributing factors, evaluating hair loss patterns, and discussing individualized treatment options.

What Is Hair Shedding?

Hair shedding refers to the natural loss of hair that occurs as part of the normal hair growth cycle.

Every day, people lose a certain number of hairs as older hairs complete their growth cycle and new hairs begin to grow.

However, some individuals notice shedding that appears greater than normal.

When shedding becomes excessive, persistent, or noticeable, evaluation may be helpful.

Is Hair Shedding Normal?

Yes.

Hair shedding is a normal part of the hair growth cycle.

Many people notice hair loss:

  • During washing
  • While brushing
  • During styling
  • On pillows
  • On clothing

The challenge is determining when shedding may be greater than expected.

Common Signs Of Excessive Hair Shedding

Patients frequently report:

Hair In The Shower

Large amounts of hair during washing.

Hair In Brushes

Increased hair accumulation while brushing.

Hair On Pillows

More hair than usual after sleeping.

Reduced Hair Volume

Hair may feel thinner overall.

Increased Scalp Visibility

The scalp may become easier to see through the hair.

What Causes Hair Shedding?

Hair shedding can occur for many reasons.

Stress

Physical or emotional stress may affect the normal hair growth cycle.

Common examples include:

  • Major life events
  • Illness
  • Emotional stress
  • Significant lifestyle changes

Illness

Some individuals notice increased shedding following illness or recovery from a medical condition.

Hormonal Changes

Hormonal fluctuations may influence hair growth and shedding patterns.

Women may notice changes during:

  • Pregnancy
  • Postpartum recovery
  • Perimenopause
  • Menopause

Nutritional Factors

Overall nutritional health may affect hair growth cycles.

Medical Conditions

Certain medical conditions may contribute to excessive shedding.

Hair Loss Disorders

Conditions such as androgenetic alopecia may sometimes be associated with increased shedding.

What Is Telogen Effluvium?

Telogen Effluvium is one of the most common causes of sudden or noticeable hair shedding.

Patients often describe:

"My hair is coming out everywhere."

Telogen Effluvium may occur after:

  • Significant stress
  • Illness
  • Surgery
  • Hormonal changes
  • Major life events

Because the symptoms can resemble other forms of hair loss, evaluation is important.

Hair Shedding In Women

Women frequently seek evaluation after noticing:

Increased Hair Loss In The Shower

A common concern that often prompts consultation.

Postpartum Hair Shedding

Some women experience increased shedding following pregnancy.

Menopause-Related Hair Changes

Hair density and shedding patterns may change during and after menopause.

Diffuse Thinning

Hair may appear less full throughout the scalp.

Hair Shedding In Men

Men may experience shedding associated with:

Male Pattern Baldness

Androgenetic alopecia may contribute to gradual thinning and increased hair loss.

Stress-Related Shedding

Periods of stress may influence normal hair growth cycles.

Lifestyle Factors

Changes in health, routine, or overall wellness may contribute.

Why Am I Losing So Much Hair?

This is one of the most common questions patients ask.

Potential contributing factors may include:

  • Stress
  • Hormonal changes
  • Illness
  • Genetics
  • Nutritional considerations
  • Medical conditions

Because several factors may occur simultaneously, a comprehensive evaluation is often helpful.

Hair Shedding vs Hair Thinning

Although the terms are often used interchangeably, they are not the same.

Hair Shedding

Refers to the loss of individual hairs.

Hair Thinning

Refers to reduced hair density and fullness over time.

Many patients experience both simultaneously.

When Should I Be Concerned About Hair Shedding?

Patients may consider evaluation if they notice:

  • Persistent excessive shedding
  • Progressive thinning
  • Increased scalp visibility
  • Bald patches
  • Significant changes in hair density

An evaluation can help identify potential contributing factors.

Hair Restoration Options

Treatment discussions may include:

  • Medical therapies
  • Hair restoration procedures
  • PRP
  • Wellness optimization
  • Lifestyle considerations

Recommendations should always be individualized based on the diagnosis and patient goals.

Why Is Evaluation Important?

Not all hair shedding has the same cause.

Before discussing treatment options, it is important to understand:

  • Hair loss pattern
  • Medical history
  • Hormonal influences
  • Stress factors
  • Family history
  • Previous treatments

A thoughtful evaluation helps guide treatment planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my hair falling out?

Hair shedding may occur because of stress, hormonal changes, illness, genetics, medical conditions, or other factors.

Is hair shedding normal?

Yes.

Some degree of daily shedding is a normal part of the hair growth cycle.

What is Telogen Effluvium?

Telogen Effluvium is a common cause of increased hair shedding that may occur following stress, illness, surgery, or major life events.

Can stress cause hair shedding?

Stress may influence the normal hair growth cycle and contribute to increased shedding.

Why am I losing hair in the shower?

Hair shedding often becomes more noticeable during washing because loose hairs are collected at one time.

Should I have excessive shedding evaluated?

If shedding is persistent, progressive, or associated with thinning, an evaluation may help identify contributing factors.

The Dream Regen Perspective

Hair shedding can be frustrating, especially when it seems to occur suddenly or without an obvious explanation.

The good news is that hair shedding is often a symptom rather than a diagnosis.

The most important step is understanding why it is happening.

By identifying contributing factors and understanding the specific pattern of hair loss, patients can make informed decisions about their options and develop a personalized approach to hair restoration.
