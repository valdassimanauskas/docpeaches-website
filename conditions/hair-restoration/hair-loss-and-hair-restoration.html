Hair Loss & Hair Restoration

Understanding Hair Loss

Hair loss affects millions of men and women and can occur for a variety of reasons.

For some individuals, hair thinning develops gradually over time. Others may notice increased shedding, a widening part, or a receding hairline.

Because many factors can contribute to hair loss, understanding the underlying cause is often the first step toward developing an appropriate treatment plan.

At Dr. Peaches, hair restoration consultations focus on evaluating contributing factors, discussing available treatment options, and helping patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Causes Hair Loss?

Hair loss can occur for many reasons.

Genetics

Hereditary hair loss is one of the most common causes of thinning hair in both men and women.

Hormonal Changes

Hormonal fluctuations may influence hair growth patterns.

Aging

Changes in hair density commonly occur over time.

Medical Conditions

Certain medical conditions may contribute to hair thinning or shedding.

Stress

Physical or emotional stress may affect the normal hair growth cycle.

Nutritional Factors

Dietary and nutritional considerations may play a role in overall hair health.

A comprehensive evaluation may help identify contributing factors.

Common Signs Of Hair Loss

Patients frequently notice:

Thinning Hair

Gradual reduction in hair density.

Receding Hairline

Changes involving the frontal hairline.

Widening Part

Increased visibility of the scalp through the hair.

Increased Shedding

More hair loss during brushing, washing, or styling.

Crown Thinning

Reduced density at the top of the scalp.

The pattern of hair loss often provides important diagnostic information.

Hair Loss In Men

Men commonly experience:

  • Receding hairlines
  • Crown thinning
  • Progressive changes in hair density

These patterns are frequently associated with hereditary hair loss.

Hair Loss In Women

Women may notice:

  • Diffuse thinning
  • Widening of the hair part
  • Reduced overall volume
  • Increased shedding

Because female hair loss can have multiple contributing factors, evaluation is particularly important.

Understanding Hair Restoration

Hair restoration refers to a variety of treatment approaches designed to address hair thinning and hair loss.

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Medical therapies
  • Nutritional considerations
  • Hair restoration procedures
  • Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)
  • Lifestyle factors

Treatment recommendations should always be individualized.

PRP And Hair Restoration

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) has become a commonly discussed option within hair restoration.

PRP is derived from a patient's own blood and contains concentrated platelets and naturally occurring growth factors.

During a consultation, patients may discuss whether PRP is appropriate to consider based on their specific situation and goals.

Why Is Evaluation Important?

Not all hair loss has the same cause.

Before discussing treatment options, it is important to understand:

  • Hair loss pattern
  • Medical history
  • Family history
  • Lifestyle factors
  • Previous treatments
  • Individual goals

An accurate assessment helps guide treatment planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why am I losing my hair?

Hair loss can occur because of genetics, hormonal factors, aging, stress, medical conditions, and other contributing factors.

Is hair loss common?

Yes.

Hair thinning affects both men and women and becomes increasingly common with age.

Can women experience hair loss?

Absolutely.

Hair thinning and shedding affect many women throughout life.

What is hair restoration?

Hair restoration refers to treatment approaches that may be discussed for individuals experiencing hair loss or hair thinning.

What is PRP for hair restoration?

PRP is a platelet-based biologic product derived from a patient's own blood and commonly discussed in hair restoration consultations.

Do I need an evaluation before discussing treatment?

Yes.

Understanding the cause and pattern of hair loss is an important step before considering treatment options.
