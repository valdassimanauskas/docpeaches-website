Understanding Alopecia

Alopecia is a medical term used to describe hair loss.

Many people hear the word "alopecia" and assume it refers to a single condition. In reality, alopecia is a broad term that includes several different types of hair loss, each with unique causes, patterns, and characteristics.

Hair loss may affect:

  • The scalp
  • Eyebrows
  • Beard area
  • Facial hair
  • Other areas of the body

Because different forms of alopecia require different approaches to evaluation and treatment, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important first step.

At Dream Regen, consultations focus on understanding the type of hair loss, identifying contributing factors, and discussing appropriate treatment options based on individual goals.

What Is Alopecia?

The word alopecia simply means:

Hair loss

Alopecia can occur in men and women and may develop gradually or suddenly depending on the underlying cause.

Some forms of alopecia primarily affect the scalp, while others may affect multiple areas of the body.

Common Types Of Alopecia

Androgenetic Alopecia

Also known as:

  • Male Pattern Baldness
  • Female Pattern Hair Loss

This is the most common form of hair loss worldwide.

Patients often notice:

  • Receding hairlines
  • Crown thinning
  • Widening hair parts
  • Progressive thinning

Learn More → Male Pattern Baldness Female Hair Loss

Alopecia Areata

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition that may cause hair loss in localized areas.

Patients often notice:

  • Round patches of hair loss
  • Sudden onset
  • Smooth areas without hair

The pattern and severity can vary significantly.

Telogen Effluvium

Telogen effluvium refers to increased hair shedding that may occur following:

  • Stress
  • Illness
  • Surgery
  • Hormonal changes
  • Significant life events

Many patients describe:

"My hair is falling out everywhere."

Traction Alopecia

This form of hair loss may occur because of prolonged tension on hair follicles.

Examples may include:

  • Tight hairstyles
  • Braids
  • Extensions
  • Repetitive tension

Scarring Alopecia

Certain conditions may lead to scarring of hair follicles.

Because these conditions differ from more common forms of hair loss, evaluation by an appropriate medical professional is important.

What Causes Alopecia?

The cause depends on the specific type of alopecia.

Potential contributing factors include:

Genetics

A major contributor to androgenetic alopecia.

Autoimmune Conditions

Certain forms of alopecia involve immune system activity.

Hormonal Changes

Hormonal fluctuations may affect hair growth cycles.

Stress

Physical and emotional stress may contribute to increased shedding.

Medical Conditions

Certain medical conditions may influence hair growth and hair retention.

Nutritional Factors

Overall nutritional health may impact hair quality and growth patterns.

Symptoms Of Alopecia

Symptoms vary depending on the specific condition.

Common complaints include:

Hair Thinning

Gradual reduction in hair density.

Increased Shedding

More hair loss during brushing, washing, or styling.

Receding Hairline

Often associated with androgenetic alopecia.

Bald Patches

More commonly seen with alopecia areata.

Widening Hair Part

A common finding in women experiencing hair thinning.

Alopecia In Men

Men most commonly experience:

Male Pattern Baldness

Typical signs include:

  • Receding hairline
  • Crown thinning
  • Progressive hair loss

Genetics often play a significant role.

Alopecia In Women

Women may experience:

  • Diffuse thinning
  • Widening hair part
  • Increased shedding
  • Reduced hair volume

Because multiple causes may contribute, evaluation is especially important.

Is Alopecia Permanent?

The answer depends on the specific diagnosis.

Different forms of alopecia behave differently.

Some forms involve progressive thinning over time, while others may follow different patterns.

Understanding the specific type of alopecia is essential before discussing treatment options.

How Is Alopecia Diagnosed?

Evaluation may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, medical conditions, medications, and family history.

Scalp Examination

Assessment of hair density, distribution, and hair loss patterns.

Review Of Contributing Factors

Discussion of:

  • Hormonal influences
  • Stress
  • Lifestyle factors
  • Previous treatments

The goal is to understand the underlying cause of hair loss.

Hair Restoration And Alopecia

Treatment discussions vary depending on the diagnosis.

Potential topics may include:

  • Hair restoration procedures
  • Medical therapies
  • PRP
  • Lifestyle considerations
  • Wellness optimization

Recommendations should always be individualized.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does alopecia mean?

Alopecia is a medical term that refers to hair loss.

Is alopecia the same as male pattern baldness?

Not exactly.

Male pattern baldness is one type of alopecia.

What is alopecia areata?

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition that may cause patchy hair loss.

Why is my hair falling out?

Hair loss may occur because of genetics, hormonal changes, stress, medical conditions, autoimmune factors, and other causes.

Can women develop alopecia?

Yes.

Alopecia affects both men and women.

Is alopecia common?

Yes.

Hair loss is extremely common and affects millions of people.
