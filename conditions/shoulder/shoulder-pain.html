Understanding Shoulder Pain: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Shoulder pain is one of the most common orthopedic complaints affecting active adults, athletes, workers, and individuals simply trying to perform everyday activities.

Whether you're reaching into a cabinet, throwing a baseball, serving a tennis ball, lifting weights, golfing, playing pickleball, or sleeping on your side, the shoulder plays a critical role in daily life.

Because the shoulder is designed to be one of the most mobile joints in the body, it is also susceptible to a wide variety of injuries and conditions.

Understanding the source of shoulder pain is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt?

The shoulder is a complex joint composed of:

  • Bones
  • Tendons
  • Ligaments
  • Muscles
  • Cartilage
  • Bursa
  • Joint surfaces

Because so many structures work together to create movement, pain may originate from several different sources.

Common causes of shoulder pain include:

Rotator Cuff Injuries

One of the most common causes of shoulder pain.

Shoulder Impingement

Irritation involving structures within the shoulder during movement.

Shoulder Arthritis

Age-related or wear-related changes involving the joint.

Frozen Shoulder

A condition associated with pain and loss of motion.

Labral Injuries

Damage involving cartilage structures within the shoulder.

Tendon Conditions

Inflammation, degeneration, or injury involving shoulder tendons.

Sports Injuries

Acute or repetitive stress injuries from athletic activities.

Common Symptoms Of Shoulder Pain

Symptoms vary depending on the underlying condition.

Patients may experience:

  • Pain with overhead activities
  • Difficulty reaching behind the back
  • Pain while lifting objects
  • Night pain
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Weakness
  • Clicking sensations
  • Activity limitations

Some patients notice symptoms only during sports, while others experience pain during routine daily activities.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt At Night?

Night pain is one of the most common shoulder complaints.

Patients frequently report:

  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Waking up because of shoulder pain
  • Pain while rolling over in bed

Several shoulder conditions may contribute to nighttime symptoms.

Because multiple diagnoses can produce similar complaints, evaluation is important.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt When I Lift My Arm?

Pain during lifting or reaching activities may occur when structures within the shoulder become irritated during movement.

Patients often notice symptoms while:

  • Reaching overhead
  • Throwing
  • Lifting weights
  • Serving in tennis
  • Playing pickleball
  • Performing household tasks

Understanding which structures are involved helps guide treatment recommendations.

Shoulder Pain In Athletes

Athletes place unique demands on the shoulder.

Sports commonly associated with shoulder injuries include:

  • Baseball
  • Tennis
  • Pickleball
  • Golf
  • Swimming
  • Volleyball
  • Weightlifting
  • CrossFit

Athletic shoulder pain may result from:

  • Acute injuries
  • Repetitive stress
  • Overuse
  • Biomechanical factors

A comprehensive evaluation helps identify contributing factors.

Shoulder Pain And The Rotator Cuff

The rotator cuff is one of the most important structures in the shoulder and one of the most common sources of shoulder pain.

Patients often hear terms such as:

  • Rotator cuff tear
  • Rotator cuff tendinopathy
  • Rotator cuff injury

However, not every rotator cuff finding on MRI causes symptoms.

This is why physicians evaluate the entire patient rather than relying solely on imaging studies.

Why MRI Findings Don't Always Match Symptoms

Many individuals have MRI abnormalities without significant pain.

Others experience substantial symptoms despite relatively modest imaging findings.

This is why physicians evaluate:

  • Symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging studies

together.

The MRI provides information.

The patient provides context.

How Is Shoulder Pain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Movement assessment
  • Strength testing
  • Functional evaluation

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the true source of symptoms and develop an individualized treatment strategy.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Strength training
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The most appropriate treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Shoulder pain persists.
  • Sleep is affected.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Weakness develops.
  • Range of motion decreases.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward improvement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common cause of shoulder pain?

Several conditions may contribute, including rotator cuff injuries, impingement, arthritis, frozen shoulder, and tendon-related problems.

Why does my shoulder hurt when I sleep?

Night pain is common with several shoulder conditions and should be evaluated in the context of the overall clinical picture.

Can shoulder pain come from the neck?

Yes.

Certain cervical spine conditions may create symptoms that mimic shoulder problems.

Do I need an MRI for shoulder pain?

Not always.

Imaging recommendations depend on symptoms, examination findings, and the suspected diagnosis.

Does shoulder pain always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.

Should I bring prior imaging to my consultation?

Yes.

MRI studies, X-rays, and prior treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
