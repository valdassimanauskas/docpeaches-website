Shoulder Impingement

Understanding Shoulder Impingement: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

One of the most common shoulder diagnoses patients hear is:

"You have shoulder impingement."

Unfortunately, many people leave their appointment without fully understanding what that means.

Patients often wonder:

  • What is being impinged?
  • Why does my shoulder hurt when I lift my arm?
  • Is this the same thing as a rotator cuff tear?
  • Do I need surgery?

The reality is that shoulder impingement is a common source of shoulder pain, particularly among active individuals, athletes, and people who frequently perform overhead activities.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment strategy.

What Is Shoulder Impingement?

Shoulder impingement is a term commonly used to describe irritation involving structures within the shoulder during movement.

As the arm moves, several important structures must function together smoothly.

These include:

  • Rotator cuff tendons
  • Bursa
  • Shoulder joint structures
  • Supporting muscles

When these structures become irritated, compressed, or overloaded during movement, symptoms may develop.

What Causes Shoulder Impingement?

Several factors may contribute, including:

Repetitive Overhead Activity

Repeated overhead motion may increase stress on the shoulder.

Sports Participation

Activities such as:

  • Tennis
  • Pickleball
  • Baseball
  • Swimming
  • Volleyball

may contribute to shoulder irritation.

Rotator Cuff Dysfunction

Changes involving the rotator cuff may alter shoulder mechanics.

Muscle Imbalances

Altered movement patterns may increase stress on the shoulder.

Previous Injuries

Past shoulder injuries may affect normal movement.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of Shoulder Impingement

Patients may experience:

  • Pain when lifting the arm
  • Pain reaching overhead
  • Shoulder aching
  • Pain during exercise
  • Pain while throwing
  • Pain during sports
  • Difficulty lifting objects
  • Night pain
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms often develop gradually over time.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt Between Shoulder Height And Overhead?

Many patients describe a painful range of motion when lifting the arm.

This is one of the classic complaints associated with shoulder impingement.

Patients often report discomfort while:

  • Reaching into cabinets
  • Washing their hair
  • Throwing a ball
  • Serving in tennis
  • Playing pickleball
  • Lifting weights

Because multiple shoulder conditions can create similar symptoms, proper evaluation is important.

Is Shoulder Impingement The Same As A Rotator Cuff Tear?

Not necessarily.

These terms are related but not identical.

Shoulder Impingement

Describes irritation associated with shoulder movement.

Rotator Cuff Tear

Refers to structural changes involving one or more rotator cuff tendons.

Some patients have both conditions simultaneously.

Others do not.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the relationship between symptoms and imaging findings.

Why Does Shoulder Impingement Cause Night Pain?

Many patients with shoulder impingement report:

  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Pain when rolling over
  • Sleep disturbances

Night pain is a common symptom of several shoulder conditions and should be evaluated in the context of the overall diagnosis.

Shoulder Impingement In Athletes

Athletes place unique demands on the shoulder.

Sports frequently associated with shoulder impingement include:

  • Tennis
  • Pickleball
  • Baseball
  • Swimming
  • Volleyball
  • Golf
  • CrossFit
  • Weightlifting

Athletic shoulder pain often involves a combination of movement patterns, training demands, and repetitive loading.

How Is Shoulder Impingement Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Movement assessment
  • Strength testing
  • Functional evaluation

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to determine which structures are contributing to symptoms.

Why MRI Findings Don't Always Explain Symptoms

Many patients become focused on MRI findings.

However, imaging studies must always be interpreted alongside:

  • Symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Activity goals
  • Functional limitations

Some individuals have significant imaging abnormalities and little discomfort.

Others experience substantial pain despite relatively modest imaging findings.

The patient always comes before the MRI.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Strengthening programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Sports-specific rehabilitation
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The most appropriate treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Shoulder pain persists.
  • Overhead activities become difficult.
  • Sports performance is affected.
  • Sleep is disrupted by pain.
  • Weakness develops.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the source of symptoms is often the first step toward improvement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is shoulder impingement?

Shoulder impingement refers to irritation involving structures within the shoulder during movement.

Why does my shoulder hurt when I lift my arm?

Several shoulder conditions may contribute to pain during lifting or overhead motion, including shoulder impingement.

Is shoulder impingement the same as a rotator cuff tear?

No.

Although they may be related, they are different diagnoses.

Why does shoulder impingement hurt at night?

Night pain is common with several shoulder conditions and should be evaluated within the context of the overall diagnosis.

Can athletes continue playing sports?

Many athletes remain active while managing shoulder symptoms, although recommendations should be individualized.

Yes.

MRI studies, X-rays, and treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
