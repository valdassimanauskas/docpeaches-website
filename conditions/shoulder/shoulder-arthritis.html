Shoulder Arthritis

Understanding Shoulder Arthritis: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

When most people think about arthritis, they think about the knees or hips.

However, arthritis can also affect the shoulder and become a significant source of pain, stiffness, and loss of function.

Patients with shoulder arthritis often describe symptoms such as:

  • Pain reaching overhead
  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Stiffness when getting dressed
  • Pain while golfing, lifting weights, or playing pickleball
  • Loss of shoulder motion

Many patients worry that arthritis automatically means surgery.

The reality is often more nuanced.

Understanding what shoulder arthritis is—and how it may relate to your symptoms—is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Is Shoulder Arthritis?

Shoulder arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the shoulder.

Healthy cartilage allows the shoulder to move smoothly.

As joint changes occur over time, patients may develop:

  • Pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Activity limitations

The severity of symptoms varies significantly between individuals.

What Causes Shoulder Arthritis?

Several factors may contribute to shoulder arthritis, including:

Age-Related Changes

Joint changes become more common over time.

Previous Injuries

Past shoulder injuries may affect joint health.

Sports Participation

Years of repetitive use may contribute to joint wear.

Genetics

Some individuals may have a greater predisposition to arthritis.

Prior Surgery

Previous procedures may influence long-term joint mechanics.

Most patients develop arthritis from a combination of factors rather than a single cause.

Common Symptoms Of Shoulder Arthritis

Patients may experience:

  • Shoulder pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Pain with overhead activities
  • Difficulty reaching behind the back
  • Clicking or grinding sensations
  • Night pain
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms often develop gradually over time.

Why Does My Shoulder Feel Stiff?

Loss of motion is one of the most common complaints associated with shoulder arthritis.

Patients frequently notice difficulty:

  • Reaching overhead
  • Putting on a jacket
  • Washing their hair
  • Fastening a bra
  • Reaching into the back seat of a car

As shoulder mobility decreases, daily activities may become increasingly challenging.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt At Night?

Night pain is common among patients with shoulder arthritis.

Many people report:

  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Waking because of shoulder discomfort
  • Pain when rolling over in bed

Several shoulder conditions may contribute to nighttime symptoms, making proper evaluation important.

Does Arthritis Always Cause Pain?

No.

This is one of the most important concepts patients should understand.

Many people have arthritis visible on imaging studies and very little pain.

Others experience substantial symptoms despite relatively modest imaging findings.

This is why physicians evaluate:

  • Symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging studies

together.

The X-ray is important.

The patient is more important.

How Is Shoulder Arthritis Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Movement assessment
  • Strength testing
  • Functional evaluation

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to understand how imaging findings relate to symptoms and quality of life.

Shoulder Arthritis vs Rotator Cuff Tear

Patients often have difficulty understanding the difference.

Shoulder Arthritis

Involves the joint surfaces and cartilage.

Rotator Cuff Tear

Involves the tendons responsible for shoulder stability and movement.

Some patients may have both conditions simultaneously.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine which structures are contributing most significantly to symptoms.

Can You Exercise With Shoulder Arthritis?

In many cases, yes.

Remaining active is often an important component of maintaining function and overall health.

Exercise recommendations should be individualized based on:

  • Symptoms
  • Activity goals
  • Shoulder function
  • Overall health

The objective is often to help patients continue participating in meaningful activities whenever possible.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings
  • Overall shoulder health

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Strengthening programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Exercise progression strategies
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Shoulder pain is becoming more frequent.
  • Sleep is affected.
  • Range of motion is decreasing.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Daily activities are becoming difficult.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.
