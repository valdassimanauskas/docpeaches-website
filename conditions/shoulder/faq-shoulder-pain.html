FAQ Shoulder Pain

Frequently Asked Questions About Shoulder Pain

Shoulder pain is one of the most common reasons patients seek orthopedic and sports medicine care.

Because the shoulder is involved in nearly every upper-body movement, symptoms can affect work, exercise, sleep, sports, and everyday activities.

Below are some of the most common questions patients ask about shoulder pain.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt?

Shoulder pain may originate from several different structures, including:

  • Rotator cuff tendons
  • Shoulder joint cartilage
  • Bursa
  • Labrum
  • Ligaments
  • Muscles
  • Tendons
  • The cervical spine (neck)

An accurate diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt At Night?

Night pain is one of the most common shoulder complaints.

Patients often report:

  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Waking because of shoulder pain
  • Pain when rolling over in bed

Several conditions may contribute, including:

  • Rotator cuff injuries
  • Shoulder impingement
  • Arthritis
  • Frozen shoulder

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the source.

Why Can't I Lift My Arm Overhead?

Difficulty lifting the arm may occur for several reasons, including:

  • Rotator cuff pathology
  • Frozen shoulder
  • Arthritis
  • Shoulder impingement
  • Pain-related weakness

Understanding the cause of limited motion is an important part of the diagnostic process.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt When I Reach Behind My Back?

Patients commonly notice pain or stiffness when:

  • Fastening a bra
  • Tucking in a shirt
  • Reaching into a back pocket
  • Putting on a jacket

This symptom may occur with several shoulder conditions, particularly those affecting mobility.

Why Does My Shoulder Click Or Pop?

Clicking and popping are common.

Many people experience these sensations without pain or injury.

However, clicking associated with:

  • Pain
  • Weakness
  • Catching
  • Functional limitations

may warrant evaluation.

Is Shoulder Popping Normal?

In many cases, yes.

Occasional popping without pain is often not concerning.

When popping is accompanied by discomfort or dysfunction, further evaluation may be appropriate.

Why Does My Shoulder Feel Weak?

Weakness may occur because of:

  • Rotator cuff conditions
  • Pain inhibition
  • Muscle dysfunction
  • Neurological conditions
  • Previous injuries

The specific cause varies from patient to patient.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt When I Throw?

Throwing places significant demands on the shoulder.

Pain during throwing activities may involve:

  • Rotator cuff pathology
  • Labral injuries
  • Shoulder impingement
  • Overuse conditions

Athletes should consider evaluation if symptoms persist.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt During Pickleball?

Pickleball involves:

  • Repetitive overhead movements
  • Quick reactions
  • Rotational forces
  • Repeated serving motions

Several shoulder conditions may contribute to symptoms in pickleball players.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt During Golf?

The golf swing creates substantial rotational forces through the shoulder.

Pain may be associated with:

  • Rotator cuff conditions
  • Arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Mobility limitations

A comprehensive evaluation helps identify contributing factors.

Is It My Shoulder Or My Neck?

This is one of the most common questions patients ask.

Certain neck conditions may create symptoms that feel like shoulder problems.

Likewise, true shoulder conditions may mimic cervical spine disorders.

A comprehensive evaluation often helps distinguish between the two.

What Is A Rotator Cuff Tear?

A rotator cuff tear is an injury involving one or more tendons that help stabilize and move the shoulder.

Rotator cuff tears may be:

  • Partial tears
  • Full-thickness tears
  • Degenerative tears
  • Traumatic tears

Not every tear causes symptoms.

Can I Have A Rotator Cuff Tear Without Pain?

Yes.

MRI studies frequently identify rotator cuff tears in individuals who have minimal symptoms or no symptoms at all.

This is why imaging findings must always be interpreted within the context of the patient.

Does Every Rotator Cuff Tear Require Surgery?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Tear characteristics
  • Overall shoulder health

Every patient deserves an individualized discussion.

What Is Frozen Shoulder?

Frozen shoulder, also known as adhesive capsulitis, is a condition involving:

  • Shoulder pain
  • Progressive stiffness
  • Loss of motion

Many patients describe feeling as though the shoulder is "stuck."

How Do I Know If I Have Frozen Shoulder?

Common symptoms may include:

  • Difficulty reaching overhead
  • Difficulty reaching behind the back
  • Progressive loss of motion
  • Night pain
  • Shoulder stiffness

A comprehensive evaluation helps establish the diagnosis.

What Is Shoulder Impingement?

Shoulder impingement is a term used to describe irritation involving structures within the shoulder during movement.

Patients commonly experience pain when:

  • Reaching overhead
  • Lifting objects
  • Throwing
  • Performing athletic activities

What Is Shoulder Arthritis?

Shoulder arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the shoulder.

Common symptoms include:

  • Pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Activity limitations

Does Shoulder Arthritis Mean I Need Shoulder Replacement?

No.

Many patients successfully manage shoulder arthritis without surgery.

Treatment recommendations should be individualized.

Can I Exercise With Shoulder Arthritis?

Many individuals remain active despite arthritis.

Exercise recommendations should be tailored to symptoms, goals, and shoulder function.

Should I Stop Exercising If My Shoulder Hurts?

Not necessarily.

The answer depends on:

  • The diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Activity goals
  • Functional limitations

Activity recommendations should always be individualized.

Do I Need An MRI?

Not always.

Many shoulder conditions can be evaluated effectively through:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • X-rays

MRI studies may be appropriate in selected situations.

What Is The Difference Between An MRI And X-Ray?

X-Ray

Primarily evaluates:

  • Bone
  • Joint space
  • Arthritis

MRI

Provides additional information regarding:

  • Rotator cuff tendons
  • Labrum
  • Cartilage
  • Ligaments
  • Soft tissues

Both studies may play important roles in the diagnostic process.

Can PRP Help Shoulder Pain?

PRP is one of several treatment options that may be discussed depending on the diagnosis and individual patient circumstances.

Treatment decisions should always be based on a comprehensive evaluation.

When Should I See A Specialist For Shoulder Pain?

You should consider evaluation if:

  • Pain persists.
  • Sleep is affected.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Weakness develops.
  • Motion decreases.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Shoulder pain is common.

The challenge is identifying the true source of symptoms.

Many different structures can create similar complaints, which is why diagnosis should always come before treatment.

Whether the goal is golfing, playing pickleball, swimming, lifting weights, working comfortably, or simply sleeping through the night, every treatment plan should begin with understanding the individual patient.
