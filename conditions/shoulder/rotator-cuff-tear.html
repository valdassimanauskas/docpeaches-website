Rotator Cuff Tear

Understanding Rotator Cuff Tears: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Few shoulder diagnoses create more concern than hearing:

"You have a rotator cuff tear."

Many patients immediately assume:

  • Surgery is inevitable.
  • Their shoulder will never be the same.
  • They should stop exercising.
  • They have permanently damaged their shoulder.

The reality is often far more nuanced.

Rotator cuff tears are common, particularly as we age. In fact, many people have rotator cuff tears visible on MRI studies and experience little or no pain.

The most important question is not:

"Do I have a rotator cuff tear?"

The most important question is:

"Is the tear responsible for my symptoms?"

Understanding the diagnosis is the first step toward making informed decisions about treatment.

What Is The Rotator Cuff?

The rotator cuff is a group of muscles and tendons that surround the shoulder joint.

The rotator cuff helps:

  • Stabilize the shoulder
  • Support movement
  • Assist with lifting
  • Assist with reaching
  • Assist with throwing activities
  • Maintain shoulder function

Because the shoulder is one of the most mobile joints in the body, the rotator cuff plays a critical role in both stability and performance.

What Is A Rotator Cuff Tear?

A rotator cuff tear occurs when one or more of the rotator cuff tendons become partially or completely disrupted.

Rotator cuff injuries exist on a spectrum and may range from:

Tendinopathy

Changes involving the tendon without a tear.

Partial Thickness Tear

Only part of the tendon is affected.

Full Thickness Tear

The tendon is completely disrupted.

Every tear is different, which is why individualized evaluation is important.

What Causes Rotator Cuff Tears?

Several factors may contribute, including:

Age-Related Changes

Rotator cuff abnormalities become increasingly common over time.

Repetitive Overhead Activity

Sports and occupations involving overhead motion may place stress on the shoulder.

Sports Participation

Activities such as:

  • Tennis
  • Pickleball
  • Baseball
  • Swimming
  • Volleyball

may contribute to shoulder injuries.

Traumatic Injuries

Falls and sudden injuries may result in rotator cuff tears.

Degenerative Changes

Some tears develop gradually over time.

Common Symptoms Of A Rotator Cuff Tear

Patients may experience:

  • Shoulder pain
  • Weakness
  • Pain while lifting
  • Difficulty reaching overhead
  • Night pain
  • Difficulty sleeping
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms vary significantly from person to person.

Can You Have A Rotator Cuff Tear Without Pain?

Yes.

This is one of the most important concepts patients should understand.

MRI studies frequently identify rotator cuff tears in people who have:

  • No shoulder pain
  • Minimal symptoms
  • Normal function

This is why physicians evaluate:

  • Symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging studies

together.

The MRI finding alone does not determine treatment.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt At Night?

Night pain is one of the most common complaints associated with shoulder conditions.

Patients often report:

  • Difficulty sleeping on the affected side
  • Pain when rolling over
  • Waking from sleep due to discomfort

Several shoulder conditions may contribute to nighttime symptoms, including rotator cuff pathology.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the source.

How Is A Rotator Cuff Tear Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Strength testing
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • X-rays
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to determine whether the tear is actually contributing to symptoms.

Does Every Rotator Cuff Tear Require Surgery?

No.

This is one of the most common misconceptions regarding rotator cuff injuries.

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Tear characteristics
  • Overall shoulder health

Every patient deserves an individualized discussion regarding available options.

Can Athletes Return To Sports?

Many athletes successfully return to activity following rotator cuff injuries.

The appropriate treatment pathway depends on:

  • The sport
  • Performance demands
  • Shoulder function
  • Individual goals

Return-to-sport decisions should always be individualized.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Strengthening programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Sports-specific rehabilitation
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a rotator cuff tear?

A rotator cuff tear is a disruption involving one or more of the tendons that help stabilize and move the shoulder.

Can I have a rotator cuff tear without pain?

Yes.

Many people have rotator cuff tears visible on MRI studies without experiencing symptoms.

Does every rotator cuff tear require surgery?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on symptoms, goals, shoulder function, and tear characteristics.

Why does my shoulder hurt more at night?

Night pain is common with several shoulder conditions and should be evaluated in the context of the overall diagnosis.

Can I continue exercising with a rotator cuff tear?

Many patients remain active, although recommendations should be individualized.

Should I bring my MRI to my consultation?

Absolutely.

MRI studies, imaging reports, and prior treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
