Morton's Neuroma

Understanding Morton's Neuroma: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Many patients with Morton's Neuroma spend months—or even years—trying to understand why their foot hurts.

They often describe symptoms such as:

"It feels like I'm walking on a pebble."

"My toes go numb when I walk."

"I get a burning pain in the ball of my foot."

Because these symptoms can mimic several other foot conditions, Morton's Neuroma is frequently misunderstood or misdiagnosed.

Understanding the condition is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Is Morton's Neuroma?

Morton's Neuroma is a condition involving irritation of a nerve in the forefoot.

The condition most commonly affects the area between the toes, particularly between the third and fourth toes.

As irritation develops, patients may experience:

  • Burning pain
  • Numbness
  • Tingling
  • Forefoot discomfort
  • Activity limitations

Despite the name, Morton's Neuroma is not typically considered a tumor.

It is more accurately viewed as a nerve-related condition affecting the forefoot.

What Causes Morton's Neuroma?

Several factors may contribute, including:

Repetitive Stress

Repeated pressure on the forefoot may contribute to symptoms.

Footwear

Certain shoes may increase pressure on structures in the forefoot.

Sports Participation

Activities involving repetitive loading of the forefoot may contribute.

Foot Mechanics

Individual foot structure and movement patterns may influence stress on the nerve.

Prolonged Standing Or Walking

Symptoms often worsen with activities that increase pressure through the forefoot.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of Morton's Neuroma

Patients may experience:

  • Burning pain in the forefoot
  • Numbness in the toes
  • Tingling sensations
  • Sharp pain while walking
  • Pain in the ball of the foot
  • A sensation of walking on a pebble
  • Discomfort during exercise
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms may come and go, especially during the early stages.

Why Does It Feel Like I'm Walking On A Pebble?

This is one of the classic descriptions associated with Morton's Neuroma.

Patients frequently report:

"It feels like something is stuck inside my shoe."

Even when no object is present, nerve irritation may create this sensation.

This symptom is often an important clue during evaluation.

Why Do My Toes Go Numb?

As the affected nerve becomes irritated, patients may experience:

  • Numbness
  • Tingling
  • Altered sensation

These symptoms often involve the toes adjacent to the affected nerve.

What Activities Make Morton's Neuroma Worse?

Many patients notice symptoms during:

  • Walking
  • Running
  • Hiking
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Golf
  • Standing for long periods

Activities that increase pressure through the forefoot may aggravate symptoms.

Morton's Neuroma In Athletes

Athletes place significant demands on the feet.

Sports commonly associated with forefoot symptoms include:

  • Running
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Hiking
  • Golf
  • Basketball

Athletic symptoms often become more noticeable with increased activity levels.

Understanding the athlete's goals is an important part of treatment planning.

Morton's Neuroma vs Plantar Fasciitis

Patients sometimes confuse these conditions because both involve foot pain.

Morton's Neuroma

Typically causes pain in the ball of the foot and toes.

Plantar Fasciitis

Usually causes pain near the heel.

The location of symptoms often helps distinguish between the two.

How Is Morton's Neuroma Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • Ultrasound
  • MRI
  • X-rays
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and rule out other causes of forefoot pain.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Duration of symptoms
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Footwear modifications
  • Activity modifications
  • Physical therapy
  • Orthotics
  • Interventional procedures
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

Can You Continue Exercising?

Many patients remain active while managing Morton's Neuroma.

The appropriate approach depends on:

  • Symptoms
  • Activity type
  • Functional limitations
  • Individual goals

The objective is often to help patients remain active while addressing factors contributing to symptoms.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Burning pain develops in the forefoot.
  • Toes become numb or tingly.
  • Walking becomes uncomfortable.
  • Exercise participation is limited.
  • Symptoms continue to worsen.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Morton's Neuroma?

Morton's Neuroma is a nerve-related condition that commonly affects the area between the toes and causes forefoot pain.

Why does it feel like I'm walking on a pebble?

This sensation is one of the most common symptoms associated with Morton's Neuroma.

Why are my toes numb?

Nerve irritation may cause numbness, tingling, or altered sensation in the toes.

Can athletes develop Morton's Neuroma?

Yes.

The condition commonly affects runners, golfers, pickleball players, tennis players, and other active individuals.

Do I need imaging?

Imaging recommendations depend on symptoms, examination findings, and the suspected diagnosis.

Does Morton's Neuroma always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their symptoms and goals.
