FAQ Ankle Pain

Frequently Asked Questions About Ankle Pain

Ankle pain is one of the most common reasons people seek orthopedic and sports medicine care.

Because the ankle plays a critical role in walking, running, exercising, sports participation, and everyday movement, even minor symptoms can significantly affect quality of life.

Below are some of the most common questions patients ask about ankle pain.

Why Does My Ankle Hurt?

Ankle pain may originate from several different structures, including:

  • Ligaments
  • Tendons
  • Joints
  • Bones
  • Muscles
  • Nerves

Common causes include:

  • Ankle sprains
  • Achilles tendon conditions
  • Arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Sports injuries
  • Overuse conditions

An accurate diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

Why Does My Ankle Hurt When I Walk?

Walking places significant force through the ankle joint and surrounding structures.

Pain while walking may be associated with:

  • Ankle sprains
  • Arthritis
  • Tendon disorders
  • Sports injuries
  • Previous ankle injuries

The specific cause varies from patient to patient.

Why Does My Ankle Feel Weak?

Patients often describe symptoms such as:

"My ankle doesn't feel stable."

Weakness may occur because of:

  • Previous sprains
  • Ligament injuries
  • Muscle dysfunction
  • Pain-related compensation
  • Chronic instability

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the underlying cause.

Why Does My Ankle Keep Rolling?

Repeated ankle injuries may occur when ankle stability is compromised.

Patients with recurrent ankle sprains often report:

  • Difficulty on uneven surfaces
  • Lack of confidence during activity
  • Frequent twisting injuries

Persistent instability should be evaluated.

What Is An Ankle Sprain?

An ankle sprain is an injury involving one or more ligaments that help stabilize the ankle.

Common symptoms include:

  • Pain
  • Swelling
  • Bruising
  • Instability
  • Difficulty walking

Ankle sprains are among the most common orthopedic injuries.

Can I Walk On A Sprained Ankle?

Sometimes.

Many patients can walk despite having a significant ankle sprain.

The ability to walk does not necessarily indicate that an injury is minor.

Why Does My Ankle Still Hurt Months After A Sprain?

Most ankle sprains improve over time.

However, some patients continue to experience:

  • Pain
  • Swelling
  • Instability
  • Recurrent injuries

Persistent symptoms may warrant further evaluation.

Why Does The Back Of My Ankle Hurt?

Pain at the back of the ankle is often associated with:

  • Achilles tendinopathy
  • Achilles tendon injuries
  • Tendon-related conditions

The specific diagnosis depends on the location and pattern of symptoms.

What Is Achilles Tendinopathy?

Achilles tendinopathy is a condition involving irritation and structural changes within the Achilles tendon.

Common symptoms include:

  • Morning stiffness
  • Pain during exercise
  • Pain after activity
  • Tenderness along the tendon

Why Does My Achilles Hurt In The Morning?

Morning stiffness is a common symptom associated with Achilles tendinopathy.

Many patients notice symptoms during their first steps of the day.

What Does An Achilles Tear Feel Like?

Many patients describe:

"A pop."

or

"It felt like someone kicked me in the back of the leg."

Symptoms vary depending on the extent of the injury.

Can I Walk With An Achilles Tear?

Sometimes.

The ability to walk does not necessarily rule out a significant Achilles injury.

Prompt evaluation is important when an Achilles tear is suspected.

What Is Ankle Arthritis?

Ankle arthritis refers to changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces of the ankle.

Common symptoms include:

  • Pain
  • Swelling
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Difficulty walking

Why Does My Ankle Feel Stiff?

Stiffness may occur because of:

  • Arthritis
  • Previous injuries
  • Joint-related conditions
  • Tendon disorders

The timing and location of symptoms often help guide diagnosis.

Can I Exercise With Ankle Arthritis?

Many individuals remain active despite arthritis.

Exercise recommendations should be individualized based on symptoms, function, and goals.

Can Pickleball Cause Ankle Pain?

Yes.

Pickleball involves:

  • Quick directional changes
  • Lateral movements
  • Repetitive loading

which may contribute to ankle injuries and symptoms in some individuals.

Can Running Cause Ankle Pain?

Yes.

Running places repetitive forces through the ankle and surrounding structures.

Several conditions may contribute to symptoms in runners.

Can Golf Cause Ankle Pain?

Yes.

Walking, uneven terrain, and repetitive movement patterns may contribute to symptoms in some golfers.

Why Is My Ankle Swollen?

Swelling may occur because of:

  • Sprains
  • Arthritis
  • Tendon injuries
  • Joint irritation
  • Overuse conditions

Persistent swelling should be evaluated.

Do I Need An MRI?

Not always.

Many ankle conditions can be evaluated effectively through:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • X-rays

MRI studies may be appropriate in selected situations.

What Is The Difference Between An MRI And X-Ray?

X-Ray

Primarily evaluates:

  • Bone
  • Joint alignment
  • Arthritis

MRI

Provides additional information regarding:

  • Ligaments
  • Tendons
  • Cartilage
  • Soft tissues

Both studies may play important roles in the diagnostic process.

Does Ankle Pain Always Require Surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals

Treatment recommendations should always be individualized.

When Should I See A Specialist For Ankle Pain?

You should consider evaluation if:

  • Pain persists.
  • Walking becomes difficult.
  • Swelling continues.
  • Instability develops.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Ankle pain is common.

The challenge is determining why the ankle hurts.

Because multiple structures can create similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step in the treatment process.

Whether the goal is golfing, pickleball, running, hiking, travel, exercise, work, or simply walking comfortably, treatment should be individualized and centered around helping patients remain active.
