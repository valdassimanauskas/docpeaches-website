Ankle Sprain

Understanding Ankle Sprains: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Ankle sprains are among the most common orthopedic injuries seen in athletes and active adults.

Many patients hear the phrase:

"It's just a sprain."

Unfortunately, that description can sometimes minimize the impact an ankle sprain may have on long-term ankle health and function.

While many sprains improve with time, others may contribute to:

  • Ongoing pain
  • Recurrent ankle injuries
  • Chronic instability
  • Difficulty returning to sports

Understanding the injury is often the first step toward making informed decisions about treatment and recovery.

What Is An Ankle Sprain?

An ankle sprain occurs when one or more ligaments of the ankle are stretched or injured.

Ligaments are strong bands of tissue that help:

  • Stabilize joints
  • Guide movement
  • Prevent excessive motion

When forces exceed what the ligament can tolerate, an injury may occur.

The severity of a sprain can vary significantly.

How Do Ankle Sprains Happen?

Most ankle sprains occur when the foot unexpectedly rolls or twists.

Common situations include:

  • Stepping off a curb
  • Sports participation
  • Uneven ground
  • Running
  • Hiking
  • Jumping and landing
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Basketball

The injury may occur suddenly and often without warning.

Common Symptoms Of An Ankle Sprain

Patients may experience:

  • Ankle pain
  • Swelling
  • Bruising
  • Tenderness
  • Difficulty walking
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Instability
  • Activity limitations

Symptoms vary depending on the severity of the injury.

Why Is The Outside Of My Ankle Hurting?

Most ankle sprains involve ligaments located along the outer aspect of the ankle.

As a result, patients frequently notice:

  • Pain on the outside of the ankle
  • Swelling near the outer ankle bone
  • Tenderness in that region

However, symptoms can occur in other areas depending on the specific injury pattern.

Can You Walk On A Sprained Ankle?

Sometimes.

Many patients are able to walk despite having a significant ankle sprain.

The ability to walk does not necessarily indicate that the injury is minor.

This is one reason why proper evaluation can be important, particularly when symptoms persist.

Why Does My Ankle Still Hurt Months Later?

Most ankle sprains improve over time.

However, some patients continue to experience:

  • Pain
  • Instability
  • Swelling
  • Recurrent injuries

Persistent symptoms may indicate that additional evaluation is warranted.

Understanding the reason symptoms continue is often the key to identifying appropriate treatment options.

What Is Chronic Ankle Instability?

Some patients describe the sensation that:

"My ankle keeps giving out."

Repeated sprains or incomplete recovery may contribute to ongoing instability.

Patients with chronic ankle instability may experience:

  • Recurrent sprains
  • Difficulty on uneven surfaces
  • Reduced confidence during activity
  • Limitations in sports participation

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the underlying cause.

Ankle Sprains In Athletes

Athletes frequently experience ankle sprains during:

  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Basketball
  • Soccer
  • Volleyball
  • Running
  • Hiking

Because athletes often want to return to activity quickly, understanding the severity of the injury is important when discussing recovery and return-to-sport decisions.

How Is An Ankle Sprain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Stability assessment
  • Functional evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to understand the extent of the injury and identify any associated conditions.

Do All Ankle Sprains Heal The Same Way?

No.

Ankle sprains vary significantly in severity.

Some involve minor stretching of ligament fibers.

Others involve more significant injury patterns.

Every patient deserves an individualized evaluation and treatment discussion.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Injury severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Activity goals
  • Stability concerns
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Balance training
  • Strengthening programs
  • Bracing
  • Activity modifications
  • Sports-specific rehabilitation
  • Interventional procedures
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Pain persists after an ankle injury.
  • Swelling continues.
  • Walking remains difficult.
  • The ankle feels unstable.
  • Recurrent sprains occur.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an ankle sprain?

An ankle sprain is an injury involving one or more ligaments that help stabilize the ankle.

Can I walk on a sprained ankle?

Many patients can walk despite a sprain, although the ability to walk does not necessarily indicate a minor injury.

Why does my ankle keep giving out?

Recurrent instability may occur after previous ankle injuries and should be evaluated if symptoms persist.

Why is the outside of my ankle hurting?

Many ankle sprains involve ligaments located on the outer aspect of the ankle.

Do all ankle sprains require surgery?

No.

Most patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on the severity of the injury and their goals.

Should I bring prior imaging to my consultation?

Yes.

Imaging studies and treatment records provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
