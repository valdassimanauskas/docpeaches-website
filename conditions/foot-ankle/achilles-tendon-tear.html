Achilles Tendon Tear

Understanding Achilles Tendon Tears: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Few injuries create as much concern as an Achilles tendon tear.

Many patients describe the injury with phrases such as:

"It felt like someone kicked me in the back of the leg."

"I heard a pop."

"I suddenly couldn't push off normally."

Achilles tendon tears often occur unexpectedly and can affect both competitive athletes and recreationally active adults.

Because the Achilles tendon plays such an important role in walking, running, jumping, and athletic performance, understanding the injury is often the first step toward making informed decisions about treatment and recovery.

What Is The Achilles Tendon?

The Achilles tendon is the largest tendon in the body.

It connects the calf muscles to the heel bone and helps generate the force necessary for:

  • Walking
  • Running
  • Jumping
  • Climbing stairs
  • Athletic activities

Every step places stress through the Achilles tendon.

As a result, injury to this structure can significantly affect mobility and function.

What Is An Achilles Tendon Tear?

An Achilles tendon tear occurs when tendon fibers become partially or completely disrupted.

Patients may hear terms such as:

Partial Tear

Only a portion of the tendon is affected.

Complete Tear

The tendon is completely disrupted.

The severity of symptoms and treatment considerations vary depending on the nature of the injury.

How Do Achilles Tendon Tears Occur?

Many injuries occur during activities involving sudden forceful movement.

Common scenarios include:

  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Basketball
  • Soccer
  • Running
  • Sprinting
  • Jumping

Patients often describe feeling a sudden pop followed by immediate discomfort or weakness.

However, not every Achilles tear occurs during sports.

Some injuries occur during routine daily activities.

Common Symptoms Of An Achilles Tendon Tear

Patients may experience:

  • Sudden pain in the back of the ankle
  • A popping sensation
  • Difficulty pushing off
  • Weakness
  • Trouble walking
  • Swelling
  • Bruising
  • Reduced athletic function

Symptoms vary depending on the extent of the injury.

What Does An Achilles Tear Feel Like?

Many patients describe:

"It felt like someone kicked me in the back of the leg."

Others report:

"I heard or felt a pop."

While these descriptions are common, symptoms vary from person to person.

Can You Walk With An Achilles Tendon Tear?

Sometimes.

Many patients are surprised to learn they can still walk after an Achilles injury.

The ability to walk does not necessarily rule out a significant tear.

This is one reason why proper evaluation is important when symptoms suggest an Achilles injury.

Achilles Tendinopathy vs Achilles Tear

Patients frequently wonder whether ongoing Achilles pain means the tendon has torn.

Achilles Tendinopathy

Typically involves tendon irritation and structural changes.

Achilles Tendon Tear

Involves a disruption of tendon fibers.

These conditions are different and may require different treatment discussions.

A comprehensive evaluation helps clarify the diagnosis.

Achilles Tears In Athletes

Athletes place substantial demands on the Achilles tendon.

Sports commonly associated with Achilles tears include:

  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Basketball
  • Soccer
  • Running
  • CrossFit

Because athletes often wish to return to activity as safely and efficiently as possible, individualized treatment planning is particularly important.

How Is An Achilles Tendon Tear Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Strength evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • Ultrasound
  • MRI
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to determine the extent of injury and understand how it affects function.

Does Every Achilles Tear Require Surgery?

No.

This is one of the most common misconceptions regarding Achilles injuries.

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Injury characteristics
  • Functional demands
  • Activity goals
  • Overall health
  • Individual circumstances

Every patient deserves an individualized discussion regarding available treatment options.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Injury severity
  • Activity goals
  • Functional limitations
  • Imaging findings
  • Overall health

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Immobilization
  • Bracing
  • Physical therapy
  • Rehabilitation programs
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The most appropriate treatment strategy is individualized.

Can Athletes Return To Sports?

Many athletes successfully return to activity following Achilles injuries.

Return-to-sport decisions depend on:

  • Healing progression
  • Strength
  • Function
  • Sport-specific demands

The goal is to help patients safely return to the activities that matter most to them.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You should seek prompt evaluation if:

  • You felt a pop in the back of the ankle.
  • Walking suddenly becomes difficult.
  • Push-off strength decreases.
  • Significant swelling develops.
  • Athletic participation becomes impossible.
  • You suspect an Achilles injury.

Early evaluation helps clarify the diagnosis and guide treatment decisions.
