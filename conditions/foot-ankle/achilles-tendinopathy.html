Achilles Tendinopathy

Understanding Achilles Tendinopathy: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Pain at the back of the ankle can be frustrating, especially for active individuals who enjoy walking, running, golfing, pickleball, tennis, hiking, and exercise.

One of the most common causes of pain in this region is Achilles tendinopathy.

Patients frequently describe symptoms such as:

"My Achilles feels tight when I first start moving."

"The back of my ankle hurts after activity."

"I can still exercise, but the pain keeps coming back."

Achilles tendinopathy is a common condition affecting athletes and non-athletes alike.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Is The Achilles Tendon?

The Achilles tendon is the largest tendon in the body.

It connects the calf muscles to the heel bone and plays a critical role in:

  • Walking
  • Running
  • Jumping
  • Climbing stairs
  • Athletic performance

Because the Achilles tendon is subjected to substantial forces during daily activities and sports, it can become a source of pain and dysfunction.

What Is Achilles Tendinopathy?

Achilles tendinopathy is a condition involving irritation and structural changes within the Achilles tendon.

Patients may hear terms such as:

  • Achilles tendonitis
  • Achilles tendinosis
  • Achilles tendinopathy

Although these terms are often used interchangeably, Achilles tendinopathy is commonly used as a broader description of tendon-related symptoms.

What Causes Achilles Tendinopathy?

Several factors may contribute, including:

Repetitive Stress

Repeated loading of the tendon during activity.

Running

One of the most common athletic contributors.

Pickleball And Tennis

Sports involving acceleration, deceleration, and rapid changes in direction.

Sudden Changes In Activity

Increasing exercise intensity or volume too quickly may contribute.

Biomechanical Factors

Movement patterns and lower-extremity mechanics may influence tendon loading.

Age-Related Changes

Tendons may become more susceptible to injury over time.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Symptoms Of Achilles Tendinopathy

Patients may experience:

  • Pain at the back of the ankle
  • Morning stiffness
  • Tenderness along the tendon
  • Pain during exercise
  • Pain after activity
  • Tightness
  • Difficulty running
  • Reduced athletic performance

Symptoms often develop gradually rather than after a single injury.

Why Does My Achilles Hurt In The Morning?

Many patients describe:

"My Achilles feels stiff when I first get out of bed."

Morning stiffness is a common symptom associated with Achilles tendinopathy.

Symptoms may improve somewhat with movement before returning later in the day.

Why Does My Achilles Hurt After Exercise?

Achilles tendinopathy often follows a pattern where symptoms become noticeable:

  • During activity
  • Immediately after activity
  • Later in the day
  • The following morning

This pattern frequently helps physicians identify tendon-related conditions.

Achilles Tendinopathy In Athletes

Athletes place significant demands on the Achilles tendon.

Sports commonly associated with Achilles symptoms include:

  • Running
  • Pickleball
  • Tennis
  • Basketball
  • Soccer
  • Hiking
  • CrossFit

Athletic symptoms often develop gradually over time.

Understanding the athlete's goals is an important part of treatment planning.

Achilles Tendinopathy vs Achilles Tendon Tear

Patients often worry that persistent Achilles pain means the tendon is torn.

Achilles Tendinopathy

Typically involves irritation and structural tendon changes.

Achilles Tendon Tear

Involves a partial or complete disruption of tendon fibers.

These conditions are different and may require different treatment considerations.

A comprehensive evaluation helps clarify the diagnosis.

How Is Achilles Tendinopathy Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • Ultrasound
  • MRI
  • X-rays
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to understand the condition of the tendon and identify factors contributing to symptoms.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Symptom severity
  • Activity goals
  • Functional limitations
  • Duration of symptoms
  • Imaging findings

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Strengthening programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Exercise progression strategies
  • Footwear recommendations
  • Interventional procedures when appropriate

The best treatment strategy is individualized.

Can You Continue Exercising?

Many patients remain active while managing Achilles tendinopathy.

The appropriate approach depends on:

  • Symptoms
  • Activity type
  • Functional limitations
  • Individual goals

The objective is often to help patients remain active while addressing the factors contributing to symptoms.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Achilles pain persists.
  • Walking becomes uncomfortable.
  • Running is limited.
  • Morning stiffness continues to worsen.
  • Sports participation is affected.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.
