FAQ Knee Pain

Frequently Asked Questions About Knee Pain

Knee pain is one of the most common reasons people seek orthopedic care.

Whether you're an athlete, weekend warrior, golfer, pickleball player, runner, or simply trying to stay active, knee pain can interfere with daily life and the activities you enjoy.

Below are some of the most common questions patients ask about knee pain.

Why Does My Knee Hurt?

Knee pain may originate from several different structures within the knee.

Common causes include:

  • Arthritis
  • Meniscus tears
  • Ligament injuries
  • Tendon disorders
  • Overuse injuries
  • Patellofemoral conditions
  • Sports injuries

An accurate diagnosis is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options.

How Do I Know If My Knee Pain Is Serious?

You should consider evaluation if:

  • Pain persists
  • Swelling develops
  • Walking becomes difficult
  • Instability occurs
  • Sports participation is limited
  • Symptoms interfere with daily activities

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the significance of symptoms.

Why Does My Knee Hurt When I Walk?

Walking places force through the knee joint and surrounding structures.

Pain while walking may be associated with:

  • Arthritis
  • Meniscus injuries
  • Tendon conditions
  • Cartilage abnormalities
  • Other joint-related problems

The specific cause depends on the individual patient.

Why Does My Knee Hurt Going Up Stairs?

Pain during stair climbing may occur with several conditions, including:

  • Arthritis
  • Patellofemoral pain syndrome
  • Tendon disorders
  • Meniscus pathology

The location of pain often provides important diagnostic clues.

Why Does My Knee Hurt Going Down Stairs?

Many patients report more discomfort descending stairs than climbing them.

This may occur because stair descent increases forces traveling through portions of the knee joint.

Several diagnoses may contribute to this symptom pattern.

Why Does My Knee Hurt After Sitting?

Patients frequently describe stiffness or discomfort after:

  • Driving
  • Flying
  • Sitting at work
  • Watching movies

This symptom may occur with several knee conditions and should be evaluated within the context of the overall clinical picture.

Why Does My Knee Click Or Pop?

Clicking and popping are common.

In many cases, these sensations occur without significant underlying pathology.

However, clicking associated with:

  • Pain
  • Locking
  • Swelling
  • Functional limitations

may warrant evaluation.

Is Knee Popping Normal?

Many individuals experience occasional popping sensations without symptoms.

Popping alone does not necessarily indicate injury.

When popping is accompanied by pain or dysfunction, further evaluation may be appropriate.

Why Does My Knee Feel Like It Wants To Give Out?

Instability may occur for several reasons, including:

  • Ligament injuries
  • Muscle weakness
  • Pain-related compensation
  • Joint dysfunction

Patients experiencing instability should consider a comprehensive evaluation.

Why Is My Knee Swollen?

Swelling may develop from:

  • Injury
  • Arthritis
  • Inflammation
  • Joint irritation
  • Overuse

The cause depends on the underlying diagnosis.

What Does It Mean If My Knee Locks?

Locking refers to difficulty fully bending or straightening the knee.

Some patients describe a sensation that the knee becomes temporarily stuck.

This symptom should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

How Do I Know If I Tore My Meniscus?

Common symptoms may include:

  • Joint line pain
  • Swelling
  • Clicking
  • Catching
  • Locking

However, symptoms alone cannot confirm a diagnosis.

A comprehensive evaluation is often necessary.

How Do I Know If I Tore My ACL?

ACL injuries often occur during:

  • Pivoting
  • Cutting
  • Jumping
  • Sports participation

Common symptoms may include:

  • A popping sensation
  • Swelling
  • Instability
  • Difficulty returning to sports

Imaging and examination findings help establish the diagnosis.

Can Arthritis Cause Knee Pain?

Yes.

Arthritis is one of the most common causes of knee pain, particularly in adults.

However, not all knee pain is caused by arthritis.

Does Arthritis Always Mean I Need Knee Replacement?

No.

Many patients successfully manage arthritis without surgery.

Treatment recommendations should be individualized based on symptoms, goals, and overall health.

Can I Exercise With Knee Arthritis?

Many individuals remain active despite arthritis.

Exercise recommendations should be individualized and based on symptoms, function, and activity goals.

Is Running Bad For My Knees?

This is one of the most common questions in sports medicine.

The relationship between running and knee health is more complex than many people realize.

Activity recommendations should always be individualized.

Can Pickleball Cause Knee Pain?

Yes.

Pickleball involves:

  • Pivoting
  • Cutting
  • Quick directional changes

which may stress the knee joint and surrounding structures.

However, pickleball remains an excellent form of exercise for many individuals.

Can Golf Cause Knee Pain?

Yes.

Walking, rotational forces, and repetitive movement patterns may contribute to symptoms in some golfers.

Should I Use Ice Or Heat?

The answer depends on:

  • The diagnosis
  • Timing of symptoms
  • Activity level
  • Individual response

Treatment recommendations should be tailored to the specific condition.

Do I Need An MRI?

Not always.

Many knee conditions can be evaluated effectively through:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • X-rays

MRI studies may be appropriate in selected situations.

What Is The Difference Between An X-Ray And MRI?

X-Ray

Primarily evaluates bone and joint structure.

MRI

Provides additional information regarding:

  • Meniscus
  • Ligaments
  • Cartilage
  • Tendons
  • Soft tissues

Both studies may play important roles in the diagnostic process.

Does Every Meniscus Tear Require Surgery?

No.

Many patients with meniscus tears explore non-surgical treatment options depending on symptoms and goals.

Does Every ACL Tear Require Surgery?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Activity level
  • Functional goals
  • Knee stability
  • Associated injuries

Every patient deserves an individualized discussion.

When Should I See A Specialist For Knee Pain?

You should consider evaluation if:

  • Pain persists.
  • Walking becomes difficult.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Swelling develops.
  • Instability occurs.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the diagnosis is often the first step toward understanding your options.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Knee pain is common.

The challenge is determining why the knee hurts.

Many different structures can create similar symptoms, which is why accurate diagnosis is so important.

The goal is not simply to treat pain.

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and help patients return to the activities that matter most to them.

Whether that means golf, pickleball, running, hiking, exercise, work, travel, or simply walking comfortably, every treatment plan should begin with understanding the individual patient.
