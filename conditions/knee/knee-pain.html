Knee Pain

Understanding Knee Pain: Causes, Symptoms, and Treatment Options

Knee pain is one of the most common reasons people seek medical care.

Whether you're an athlete trying to stay active, a parent chasing after your kids, a golfer walking 18 holes, or simply someone who wants to move without discomfort, knee pain can quickly interfere with the activities you enjoy most.

The knee is one of the hardest-working joints in the body. It absorbs force with every step, squat, jump, pivot, and change of direction. Because of this, many different conditions can contribute to knee pain.

Understanding the cause of your symptoms is often the first step toward identifying the most appropriate treatment options.

What Causes Knee Pain?

Knee pain is not a diagnosis.

It is a symptom that may originate from several different structures within or around the knee.

Common causes include:

Arthritis

Wear-related changes involving the cartilage and joint surfaces.

Meniscus Tears

Injuries involving the shock-absorbing cartilage inside the knee.

Ligament Injuries

Conditions involving structures such as the ACL, PCL, MCL, or LCL.

Tendon Conditions

Irritation or injury involving the tendons surrounding the knee.

Overuse Injuries

Repetitive stress from sports, exercise, or occupational activities.

Patellofemoral Conditions

Problems involving the kneecap and surrounding structures.

Biomechanical Factors

Movement patterns, muscle imbalances, and joint mechanics may contribute to symptoms.

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine the source of pain.

Common Symptoms Of Knee Pain

Symptoms vary depending on the underlying condition.

Patients may experience:

  • Pain with walking
  • Pain while climbing stairs
  • Swelling
  • Stiffness
  • Clicking or popping sensations
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Instability
  • Difficulty exercising
  • Difficulty squatting
  • Difficulty kneeling

The location and severity of symptoms often provide important clues regarding the underlying diagnosis.

Where Is Your Knee Pain Located?

The location of pain can sometimes help guide evaluation.

Pain On The Inside Of The Knee

May be associated with structures located along the inner portion of the joint.

Pain On The Outside Of The Knee

May involve structures along the outer aspect of the knee.

Pain Behind The Kneecap

Often associated with patellofemoral conditions.

Pain In The Back Of The Knee

May involve several different anatomical structures.

Location alone does not establish a diagnosis, but it may help guide further evaluation.

Knee Pain In Athletes

Athletes place unique demands on the knee joint.

Sports commonly associated with knee injuries include:

  • Running
  • Basketball
  • Football
  • Soccer
  • Tennis
  • Pickleball
  • Golf
  • CrossFit
  • Weightlifting

Athletic knee pain may result from acute injuries, repetitive stress, or a combination of both.

Knee Pain And Arthritis

Many patients assume that all knee pain is caused by arthritis.

While arthritis is common, it is only one of many potential causes.

Likewise, some patients have significant arthritis visible on imaging and relatively mild symptoms.

Others experience substantial pain despite modest imaging findings.

This is why physicians evaluate the entire patient rather than relying solely on imaging studies.

How Is Knee Pain Diagnosed?

A comprehensive evaluation typically includes:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Functional assessment
  • Movement evaluation
  • Review of symptoms

Diagnostic studies may include:

  • X-rays
  • MRI
  • Ultrasound
  • Additional imaging when appropriate

The goal is to identify the source of symptoms and develop an individualized treatment strategy.

Why Accurate Diagnosis Matters

The knee contains many structures capable of producing pain, including:

  • Cartilage
  • Meniscus
  • Ligaments
  • Tendons
  • Bone
  • Joint lining
  • Muscles

Because multiple conditions may create similar symptoms, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step in the treatment process.

The diagnosis guides the treatment plan.

Treatment Considerations

Treatment recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Functional limitations
  • Imaging findings
  • Activity goals

Potential treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Weight management strategies
  • Bracing
  • Interventional procedures
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

No single treatment is appropriate for every patient.

Individualized care is essential.

When Should You Seek Evaluation?

You may benefit from evaluation if:

  • Knee pain persists.
  • Walking becomes difficult.
  • Swelling develops.
  • Sports participation is limited.
  • Symptoms interfere with work or daily activities.
  • Previous treatment has not provided sufficient improvement.

Understanding the cause of symptoms is often the first step toward improvement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common cause of knee pain?

Several conditions may contribute to knee pain, including arthritis, meniscus injuries, ligament injuries, tendon conditions, and overuse-related problems.

Do I need an MRI for knee pain?

Not always.

Imaging recommendations depend on symptoms, physical examination findings, and the suspected diagnosis.

Can knee arthritis be present without severe pain?

Yes.

Imaging findings do not always correlate perfectly with symptoms.

Why does my knee hurt when climbing stairs?

Several knee conditions may contribute to pain during stair climbing, including patellofemoral conditions, arthritis, and other joint-related issues.

Does knee pain always require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.

Should I bring prior imaging to my consultation?

Yes.

X-rays, MRI studies, and previous treatment records can provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
