Running Injuries

Understanding Common Running Injuries: Staying Active and Staying in the Game

Running is one of the most accessible and effective forms of exercise.

Whether you're training for a marathon, preparing for a 5K, maintaining fitness, or simply enjoying an active lifestyle, running places repetitive demands on the musculoskeletal system.

Every step creates forces that travel through:

  • The feet
  • Ankles
  • Achilles tendons
  • Knees
  • Hips
  • Spine

Over time, these repetitive forces may contribute to injury, discomfort, or performance limitations.

Understanding common running injuries can help runners recognize symptoms early and make informed decisions regarding evaluation and treatment.

Why Do Running Injuries Occur?

Running injuries often develop because of multiple contributing factors.

Training Volume

Rapid increases in mileage or intensity may increase stress on tissues.

Recovery

Insufficient recovery between workouts may contribute to overuse symptoms.

Strength And Mobility

Muscle imbalances and mobility limitations may affect running mechanics.

Previous Injuries

Past injuries may influence movement patterns and symptom development.

Running Mechanics

Individual biomechanics may contribute to stress on specific structures.

Often, several factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Running Injuries

Plantar Fasciitis

One of the most common causes of heel pain in runners.

Symptoms may include:

  • Heel pain
  • Pain with the first steps in the morning
  • Pain after running
  • Activity-related discomfort

Learn More → Plantar Fasciitis

Achilles Tendinopathy

The Achilles tendon absorbs substantial force during running.

Common symptoms include:

  • Achilles pain
  • Morning stiffness
  • Tightness
  • Pain during or after activity

Learn More → Achilles Tendinopathy

Knee Pain

Knee pain is among the most common complaints in runners.

Symptoms may include:

  • Pain during running
  • Pain after running
  • Difficulty with hills
  • Activity limitations

Learn More → Knee Pain

Patellofemoral Pain

Pain involving the front of the knee may become noticeable during:

  • Running
  • Stairs
  • Squatting
  • Hills

This is commonly referred to as "runner's knee."

Hip Pain

The hips play an important role in force generation and stability.

Runners may experience:

  • Groin pain
  • Lateral hip pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced performance

Learn More → Hip Pain

IT Band Syndrome

Iliotibial Band (IT Band) irritation commonly affects runners.

Symptoms often involve:

  • Pain on the outside of the knee
  • Activity-related discomfort
  • Symptoms during longer runs

Shin Pain

Pain involving the lower leg may occur because of:

  • Training changes
  • Overuse
  • Repetitive loading

The underlying cause varies depending on the individual.

Low Back Pain

Running places repetitive demands on the spine and surrounding musculature.

Runners may experience:

  • Back stiffness
  • Activity-related discomfort
  • Reduced mobility

Learn More → Low Back Pain

Why Does My Knee Hurt When I Run?

This is one of the most common questions runners ask.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Patellofemoral pain
  • Tendon-related conditions
  • Training changes
  • Biomechanical factors
  • Joint-related conditions

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the source of symptoms.

Why Does My Heel Hurt After Running?

Heel pain is frequently associated with:

  • Plantar fasciitis
  • Achilles-related conditions
  • Foot and ankle disorders

The location and timing of symptoms often provide important diagnostic clues.

Why Does My Achilles Hurt In The Morning?

Morning stiffness is a common complaint among runners experiencing Achilles-related symptoms.

Many runners notice:

  • Tightness when getting out of bed
  • Pain during the first few steps
  • Improvement after movement

A thorough evaluation can help clarify the diagnosis.

Why Does My Hip Hurt During Long Runs?

The hip absorbs substantial forces during running.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Hip joint conditions
  • Tendon-related disorders
  • Muscle imbalances
  • Mobility limitations

Understanding the source of symptoms is essential for treatment planning.

Sports Medicine Evaluation For Runners

A running-focused evaluation may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, training history, mileage, and goals.

Physical Examination

Assessment of strength, flexibility, mobility, and function.

Running-Related Assessment

Evaluation of factors that may influence running performance and injury risk.

Imaging Review

X-rays, MRI studies, ultrasound findings, or other imaging when appropriate.

Treatment Discussion

Review of available options and individualized recommendations.

Returning To Running

One of the most common questions runners ask is:

"When can I run again?"

Return-to-running recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom severity
  • Training goals
  • Functional status
  • Individual recovery progress

Every runner is different.

Recommendations should always be individualized.

Staying Active While Managing Injury

Many runners worry that injury automatically means complete rest.

In many situations, treatment discussions focus on:

  • Understanding the diagnosis
  • Managing training volume
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Activity modification
  • Return-to-running progression

The objective is to support long-term participation whenever possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common running injury?

Plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendinopathy, and knee pain are among the most common complaints seen in runners.

Why does my knee hurt when I run?

Several conditions may contribute, including patellofemoral pain, tendon disorders, and biomechanical factors.

Can running cause Achilles pain?

Yes.

The Achilles tendon absorbs significant force during running activities.

Why does my heel hurt after running?

Heel pain may be associated with plantar fasciitis, Achilles-related conditions, or other foot and ankle disorders.

Can I continue running while being evaluated?

Activity recommendations should always be individualized based on symptoms, diagnosis, and physician guidance.

Do all running injuries require surgery?

No.

Many runners explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.
