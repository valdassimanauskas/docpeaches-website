Pickleball Injuries

Understanding Common Pickleball Injuries: Staying Active and Staying in the Game

Pickleball has become one of the fastest-growing sports in the United States.

The game combines elements of tennis, badminton, and table tennis while requiring quick movements, rapid changes in direction, repetitive arm motions, and sustained physical activity.

Because pickleball is enjoyed by players of all ages and skill levels, injuries can occur in both new and experienced athletes.

Commonly affected areas include:

  • Shoulder
  • Elbow
  • Wrist
  • Hand
  • Hip
  • Knee
  • Achilles tendon
  • Foot and ankle

Understanding common pickleball injuries can help players recognize symptoms early and make informed decisions regarding evaluation and treatment.

Why Do Pickleball Injuries Occur?

Several factors may contribute to injury development.

Repetitive Motion

Repeated swinging and paddle use may place stress on muscles, tendons, and joints.

Quick Direction Changes

Pickleball requires rapid lateral movement and sudden acceleration.

Overuse

Frequent play without adequate recovery may increase tissue stress.

Limited Mobility

Restrictions in flexibility and movement may affect performance and increase strain on certain structures.

Previous Injuries

Existing orthopedic conditions may become more noticeable during play.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Pickleball Injuries

Tennis Elbow

Despite the name, tennis elbow is one of the most common pickleball-related injuries.

Symptoms may include:

  • Pain on the outside of the elbow
  • Pain with gripping
  • Weakness
  • Difficulty lifting objects

Learn More → Tennis Elbow

Golfer's Elbow

Some players develop symptoms on the inside of the elbow.

Common complaints include:

  • Medial elbow pain
  • Tenderness
  • Grip-related discomfort

Learn More → Golfer's Elbow

Shoulder Pain

The shoulder is heavily involved in serving, overhead shots, and repetitive paddle use.

Players may notice:

  • Pain while serving
  • Weakness
  • Reduced range of motion
  • Difficulty reaching overhead

Learn More → Shoulder Pain

Rotator Cuff Conditions

The rotator cuff plays a critical role in shoulder stability and function.

Symptoms may include:

  • Pain
  • Weakness
  • Night discomfort
  • Activity limitations

Learn More → Rotator Cuff Tear

Wrist Pain

Repetitive paddle use may place stress on wrist structures.

Players may experience:

  • Wrist pain
  • Reduced grip strength
  • Activity-related discomfort

Learn More → Wrist Pain

Knee Pain

Quick starts, stops, and directional changes place significant demands on the knee.

Symptoms may include:

  • Pain during play
  • Swelling
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced mobility

Learn More → Knee Pain

Hip Pain

The hips play a major role in movement, balance, and power generation.

Players may report:

  • Groin pain
  • Hip stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Activity limitations

Learn More → Hip Pain

Achilles Tendinopathy

Repeated acceleration and deceleration may increase stress on the Achilles tendon.

Common symptoms include:

  • Morning stiffness
  • Achilles pain
  • Tightness
  • Activity-related discomfort

Learn More → Achilles Tendinopathy

Plantar Fasciitis

Heel pain is common among active pickleball players.

Symptoms often include:

  • Heel pain
  • Pain with first steps in the morning
  • Activity-related discomfort

Learn More → Plantar Fasciitis

Ankle Sprains

Rapid lateral movement may increase the risk of ankle injuries.

Learn More → Ankle Sprain

Why Does My Elbow Hurt After Pickleball?

Elbow pain is one of the most common reasons players seek evaluation.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Tennis elbow
  • Golfer's elbow
  • Tendon-related conditions
  • Overuse injuries

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the source of symptoms.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt When I Play Pickleball?

The repetitive overhead and rotational demands of the sport may place stress on shoulder structures.

Potential contributors include:

  • Rotator cuff conditions
  • Tendon disorders
  • Mobility limitations
  • Overuse injuries

Accurate diagnosis is important because several conditions may create similar symptoms.

Why Do My Knees Hurt After Playing?

Pickleball involves:

  • Quick stops
  • Rapid starts
  • Lateral movement
  • Repetitive loading

These demands may contribute to knee symptoms in some athletes.

Why Does My Achilles Hurt After Playing?

The Achilles tendon absorbs substantial force during pickleball activities.

Symptoms may become noticeable:

  • During play
  • After activity
  • The following morning

Understanding the diagnosis is important for treatment planning.

Sports Medicine Evaluation For Pickleball Players

A pickleball-focused evaluation may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, playing frequency, and goals.

Physical Examination

Assessment of strength, flexibility, mobility, and function.

Movement Assessment

Evaluation of factors relevant to pickleball participation.

Imaging Review

X-rays, MRI studies, ultrasound findings, or other imaging when appropriate.

Treatment Discussion

Review of available options and individualized recommendations.

Staying Active While Managing Injury

Many pickleball players worry that pain automatically means they must stop playing.

In many situations, treatment discussions focus on:

  • Understanding the diagnosis
  • Activity modification
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Recovery strategies
  • Return-to-play progression

Every athlete is different.

Recommendations should always be individualized.

Return To Pickleball

One of the most common questions players ask is:

"When can I get back on the court?"

Return-to-play recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Injury severity
  • Functional status
  • Individual goals
  • Sport-specific demands

The objective is to support a safe and appropriate return to participation whenever possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common pickleball injury?

Tennis elbow, shoulder pain, knee pain, and Achilles-related symptoms are among the most common complaints.

Can pickleball cause shoulder pain?

Yes.

Serving and repetitive paddle use place substantial demands on the shoulder.

Why does my elbow hurt after pickleball?

Several conditions may contribute, including tennis elbow, golfer's elbow, and tendon-related disorders.

Can pickleball cause knee pain?

Yes.

The sport involves frequent directional changes and repetitive loading.

Do all pickleball injuries require surgery?

No.

Many athletes explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.

Can I continue playing while being evaluated?

Activity recommendations should always be individualized based on symptoms, diagnosis, and physician guidance.
