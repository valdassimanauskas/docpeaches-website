Tennis Injuries

Understanding Common Tennis Injuries: Staying Active and Staying in the Game

Tennis is a sport that combines speed, power, endurance, agility, and repetitive movement.

Whether you play recreationally, compete in leagues, participate in tournaments, or enjoy doubles matches with friends, tennis places substantial demands on the musculoskeletal system.

The repetitive motions involved in serving, hitting groundstrokes, volleying, and changing direction can contribute to injury over time.

Commonly affected areas include:

  • Shoulder
  • Elbow
  • Wrist
  • Hand
  • Hip
  • Knee
  • Foot and ankle

Understanding common tennis injuries can help players recognize symptoms early and make informed decisions regarding evaluation and treatment.

Why Do Tennis Injuries Occur?

Several factors may contribute to injury development.

Repetitive Motion

Thousands of swings over time place stress on muscles, tendons, joints, and ligaments.

Overuse

Frequent play without adequate recovery may increase cumulative tissue stress.

Technique

Movement patterns and stroke mechanics may influence loading throughout the body.

Conditioning

Strength, mobility, flexibility, and balance may affect performance and injury risk.

Previous Injuries

Prior orthopedic conditions may become more noticeable during tennis activities.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Tennis Injuries

Tennis Elbow

One of the most well-known tennis-related injuries.

Symptoms may include:

  • Pain on the outside of the elbow
  • Pain with gripping
  • Difficulty lifting objects
  • Weakness

Despite its name, tennis elbow affects many non-tennis players as well.

Learn More → Tennis Elbow

Golfer's Elbow

Pain on the inside of the elbow may develop because of repetitive gripping and swinging motions.

Symptoms may include:

  • Medial elbow pain
  • Tenderness
  • Grip-related discomfort

Learn More → Golfer's Elbow

Shoulder Pain

Serving and overhead movements place substantial demands on the shoulder.

Players may experience:

  • Pain during serves
  • Weakness
  • Reduced performance
  • Difficulty reaching overhead

Learn More → Shoulder Pain

Rotator Cuff Conditions

The rotator cuff helps stabilize the shoulder during repetitive athletic activity.

Symptoms may include:

  • Shoulder pain
  • Weakness
  • Night discomfort
  • Activity limitations

Learn More → Rotator Cuff Tear

Wrist Pain

Tennis requires repetitive wrist motion and force transmission through the upper extremity.

Players may experience:

  • Wrist pain
  • Reduced grip strength
  • Activity-related symptoms
  • Difficulty during strokes

Learn More → Wrist Pain

Hand Pain

Repetitive gripping may contribute to symptoms involving the hand and fingers.

Learn More → Hand Pain

Knee Pain

Quick changes in direction and repetitive loading may contribute to knee symptoms.

Players may report:

  • Pain while moving laterally
  • Stiffness
  • Swelling
  • Activity limitations

Learn More → Knee Pain

Hip Pain

The hips play an important role in generating power and rotational force.

Symptoms may include:

  • Groin pain
  • Hip stiffness
  • Reduced mobility
  • Performance limitations

Learn More → Hip Pain

Achilles Tendinopathy

Repeated acceleration and deceleration may increase demands on the Achilles tendon.

Symptoms often include:

  • Morning stiffness
  • Activity-related discomfort
  • Tightness

Learn More → Achilles Tendinopathy

Ankle Sprains

Tennis requires rapid lateral movement, making ankle injuries relatively common.

Learn More → Ankle Sprain

Why Does My Elbow Hurt When I Play Tennis?

Elbow pain is one of the most common reasons tennis players seek medical evaluation.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Tennis elbow
  • Golfer's elbow
  • Tendon-related conditions
  • Overuse injuries

A comprehensive evaluation can help determine the source of symptoms.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt When I Serve?

Serving places significant forces through the shoulder.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Rotator cuff conditions
  • Tendon disorders
  • Mobility limitations
  • Overuse injuries

The location and timing of symptoms often provide important diagnostic information.

Why Does My Wrist Hurt During Groundstrokes?

Repetitive force transmission through the wrist may contribute to symptoms.

Potential causes vary depending on:

  • Stroke mechanics
  • Training volume
  • Previous injuries
  • Individual anatomy

Accurate diagnosis is essential.

Sports Medicine Evaluation For Tennis Players

A tennis-focused evaluation may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, playing frequency, and activity goals.

Physical Examination

Assessment of mobility, strength, flexibility, and function.

Movement Assessment

Evaluation of factors relevant to tennis participation.

Imaging Review

X-rays, MRI studies, ultrasound findings, or other imaging when appropriate.

Treatment Discussion

Review of available options and individualized recommendations.

Staying Active While Managing Injury

Many tennis players assume that pain automatically means they must stop playing.

In many situations, treatment discussions focus on:

  • Understanding the diagnosis
  • Activity modification
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Recovery strategies
  • Return-to-play progression

Every athlete is different.

Recommendations should always be individualized.

Return To Tennis

One of the most common questions players ask is:

"When can I get back on the court?"

Return-to-sport recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Injury severity
  • Functional status
  • Individual goals
  • Sport-specific demands

The objective is to support a safe and appropriate return to play whenever possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common tennis injury?

Tennis elbow is among the most commonly reported injuries in tennis players.

Can tennis cause shoulder pain?

Yes.

Serving and overhead movements place substantial demands on the shoulder.

Why does my wrist hurt when I play tennis?

Several wrist conditions may contribute, including overuse injuries, tendon disorders, and joint-related conditions.

Can tennis cause knee pain?

Yes.

The sport involves frequent stopping, starting, and directional changes that place demands on the knees.

Do all tennis injuries require surgery?

No.

Many athletes explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.

Can I continue playing tennis while being evaluated?

Activity recommendations should always be individualized based on symptoms, diagnosis, and physician guidance.
