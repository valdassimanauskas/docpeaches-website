Golf Injuries

Understanding Common Golf Injuries: Staying Active and Staying in the Game

Golf is one of the most popular lifetime sports.

Whether you play a few rounds each month, compete in tournaments, or spend several days a week on the course, golf places unique physical demands on the body.

The modern golf swing generates significant force through:

  • The spine
  • Hips
  • Shoulders
  • Elbows
  • Wrists
  • Knees

As a result, golfers may develop a variety of musculoskeletal injuries that can affect both performance and quality of life.

Understanding common golf injuries is often the first step toward identifying appropriate treatment options and returning to the game.

Why Do Golf Injuries Occur?

Golf injuries may develop for several reasons.

Repetitive Swing Mechanics

The golf swing involves repetitive rotational movement that places stress on multiple joints and soft tissues.

Overuse

Frequent practice and play may increase cumulative stress on the body.

Limited Mobility

Restrictions in flexibility or mobility may alter swing mechanics and increase demands on certain structures.

Strength Deficits

Muscular imbalances may contribute to movement inefficiencies.

Previous Injuries

Prior orthopedic conditions may become more noticeable during golf activities.

Often, multiple factors contribute simultaneously.

Common Golf Injuries

Low Back Pain

Low back pain is one of the most common complaints among golfers.

The golf swing requires repeated rotation, extension, and force generation through the lumbar spine.

Golfers may notice:

  • Pain during the swing
  • Pain after a round
  • Morning stiffness
  • Reduced mobility

Learn More → Low Back Pain

Hip Pain

The hips play a critical role in power generation and rotational movement.

Golfers experiencing hip symptoms may report:

  • Groin pain
  • Stiffness
  • Reduced rotation
  • Difficulty generating power

Learn More → Hip Pain

Shoulder Pain

The shoulders are subjected to substantial forces during the golf swing.

Common complaints include:

  • Pain during the backswing
  • Pain during follow-through
  • Weakness
  • Difficulty reaching overhead

Learn More → Shoulder Pain

Rotator Cuff Conditions

The rotator cuff helps stabilize the shoulder during golf activities.

Symptoms may include:

  • Shoulder pain
  • Weakness
  • Night discomfort
  • Activity limitations

Learn More → Rotator Cuff Tear

Golfer's Elbow

Despite the name, golfer's elbow can affect athletes in many sports.

In golfers, symptoms often involve:

  • Pain on the inside of the elbow
  • Pain with gripping
  • Pain during the swing

Learn More → Golfer's Elbow

Tennis Elbow

Tennis elbow is also common among golfers.

Symptoms may include:

  • Pain on the outside of the elbow
  • Weakness
  • Difficulty gripping clubs

Learn More → Tennis Elbow

Wrist And Hand Injuries

Golf requires repetitive gripping and impact forces through the hands and wrists.

Common symptoms include:

  • Wrist pain
  • Hand pain
  • Reduced grip strength
  • Activity-related discomfort

Learn More → Wrist Pain Hand Pain

Knee Pain

The golf swing places rotational forces through the knees.

Golfers may experience:

  • Activity-related knee pain
  • Stiffness
  • Swelling
  • Reduced performance

Learn More → Knee Pain

Foot And Ankle Conditions

The lower extremities provide stability throughout the swing.

Golfers may develop:

  • Plantar fasciitis
  • Achilles tendinopathy
  • Ankle pain

Learn More → Plantar Fasciitis Achilles Tendinopathy

Why Does My Back Hurt After Golf?

This is one of the most common questions golfers ask.

Potential contributors may include:

  • Swing mechanics
  • Mobility limitations
  • Strength deficits
  • Previous injuries
  • Spine-related conditions

A comprehensive evaluation can help identify contributing factors.

Why Does My Elbow Hurt When I Golf?

The repetitive forces involved in gripping and swinging can place stress on elbow tendons and surrounding structures.

Common causes include:

  • Golfer's elbow
  • Tennis elbow
  • Tendon-related conditions

An accurate diagnosis is important because treatment recommendations vary depending on the underlying condition.

Why Does My Hip Feel Tight During My Swing?

Hip mobility plays an important role in golf performance.

Limitations in hip motion may affect:

  • Rotation
  • Power generation
  • Swing mechanics
  • Overall comfort

Hip stiffness is a common reason golfers seek evaluation.

Why Does My Shoulder Hurt During The Golf Swing?

Shoulder symptoms may develop because of:

  • Rotational demands
  • Repetitive loading
  • Overuse
  • Previous injuries
  • Rotator cuff-related conditions

The location and timing of symptoms often provide important diagnostic clues.

Sports Medicine Evaluation For Golfers

A golf-focused evaluation may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, playing frequency, and goals.

Physical Examination

Assessment of mobility, strength, flexibility, and function.

Movement Assessment

Evaluation of movement patterns relevant to golf activities.

Imaging Review

X-rays, MRI studies, ultrasound, or other imaging when appropriate.

Treatment Discussion

Review of available options and individualized recommendations.

Staying Active While Managing Injury

Many golfers worry that injury automatically means they must stop playing.

In reality, treatment discussions often focus on:

  • Understanding the diagnosis
  • Identifying contributing factors
  • Activity modifications
  • Rehabilitation strategies
  • Return-to-play planning

Every golfer is different.

Recommendations should be individualized.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common golf injury?

Low back pain is one of the most commonly reported complaints among golfers.

Can golf cause elbow pain?

Yes.

Both golfer's elbow and tennis elbow are commonly seen in golfers.

Why does my hip hurt when I golf?

The golf swing places substantial rotational demands on the hips.

Several hip conditions may contribute to symptoms.

Can golf cause shoulder pain?

Yes.

The shoulder experiences significant forces throughout the golf swing.

Do all golf injuries require surgery?

No.

Many golfers explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.

Can I continue playing golf while being evaluated?

Activity recommendations should always be individualized based on symptoms, diagnosis, and physician guidance.
