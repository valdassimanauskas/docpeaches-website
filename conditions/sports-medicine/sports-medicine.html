Sports Medicine

Helping Active People Stay In The Game

Sports medicine focuses on helping individuals stay active, recover from injuries, optimize performance, and return to the activities they enjoy.

At Dr. Peaches, sports medicine is not limited to professional athletes.

Sports medicine is for anyone who wants to remain active, including:

  • Golfers
  • Pickleball players
  • Runners
  • Tennis players
  • Cyclists
  • Fitness enthusiasts
  • Weekend warriors
  • Active adults

Whether you're training for a race, competing in a tournament, exercising for your health, or simply trying to remain active as you age, musculoskeletal injuries can affect your ability to participate in the activities that matter most.

The goal of sports medicine is to identify the source of symptoms, understand contributing factors, and develop an individualized treatment strategy that supports your goals.

What Is Sports Medicine?

Sports medicine is a specialized area of orthopedic and musculoskeletal care focused on:

  • Injury evaluation
  • Injury prevention
  • Activity optimization
  • Rehabilitation
  • Return-to-sport planning
  • Non-surgical treatment options
  • Performance-focused care

Sports medicine physicians evaluate injuries involving:

  • Muscles
  • Tendons
  • Ligaments
  • Joints
  • Cartilage
  • Nerves
  • Bone

The focus is not simply treating pain.

The focus is helping individuals remain active and engaged in their chosen activities.

Who Benefits From Sports Medicine?

Many people assume sports medicine is only for competitive athletes.

In reality, sports medicine is appropriate for:

Active Adults

Individuals who exercise regularly and want to maintain an active lifestyle.

Recreational Athletes

Weekend warriors and recreational competitors.

Competitive Athletes

High school, collegiate, and adult athletes.

Fitness Enthusiasts

Individuals involved in strength training, fitness programs, and exercise routines.

Aging Athletes

Patients who want to remain active as they get older.

Returning Athletes

Individuals seeking guidance after injury or time away from activity.

Sports We Commonly Evaluate

Golf

Rotational forces place significant demands on the shoulders, spine, hips, elbows, and knees.

Pickleball

One of the fastest-growing sports in America and a common source of overuse and activity-related injuries.

Running

Running places repetitive stress on the lower extremities and spine.

Tennis

Tennis requires repetitive upper-extremity loading and rotational movement.

Weightlifting

Strength training may contribute to tendon, joint, and soft tissue symptoms.

Fitness & Exercise

Activity-related injuries can occur across all levels of fitness participation.

The Importance Of An Accurate Diagnosis

Many athletes assume they know the source of their pain.

However, similar symptoms can originate from very different structures.

For example:

  • Hip pain may originate from the hip, low back, or SI joint.
  • Shoulder pain may involve the rotator cuff, bursa, labrum, or surrounding muscles.
  • Knee pain may involve cartilage, tendons, ligaments, or joint surfaces.

Accurate diagnosis is often the most important step in developing an effective treatment strategy.

Sports Medicine And Non-Surgical Care

Many sports medicine patients explore non-surgical treatment options.

Depending on the diagnosis and individual circumstances, treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modification
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Image-guided procedures
  • Orthobiologic procedures
  • Shockwave therapy
  • Return-to-sport planning

Every treatment plan should be individualized.

Return To Sport

One of the most common questions patients ask is:

"When can I get back to playing?"

Return-to-sport planning is an important part of sports medicine care.

Recommendations depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Injury severity
  • Functional status
  • Sport-specific demands
  • Individual goals

The objective is to support a safe and appropriate return to activity whenever possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is sports medicine?

Sports medicine focuses on the evaluation, treatment, rehabilitation, and prevention of musculoskeletal injuries in active individuals.

Do I need to be an athlete?

No.

Sports medicine is appropriate for anyone who wants to remain active.

What sports injuries are most common?

Common injuries include tendon disorders, overuse injuries, joint pain, ligament injuries, and muscle strains.

Can sports medicine help active adults?

Absolutely.

Many sports medicine patients are active adults rather than competitive athletes.

Do all sports injuries require surgery?

No.

Many patients explore non-surgical treatment options depending on their diagnosis and goals.
