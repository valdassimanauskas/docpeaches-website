Bone Marrow Concentrate (BMC)

Understanding Bone Marrow Concentrate (BMC)

Bone Marrow Concentrate (BMC) is a biologic product derived from a patient's own bone marrow.

In orthopedic and sports medicine settings, BMC is commonly discussed as part of the broader field of regenerative medicine and orthobiologics.

Because patients often hear terms such as:

  • Bone Marrow Aspirate
  • Bone Marrow Concentrate
  • Stem Cells
  • Cellular Therapy
  • Orthobiologics

Understanding the terminology can help clarify treatment discussions and support informed decision-making.

What Is Bone Marrow Concentrate?

Bone Marrow Concentrate is a biologic preparation created from bone marrow obtained from the patient.

After collection, specialized processing techniques are used to concentrate certain cellular and biologic components found within bone marrow.

The final preparation differs from unprocessed bone marrow and contains a variety of naturally occurring cell populations and biologic substances.

What Is Bone Marrow?

Bone marrow is a soft tissue found inside certain bones.

Bone marrow contains numerous biologic components, including:

  • Blood-forming cells
  • Progenitor cells
  • Immune cells
  • Growth factors
  • Other naturally occurring cellular elements

Researchers continue to study the role of these components in regenerative medicine and orthobiologic applications.

How Is BMC Obtained?

Although protocols vary, the general process may include:

  • Medical evaluation
  • Procedure planning
  • Bone marrow collection
  • Processing and concentration
  • Preparation for procedural use

Specific techniques vary depending on physician preference, equipment, and patient factors.

What Is The Difference Between Bone Marrow Aspirate And Bone Marrow Concentrate?

Patients frequently hear both terms.

Bone Marrow Aspirate (BMA)

Refers to bone marrow collected directly from the body.

Bone Marrow Concentrate (BMC)

Refers to a processed preparation created after concentrating components from the collected marrow.

The terms are related but not interchangeable.

BMC And Regenerative Medicine

Bone Marrow Concentrate is commonly discussed within regenerative medicine and orthobiologics.

Other biologic therapies may include:

  • PRP
  • PRF
  • Cellular therapies
  • Orthobiologic procedures

Each modality has unique characteristics and should be discussed within the context of an individual patient's diagnosis and goals.

Why Is Evaluation Important?

The most important step is not choosing a procedure.

The most important step is understanding:

  • The diagnosis
  • The source of symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Imaging findings
  • Previous treatments
  • Patient goals

Treatment planning should always begin with an accurate diagnosis.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Bone Marrow Concentrate?

A biologic preparation created from a patient's own bone marrow using specialized processing techniques.

Is BMC the same as bone marrow aspirate?

No.

Bone marrow aspirate refers to collected marrow, while BMC refers to a concentrated preparation created after processing.

Is BMC considered an orthobiologic?

Yes.

BMC is commonly discussed within the field of orthobiologics.

Is BMC part of regenerative medicine?

BMC is frequently discussed within regenerative medicine and orthopedic biologic procedures.

Does everyone qualify for BMC procedures?

Not necessarily.

Treatment discussions should always be individualized.
