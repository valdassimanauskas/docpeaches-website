Orthobiologics

Understanding Orthobiologics

Orthobiologics are biologic substances commonly used in orthopedic and sports medicine settings.

The term "orthobiologic" refers to biologic materials that are derived from the body or biologic sources and are utilized in musculoskeletal medicine.

As regenerative medicine continues to evolve, many patients encounter terms such as:

  • Orthobiologics
  • Regenerative Medicine
  • PRP
  • PRF
  • Bone Marrow Concentrate
  • Cellular Therapies

Understanding what orthobiologics are—and how they fit into modern orthopedic care—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their treatment options.

What Are Orthobiologics?

Orthobiologics are biologic products utilized in orthopedic and musculoskeletal medicine.

These products may be derived from:

  • Blood
  • Bone marrow
  • Adipose tissue
  • Other biologic sources

Orthobiologics are commonly discussed as part of the broader field of regenerative medicine.

Why Are Orthobiologics Used In Orthopedic Medicine?

Orthopedic specialists frequently evaluate conditions involving:

  • Joints
  • Tendons
  • Ligaments
  • Muscles
  • Cartilage
  • Bone

Researchers continue to study biologic products and their potential roles within musculoskeletal care.

As scientific understanding evolves, orthobiologics have become an increasingly important area of interest within orthopedic and sports medicine research.

Common Types Of Orthobiologics

Several biologic products are commonly discussed in orthopedic medicine.

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)

PRP is created from a patient's own blood and contains concentrated platelets and naturally occurring growth factors.

Learn More → Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)

Platelet-Rich Fibrin (PRF)

PRF is a fibrin-based biologic preparation derived from a patient's own blood.

Learn More → Platelet-Rich Fibrin (PRF)

Bone Marrow Concentrate (BMC)

Bone Marrow Concentrate is created from bone marrow obtained from the patient and contains a variety of naturally occurring cellular and biologic components.

Cellular Therapies

Cell-based therapies continue to be an active area of research and scientific investigation.

Exosome Research

Exosomes are microscopic extracellular vesicles involved in cellular communication and continue to be studied in regenerative medicine research.

Orthobiologics And Regenerative Medicine

Patients often wonder whether orthobiologics and regenerative medicine are the same thing.

The terms are closely related but not identical.

Regenerative Medicine

A broad field focused on understanding biological repair, maintenance, and regenerative processes.

Orthobiologics

A category of biologic products commonly utilized within orthopedic and sports medicine settings.

Orthobiologics represent one component of regenerative medicine.

How Are Orthobiologics Evaluated?

Before discussing any biologic procedure, physicians typically perform a comprehensive evaluation.

This may include:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, activity levels, and previous treatments.

Physical Examination

Assessment of movement, strength, flexibility, and function.

Imaging Review

Evaluation of X-rays, MRI studies, ultrasound findings, or other imaging when appropriate.

Treatment Discussion

Review of available options and individualized recommendations.

The diagnosis should always guide treatment planning.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

One of the most common misconceptions is that a specific procedure should be selected before understanding the diagnosis.

At Dr. Peaches, the process begins with understanding:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Imaging findings
  • Previous treatments
  • Individual goals

Only after a thorough evaluation can treatment options be discussed appropriately.

Orthobiologics In Sports Medicine

Orthobiologics are frequently discussed in sports medicine settings because athletes and active individuals often seek information regarding non-surgical treatment options.

Common reasons patients inquire about orthobiologics include:

  • Sports injuries
  • Joint pain
  • Tendon disorders
  • Activity-related symptoms
  • Performance goals
  • Recovery planning

Every patient is unique, and treatment discussions should always be individualized.
