Joint Injections

Understanding Joint Injections

Joint injections are among the most commonly performed image-guided procedures in orthopedic and sports medicine practice.

These procedures may be discussed as part of a comprehensive treatment plan for selected patients with joint-related pain, stiffness, or functional limitations.

Joint injections can be performed in many areas of the body and are often used alongside physical therapy, exercise programs, activity modifications, and other treatment strategies.

Understanding what a joint injection is—and when it may be considered—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is A Joint Injection?

A joint injection is a procedure in which medication or biologic material is placed into a joint using imaging guidance.

The specific substance utilized depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Clinical findings
  • Physician recommendations
  • Patient goals
  • Individual circumstances

Joint injections are commonly performed using:

  • Ultrasound guidance
  • Fluoroscopic (X-ray) guidance

These technologies help improve procedural accuracy.

Why Are Joint Injections Performed?

Joint injections may be discussed for several reasons.

Diagnostic Purposes

In some situations, an injection may help physicians better understand whether a specific joint is contributing to symptoms.

Treatment Planning

Joint injections may also be incorporated into broader treatment discussions depending on the diagnosis and individual circumstances.

The role of an injection varies from patient to patient.

Common Joints Evaluated For Injection Procedures

Shoulder Joint

May be discussed in patients with shoulder pain and shoulder-related conditions.

Hip Joint

May be discussed in patients with hip pain and hip-related disorders.

Knee Joint

Frequently evaluated in patients with knee pain and functional limitations.

Ankle Joint

May be considered in selected patients with ankle-related symptoms.

Wrist And Hand Joints

Small joints of the hand and wrist may be evaluated depending on the diagnosis.

Sacroiliac Joint

The SI joint is a specialized joint connecting the spine and pelvis.

Coccyx Region

Selected patients with tailbone pain may undergo evaluation of structures in the coccygeal region.

Why Is Imaging Guidance Important?

Image guidance helps physicians visualize anatomy during the procedure.

Depending on the location being treated, guidance may include:

Ultrasound

Provides real-time visualization of soft tissues and joint structures.

Fluoroscopy

Provides real-time X-ray imaging.

The type of imaging used depends on the joint being evaluated and physician preference.

What Happens During The Procedure?

Although protocols vary, joint injections generally involve:

  • Evaluation and diagnosis
  • Procedure planning
  • Skin preparation
  • Image-guided needle placement
  • Delivery of the selected material
  • Follow-up assessment

Specific details vary depending on the location and procedure.

What Happens After A Joint Injection?

Post-procedure recommendations vary.

Patients may receive guidance regarding:

  • Activity modifications
  • Exercise progression
  • Follow-up care
  • Rehabilitation planning

Recovery recommendations should always be individualized.

The Importance Of Diagnosis

A joint injection is not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any procedure, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide the treatment plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a joint injection?

A joint injection is a procedure that places medication or biologic material into a joint using imaging guidance.

Are all joint injections the same?

No.

The procedure, imaging guidance, and materials used may vary depending on the joint and clinical situation.

Do joint injections require surgery?

No.

Joint injections are minimally invasive procedures.

Why is imaging guidance used?

Imaging guidance helps physicians visualize anatomy and improve procedural accuracy.

Which joints can be injected?

Many joints throughout the body may be evaluated for image-guided injection procedures.
