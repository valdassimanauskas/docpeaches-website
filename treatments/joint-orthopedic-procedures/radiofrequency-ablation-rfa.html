Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

Understanding Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA) is an image-guided procedure that may be discussed as part of a comprehensive treatment plan for selected patients with chronic pain originating from specific nerves.

RFA is commonly utilized in interventional spine and orthopedic medicine and is often considered after a thorough evaluation, imaging review, physical examination, and diagnostic procedures.

Because many structures can contribute to pain, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step before discussing advanced procedures.

Understanding what RFA is—and how it fits into the treatment process—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is Radiofrequency Ablation?

Radiofrequency Ablation is a minimally invasive procedure that uses thermal energy generated by radiofrequency waves to target specific nerves.

The procedure is performed using specialized equipment and imaging guidance.

The goal of RFA is not to treat the underlying diagnosis itself but rather to target selected nerves that may be contributing to a patient's symptoms.

The appropriateness of RFA depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom pattern
  • Imaging findings
  • Physical examination findings
  • Previous treatment response
  • Individual goals

How Does Radiofrequency Ablation Work?

Radiofrequency energy is delivered through a specialized needle placed near a targeted nerve.

The procedure is performed using imaging guidance to assist with accurate placement.

The exact technique varies depending on:

  • The nerve being targeted
  • The anatomical region
  • The patient's diagnosis
  • Physician preference

Conditions Commonly Evaluated For RFA

Radiofrequency ablation may be discussed for selected patients with certain pain conditions.

Facet Joint-Related Neck Pain

Pain originating from structures within the cervical spine.

Facet Joint-Related Mid Back Pain

Pain involving the thoracic spine.

Facet Joint-Related Low Back Pain

Pain involving the lumbar spine.

Knee Pain

Certain patients may undergo evaluation of nerves associated with the knee joint.

Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain

Selected patients may be evaluated depending on the diagnosis and clinical findings.

Not every patient with these conditions is a candidate for RFA.

A comprehensive evaluation is essential.

What Is Genicular Nerve Radiofrequency Ablation?

Genicular nerves are small nerves associated with the knee.

For selected patients with knee pain, physicians may discuss:

Genicular Nerve RFA

This procedure targets specific nerves associated with the knee.

Treatment decisions depend on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptoms
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Individual goals

What Is The Relationship Between Medial Branch Blocks And RFA?

For patients with suspected facet joint-related pain, physicians may discuss:

Medial Branch Blocks

These diagnostic procedures target nerves associated with facet joints.

In selected patients, medial branch blocks may be part of the evaluation process before considering radiofrequency ablation.

Not all patients who undergo medial branch blocks will proceed to RFA.

The decision is individualized.

Why Is Imaging Guidance Important?

Radiofrequency ablation is typically performed using fluoroscopic guidance.

Fluoroscopy

Fluoroscopy provides real-time X-ray imaging that allows physicians to visualize anatomy and guide needle placement.

Image guidance is an important component of modern interventional procedures.

How Is RFA Performed?

Although protocols vary, the general process may include:

  • Medical evaluation
  • Physical examination
  • Imaging review
  • Procedure planning
  • Skin preparation
  • Image-guided needle placement
  • Radiofrequency treatment
  • Recovery and follow-up

Specific procedural details vary depending on the treatment area and patient circumstances.

What Happens After The Procedure?

Post-procedure recommendations vary.

Patients may receive guidance regarding:

  • Activity progression
  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Follow-up care
  • Rehabilitation planning

Recommendations should always be individualized.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

Radiofrequency ablation is a procedure—not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any advanced intervention, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Previous treatments
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide the treatment strategy.

Is RFA Surgery?

No.

Radiofrequency ablation is considered a minimally invasive image-guided procedure and does not involve surgical incisions.
