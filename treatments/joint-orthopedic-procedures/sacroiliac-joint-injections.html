Sacroiliac Joint Injections

Understanding Sacroiliac (SI) Joint Injections

The sacroiliac joint, often called the SI joint, is a common source of lower back, buttock, pelvic, and hip-region pain.

Because symptoms originating from the SI joint can closely resemble other orthopedic and spine conditions, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often one of the most important steps in developing an effective treatment plan.

Sacroiliac joint injections are image-guided procedures that may be discussed as part of a comprehensive evaluation and treatment strategy for selected patients.

Understanding the purpose of these procedures can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is The Sacroiliac Joint?

The sacroiliac joints connect the spine to the pelvis.

Every person has two SI joints:

  • One on the right side
  • One on the left side

These joints help:

  • Transfer forces between the upper and lower body
  • Provide stability
  • Assist with walking and movement
  • Support normal biomechanics

Although the SI joints have relatively limited motion, they play an important role in everyday activities.

What Is A Sacroiliac Joint Injection?

A sacroiliac joint injection is an image-guided procedure that targets the SI joint.

Using imaging guidance, medication is delivered into or around the joint.

The specific purpose of the injection depends on:

  • The diagnosis
  • Symptom pattern
  • Imaging findings
  • Clinical evaluation
  • Individual treatment goals

SI joint injections may be discussed as part of a broader diagnostic and treatment strategy.

Why Are SI Joint Injections Performed?

The lower back, pelvis, hip region, and buttocks contain multiple structures capable of producing pain.

Because symptoms often overlap, identifying the source of pain can sometimes be challenging.

Sacroiliac joint injections may be discussed to help physicians better understand whether the SI joint is contributing to a patient's symptoms.

The role of the procedure varies depending on the individual clinical situation.

Conditions Commonly Evaluated

SI joint injections may be discussed in patients experiencing:

Sacroiliac Joint Pain

Pain suspected to originate from the SI joint.

Lower Back Pain

Pain involving the lower portion of the spine and pelvis.

Buttock Pain

Pain located in the gluteal region.

Pelvic Pain

Symptoms involving the posterior pelvis.

Hip Region Pain

Pain patterns that may overlap with hip conditions.

A comprehensive evaluation is necessary to determine whether the SI joint is contributing to symptoms.

Where Is SI Joint Pain Usually Felt?

Patients commonly report symptoms involving:

  • Lower back
  • Buttocks
  • Posterior pelvis
  • Hip region
  • Upper thigh

Because several conditions may create similar symptoms, location alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

How Are Sacroiliac Joint Injections Performed?

Although protocols vary, the general process may include:

  • Medical evaluation
  • Imaging review
  • Procedure planning
  • Skin preparation
  • Imaging-guided needle placement
  • Injection procedure
  • Follow-up assessment

Specific procedural details vary depending on the patient and treatment plan.

Why Is Imaging Guidance Important?

SI joint injections are commonly performed using fluoroscopy.

Fluoroscopic Guidance

Fluoroscopy provides real-time X-ray imaging during the procedure.

This allows the physician to visualize anatomy and guide needle placement.

Image guidance is an important component of modern interventional spine and orthopedic procedures.

SI Joint Pain vs Hip Pain

Patients frequently wonder whether symptoms originate from the hip or SI joint.

The challenge is that both conditions may create similar symptoms.

Pain may occur in:

  • The buttock
  • The pelvis
  • The hip region
  • The upper thigh

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine which structures may be contributing to symptoms.

SI Joint Pain vs Low Back Pain

Similarly, SI joint pain can resemble symptoms arising from structures within the lumbar spine.

Because symptom patterns often overlap, diagnosis generally requires:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Imaging review
  • Functional assessment

The diagnosis should guide treatment decisions.

What Happens After The Procedure?

Post-procedure recommendations vary.

Patients may receive guidance regarding:

  • Activity modifications
  • Exercise progression
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Follow-up appointments

Recommendations should always be individualized.

The Importance Of Diagnosis

An SI joint injection is not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any procedure, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide the treatment strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the sacroiliac joint?

The sacroiliac joint connects the spine and pelvis and helps transfer forces between the upper and lower body.

What is an SI joint injection?

An SI joint injection is an image-guided procedure that targets the sacroiliac joint.

Why are SI joint injections performed?

They may be discussed as part of the evaluation process for selected patients with lower back, buttock, pelvic, or hip-region pain.

Is an SI joint injection surgery?

No.

SI joint injections are minimally invasive image-guided procedures.

Why is fluoroscopy used?

Fluoroscopy provides real-time imaging that helps guide needle placement during the procedure.

Is SI joint pain the same as hip pain?

Not necessarily.

The two conditions may create similar symptoms, which is why an accurate diagnosis is important.

Do all patients with low back pain need SI joint injections?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on the diagnosis, symptoms, imaging findings, previous treatments, and individual goals.
