Facet Injections & Medial Branch Blocks

Understanding Facet Injections & Medial Branch Blocks

Facet Injections and Medial Branch Blocks are commonly performed image-guided spine procedures used during the evaluation and treatment planning process for selected patients with neck pain, mid back pain, or low back pain.

Because several structures within the spine can contribute to pain, identifying the specific source of symptoms is often one of the most important steps in developing an effective treatment strategy.

Facet joints are one potential source of spinal pain and dysfunction.

Understanding these procedures can help patients better understand the evaluation process and available treatment options.

What Are Facet Joints?

Facet joints are small joints located throughout the spine.

These joints help:

  • Guide spinal movement
  • Provide stability
  • Support bending and rotation
  • Assist with normal spinal mechanics

Facet joints are present in:

Cervical Spine

The neck region.

Thoracic Spine

The mid-back region.

Lumbar Spine

The lower back region.

Like other joints in the body, facet joints may develop changes over time that can contribute to symptoms.

What Is A Facet Injection?

A facet injection is an image-guided procedure that targets a specific facet joint.

Using fluoroscopic guidance, medication is delivered into or around a targeted joint.

Facet injections may be discussed as part of a broader evaluation and treatment strategy.

The role of the procedure varies depending on the individual patient's diagnosis and symptoms.

What Is A Medial Branch Block?

Medial branch nerves are small nerves that provide sensation to the facet joints.

A medial branch block is an image-guided procedure that targets these nerves.

Rather than targeting the joint itself, the procedure focuses on the nerve supply associated with the facet joint.

Medial branch blocks are commonly discussed during the evaluation process for patients with suspected facet-related pain.

Why Are These Procedures Performed?

Several structures within the spine can create similar symptoms.

Facet injections and medial branch blocks may be discussed to help physicians better understand whether facet joints are contributing to a patient's pain.

These procedures may also play a role in determining whether additional treatment options should be considered.

Every patient is different, and treatment planning should always be individualized.

Conditions Commonly Evaluated

Facet-related procedures may be discussed in patients experiencing:

Chronic Neck Pain

Pain originating from structures in the cervical spine.

Mid Back Pain

Symptoms involving the thoracic spine.

Low Back Pain

Pain originating from structures in the lumbar spine.

Mechanical Spine Pain

Symptoms that may be aggravated by movement, extension, or rotation.

A comprehensive evaluation is necessary to determine whether facet joints may be contributing to symptoms.

How Are Facet Procedures Performed?

Although protocols vary, the general process may include:

  • Medical evaluation
  • Imaging review
  • Procedure planning
  • Skin preparation
  • Fluoroscopic guidance
  • Targeted needle placement
  • Procedure completion
  • Follow-up assessment

Specific procedural details vary depending on the spinal region and treatment plan.

Why Is Imaging Guidance Important?

Facet procedures are typically performed using fluoroscopy.

Fluoroscopic Guidance

Fluoroscopy provides real-time X-ray imaging during the procedure.

This allows the physician to visualize anatomy and guide needle placement accurately.

Image guidance is an important component of modern interventional spine procedures.

Facet Injections vs Medial Branch Blocks

Patients often hear both terms and wonder how they differ.

Facet Injection

Targets the facet joint itself.

Medial Branch Block

Targets the small nerves associated with the facet joint.

Both procedures may be discussed during the evaluation process depending on the diagnosis and treatment goals.

What Is The Relationship Between Medial Branch Blocks And RFA?

For some patients, medial branch blocks may be discussed as part of the evaluation process before considering:

Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

Radiofrequency ablation is a separate procedure that targets specific nerves associated with facet joints.

Not all patients who undergo medial branch blocks will be candidates for RFA.

Treatment recommendations depend on the diagnosis, clinical findings, and physician assessment.

What Happens After The Procedure?

Post-procedure recommendations vary.

Patients may receive guidance regarding:

  • Activity modifications
  • Follow-up appointments
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Future treatment discussions

Recommendations should always be individualized.

The Importance Of Diagnosis

Facet procedures are not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any spine procedure, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide the treatment strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are facet joints?

Facet joints are small joints located throughout the spine that help guide movement and provide stability.

What is a facet injection?

A facet injection is an image-guided procedure that targets a specific facet joint.

What is a medial branch block?

A medial branch block is an image-guided procedure that targets the small nerves associated with facet joints.

Are facet injections and medial branch blocks the same?

No.

Facet injections target the joint itself, while medial branch blocks target the nerves associated with the joint.

Why is fluoroscopy used?

Fluoroscopy provides real-time imaging that helps guide needle placement during the procedure.

Are medial branch blocks related to radiofrequency ablation?

Yes.

For selected patients, medial branch blocks may be part of the evaluation process before considering radiofrequency ablation.

Do all patients with back pain need facet procedures?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on the diagnosis, symptoms, imaging findings, previous treatments, and individual goals.
