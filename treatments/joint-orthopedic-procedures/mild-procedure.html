MILD® Procedure

Understanding The MILD® Procedure

Lumbar spinal stenosis is a common condition that may affect walking, standing, physical activity, and overall quality of life.

As people age, structural changes within the spine can contribute to narrowing around nerves and other spinal structures.

For selected patients with lumbar spinal stenosis, physicians may discuss a minimally invasive procedure known as the MILD® procedure.

Understanding what the procedure is—and how it fits into the treatment process—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is The MILD® Procedure?

MILD® stands for:

Minimally Invasive Lumbar Decompression

The procedure is designed to address specific anatomical structures within the lumbar spine that may contribute to narrowing associated with lumbar spinal stenosis.

The procedure is performed through a small incision using specialized instruments and imaging guidance.

The specific treatment plan depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Imaging findings
  • Clinical presentation
  • Previous treatments
  • Individual goals

What Is Lumbar Spinal Stenosis?

Lumbar spinal stenosis refers to narrowing within the lower spine.

This narrowing may develop because of age-related changes involving:

  • Ligaments
  • Joints
  • Discs
  • Other spinal structures

Symptoms vary from person to person.

A comprehensive evaluation is necessary to determine whether lumbar spinal stenosis may be contributing to symptoms.

What Is The Ligamentum Flavum?

The ligamentum flavum is a ligament located within the spinal canal.

As part of the natural aging process, this ligament may undergo structural changes.

In selected patients, physicians may evaluate whether these changes contribute to lumbar spinal stenosis.

The MILD® procedure is designed to address specific ligament-related anatomical changes in carefully selected patients.

Who May Be Evaluated For The MILD® Procedure?

The MILD® procedure may be discussed in selected patients with:

Lumbar Spinal Stenosis

Patients undergoing evaluation for narrowing within the lower spine.

Neurogenic Claudication

Symptoms that may occur during standing or walking and improve with sitting or forward flexion.

Chronic Low Back And Leg Symptoms

Selected patients with persistent symptoms despite previous treatment efforts.

Not every patient with spinal stenosis is a candidate for the procedure.

A comprehensive evaluation is essential.

How Is The MILD® Procedure Performed?

Although procedural details vary, the general process may include:

  • Medical evaluation
  • Imaging review
  • Procedure planning
  • Small incision placement
  • Imaging-guided decompression
  • Recovery and follow-up

Specific details vary depending on patient anatomy and physician recommendations.

Why Is Imaging Important?

Imaging plays an important role in determining whether a patient may be appropriate for the procedure.

This may include review of:

MRI Studies

Assessment of spinal anatomy and stenosis-related changes.

X-Rays

Evaluation of alignment and structural findings.

Other Imaging

Additional studies when clinically appropriate.

Imaging findings should always be interpreted within the context of the patient's symptoms and examination findings.

Conservative Treatment Before MILD®

Before discussing advanced procedures, patients often explore non-surgical options.

Depending on the diagnosis, discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modification
  • Medications
  • Epidural steroid injections
  • Rehabilitation strategies

Treatment recommendations should always be individualized.

MILD® vs Traditional Spine Surgery

Patients often ask how MILD® differs from traditional spine surgery.

The procedures are fundamentally different.

MILD®

A minimally invasive image-guided lumbar decompression procedure.

Traditional Spine Surgery

Surgical procedures that may involve different techniques, goals, and levels of intervention.

The most appropriate treatment option depends on the diagnosis, anatomy, symptoms, and individual goals.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

The MILD® procedure is a treatment option—not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any intervention, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Previous treatments
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide treatment decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does MILD® stand for?

MILD® stands for Minimally Invasive Lumbar Decompression.

What condition is the MILD® procedure associated with?

The procedure is commonly discussed in patients being evaluated for lumbar spinal stenosis.

Is MILD® considered surgery?

The procedure is minimally invasive and differs from traditional open spine surgery.

Patients should discuss procedural details with their physician.

What is neurogenic claudication?

Neurogenic claudication refers to a pattern of symptoms sometimes associated with lumbar spinal stenosis.

Does everyone with spinal stenosis qualify for MILD®?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on imaging findings, symptoms, physical examination findings, previous treatments, and individual goals.

Is imaging required?

Imaging studies are typically an important component of the evaluation process.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Successful spine care begins with understanding the source of symptoms.

Because lumbar spinal stenosis can affect patients differently, treatment decisions should always be individualized and based on a comprehensive evaluation.

For selected patients, the MILD® procedure may be one of several treatment options discussed as part of a broader treatment strategy.

The objective is always to help patients understand their diagnosis, review available options, and make informed decisions regarding their care.

Whether the goal is remaining active, walking more comfortably, traveling, working, or maintaining quality of life, treatment recommendations should be tailored to the individual.
