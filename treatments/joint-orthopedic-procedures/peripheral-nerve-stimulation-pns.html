Peripheral Nerve Stimulation (PNS)

Understanding Peripheral Nerve Stimulation

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation (PNS) is an advanced, minimally invasive procedure that may be discussed as part of a comprehensive treatment plan for selected patients with chronic pain.

PNS utilizes small electrical leads placed near specific nerves to deliver gentle stimulation.

Unlike procedures that target joints, discs, muscles, or tendons, peripheral nerve stimulation focuses on nerves that may be contributing to a patient's symptoms.

Because chronic pain can arise from many different structures, obtaining an accurate diagnosis is often the most important step before discussing advanced procedures such as PNS.

Understanding what peripheral nerve stimulation is—and how it may fit into the treatment process—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is Peripheral Nerve Stimulation?

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation is a minimally invasive procedure that uses mild electrical stimulation delivered near a targeted peripheral nerve.

Peripheral nerves travel throughout the body and help transmit information between the brain, spinal cord, muscles, joints, and other tissues.

For selected patients, physicians may discuss PNS as part of a broader treatment strategy.

The appropriateness of the procedure depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Symptom pattern
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Previous treatments
  • Individual goals

What Are Peripheral Nerves?

Peripheral nerves exist throughout the body and help:

  • Transmit sensory information
  • Support movement
  • Coordinate muscle activity
  • Communicate with tissues and organs

Different nerves serve different anatomical regions.

Because peripheral nerves are involved in many bodily functions, identifying the correct nerve is an important part of treatment planning.

How Does Peripheral Nerve Stimulation Work?

Peripheral nerve stimulation utilizes a small lead placed near a targeted nerve.

The lead delivers gentle electrical impulses generated by an external or implanted system.

The exact approach varies depending on:

  • The nerve being targeted
  • The diagnosis
  • The anatomical location
  • Physician preference
  • The specific stimulation system being utilized

Conditions Commonly Evaluated For PNS

Peripheral nerve stimulation may be discussed for selected patients with chronic pain conditions.

Examples may include:

Shoulder Pain

Selected patients with chronic shoulder-related symptoms.

Knee Pain

Selected patients with persistent knee pain.

Peripheral Nerve-Related Pain

Symptoms associated with specific peripheral nerve distributions.

Chronic Musculoskeletal Pain

Selected patients may undergo evaluation depending on their diagnosis and clinical presentation.

Not every patient with these conditions is a candidate for PNS.

A comprehensive evaluation is essential.

Temporary vs Long-Term Systems

Several peripheral nerve stimulation systems exist.

Temporary Systems

Some systems are designed for a defined treatment period.

Long-Term Systems

Other systems may be intended for longer-term use.

The type of system discussed depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Physician recommendations
  • Patient goals
  • Clinical circumstances

Why Is Imaging Guidance Important?

PNS procedures are commonly performed using imaging guidance.

Depending on the nerve being targeted, guidance may include:

Ultrasound

Provides real-time visualization of nerves and surrounding tissues.

Fluoroscopy

Provides real-time X-ray imaging.

The type of imaging utilized depends on the anatomical location and procedural goals.

How Is Peripheral Nerve Stimulation Performed?

Although protocols vary, the general process may include:

  • Medical evaluation
  • Physical examination
  • Imaging review
  • Procedure planning
  • Image-guided lead placement
  • System programming
  • Follow-up assessment

Specific procedural details vary depending on the treatment area and stimulation system.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

Peripheral nerve stimulation is a procedure—not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any advanced intervention, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Functional limitations
  • Imaging findings
  • Physical examination findings
  • Previous treatments
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide the treatment strategy.

What Happens After The Procedure?

Post-procedure recommendations vary.

Patients may receive guidance regarding:

  • Activity progression
  • Follow-up appointments
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Device management
  • Exercise programs

Recommendations should always be individualized.

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation vs Radiofrequency Ablation

Patients sometimes hear both procedures discussed and wonder how they differ.

Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)

Targets selected nerves using radiofrequency-generated thermal energy.

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation (PNS)

Utilizes electrical stimulation delivered through a lead placed near a targeted nerve.

These procedures serve different purposes and may be discussed in different clinical situations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Peripheral Nerve Stimulation?

Peripheral Nerve Stimulation is a minimally invasive procedure that delivers gentle electrical stimulation near a targeted peripheral nerve.

Is PNS surgery?

PNS is considered a minimally invasive image-guided procedure.

Specific procedural approaches vary depending on the stimulation system being utilized.

What conditions are commonly evaluated for PNS?

Selected patients with chronic shoulder pain, knee pain, peripheral nerve-related pain, and other chronic musculoskeletal conditions may undergo evaluation.

What imaging is used during PNS procedures?

Ultrasound and fluoroscopy are commonly used depending on the anatomical region being treated.

Is PNS the same as radiofrequency ablation?

No.

The procedures utilize different technologies and may be discussed in different clinical situations.

Does everyone with chronic pain qualify for PNS?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on diagnosis, symptoms, previous treatments, imaging findings, and individual goals.
