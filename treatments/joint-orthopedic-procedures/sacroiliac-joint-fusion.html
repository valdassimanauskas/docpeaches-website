Sacroiliac Joint Fusion

Understanding Sacroiliac Joint Fusion

The sacroiliac joint, commonly called the SI joint, connects the spine to the pelvis and plays an important role in transferring forces between the upper and lower body.

For some patients, the sacroiliac joint may be evaluated as a potential contributor to persistent lower back, buttock, pelvic, or hip-region symptoms.

When conservative treatment options have been explored and symptoms remain significant, physicians may discuss additional treatment approaches, including minimally invasive sacroiliac joint fusion procedures.

Understanding what SI joint fusion is—and how it fits into the treatment process—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is The Sacroiliac Joint?

Every person has two sacroiliac joints:

  • Right sacroiliac joint
  • Left sacroiliac joint

These joints connect:

  • The sacrum (base of the spine)
  • The pelvis

The SI joints help:

  • Transfer forces during movement
  • Support standing and walking
  • Provide stability
  • Assist with normal biomechanics

Although movement within the SI joint is relatively small, the joint plays an important role in everyday function.

What Is Sacroiliac Joint Fusion?

Sacroiliac joint fusion is a procedure designed to stabilize the SI joint.

Modern SI joint fusion procedures are typically performed using minimally invasive techniques and specialized instrumentation.

The specific approach varies depending on:

  • Patient anatomy
  • Diagnosis
  • Imaging findings
  • Physician recommendations
  • Individual treatment goals

Why Might SI Joint Fusion Be Discussed?

SI joint fusion is generally considered only after a comprehensive evaluation.

This may include:

  • Medical history
  • Physical examination
  • Imaging review
  • Functional assessment
  • Discussion of previous treatments

Not every patient with lower back pain or pelvic pain is a candidate for SI joint fusion.

Accurate diagnosis remains essential.

Conditions Commonly Evaluated

SI joint fusion may be discussed in selected patients being evaluated for:

Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction

Symptoms suspected to involve the SI joint.

Chronic Lower Back Pain

Persistent symptoms involving the lower back and pelvis.

Buttock Pain

Pain located near the posterior pelvis.

Pelvic Pain

Symptoms involving structures surrounding the sacroiliac region.

Persistent SI Joint Symptoms

Patients who continue to experience symptoms despite previous treatment efforts.

A thorough evaluation is necessary to determine whether the SI joint may be contributing to symptoms.

How Is The SI Joint Evaluated?

Because multiple structures can produce similar symptoms, diagnosis typically requires a combination of:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, activity limitations, and previous treatments.

Physical Examination

Assessment of movement patterns and clinical findings.

Imaging Studies

Review of X-rays, CT scans, MRI studies, or other imaging when appropriate.

Diagnostic Procedures

Selected patients may undergo image-guided procedures as part of the evaluation process.

The diagnosis should guide treatment decisions.

Minimally Invasive SI Joint Fusion

Advances in surgical technology have led to the development of minimally invasive SI joint fusion techniques.

Compared with traditional surgical approaches, modern techniques typically utilize smaller incisions and specialized instrumentation.

The specific procedure depends on the surgeon's training, experience, and treatment philosophy.

Conservative Treatment Before Fusion

Before discussing fusion procedures, patients often explore non-surgical options.

Depending on the diagnosis, discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Rehabilitation
  • Image-guided SI joint injections
  • Other interventional procedures

Every treatment plan should be individualized.

The Importance Of Diagnosis

SI joint fusion is a procedure—not a diagnosis.

Before discussing surgery, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Previous treatment history
  • Patient goals

The most appropriate treatment strategy should always be based on an accurate diagnosis.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the sacroiliac joint?

The sacroiliac joint connects the spine and pelvis and helps transfer forces throughout the body.

What is SI joint fusion?

SI joint fusion is a procedure designed to stabilize the sacroiliac joint using specialized surgical techniques.

Is SI joint fusion considered surgery?

Yes.

SI joint fusion is a surgical procedure.

Does everyone with SI joint pain need fusion?

No.

Most patients undergo a comprehensive evaluation and discussion of treatment options before surgical procedures are considered.

How is SI joint dysfunction diagnosed?

Diagnosis may involve medical history, physical examination, imaging studies, and selected diagnostic procedures.

Can SI joint symptoms resemble hip or back pain?

Yes.

Symptoms involving the SI joint often overlap with conditions involving the lumbar spine, pelvis, and hip.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Successful treatment begins with an accurate diagnosis.

Because lower back pain, pelvic pain, buttock pain, and hip-region symptoms often overlap, identifying the true source of symptoms is one of the most important steps in treatment planning.

For selected patients, sacroiliac joint fusion may be one of several treatment options discussed after a comprehensive evaluation.

The objective is always to help patients understand their diagnosis, review available options, and make informed decisions regarding their care.

Whether the goal is returning to work, remaining active, participating in sports, traveling comfortably, or maintaining quality of life, treatment decisions should be individualized.
