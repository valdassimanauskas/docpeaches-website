Epidural Steroid Injections (ESI)

Understanding Epidural Steroid Injections

Epidural Steroid Injections (ESIs) are among the most commonly performed spine procedures in interventional orthopedic and spine medicine.

These image-guided procedures may be discussed as part of a comprehensive treatment plan for selected patients experiencing neck pain, arm pain, back pain, leg pain, or symptoms involving irritated spinal nerves.

Understanding what an epidural injection is—and when it may be considered—can help patients make informed decisions regarding their care.

What Is An Epidural Steroid Injection?

An epidural steroid injection is a procedure performed within the epidural space of the spine.

The epidural space surrounds the spinal nerves as they travel through the spinal canal.

Using imaging guidance, medication is delivered into a targeted area near the spinal nerves.

The specific approach depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Imaging findings
  • Symptom location
  • Physician recommendations
  • Individual anatomy

What Is The Epidural Space?

The epidural space is a region surrounding the protective structures of the spinal cord and spinal nerves.

This space extends throughout the spine and may be accessed in different regions depending on the patient's symptoms and diagnosis.

Why Are Epidural Injections Performed?

Epidural injections may be discussed in patients with symptoms that appear to involve spinal nerves.

These procedures may be considered as part of a broader treatment strategy that could include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Activity modifications
  • Medications
  • Other interventional procedures

The role of an epidural injection varies from patient to patient.

Conditions Commonly Evaluated For Epidural Injections

Epidural injections may be discussed in patients with conditions such as:

Cervical Radiculopathy

Symptoms involving the neck, shoulder, arm, or hand.

Lumbar Radiculopathy

Symptoms involving the low back, buttock, leg, or foot.

Sciatica

Pain that travels along the course of the sciatic nerve.

Disc Herniation

Structural changes involving spinal discs that may affect nearby nerves.

Spinal Stenosis

Narrowing around spinal nerves that may contribute to symptoms.

Degenerative Spine Conditions

Age-related changes that may affect nerve structures.

Not every patient with these diagnoses is a candidate for an epidural procedure.

A comprehensive evaluation is essential.

Types Of Epidural Steroid Injections

Several epidural approaches exist.

Cervical Epidural Injections

Performed in the neck region.

May be discussed in patients experiencing symptoms involving the neck, shoulder, arm, or hand.

Thoracic Epidural Injections

Performed in the mid-back region.

Less commonly performed than cervical or lumbar procedures.

Lumbar Epidural Injections

Performed in the lower back region.

May be discussed in patients experiencing symptoms involving the low back, buttock, leg, or foot.

Why Is Imaging Guidance Important?

Epidural injections are typically performed using fluoroscopic guidance.

Fluoroscopy

Fluoroscopy provides real-time X-ray imaging during the procedure.

This allows the physician to visualize anatomy and confirm appropriate needle placement.

Image guidance is an important component of modern spine procedures.

What Happens During An Epidural Injection?

Although protocols vary, the general process may include:

  • Evaluation and diagnosis
  • Imaging review
  • Procedure planning
  • Skin preparation
  • Image-guided needle placement
  • Medication delivery
  • Recovery and follow-up

Specific procedural details vary depending on the location being treated and the individual treatment plan.

What Happens After The Procedure?

Post-procedure recommendations vary.

Patients may receive guidance regarding:

  • Activity modifications
  • Exercise progression
  • Follow-up appointments
  • Rehabilitation planning

Recovery recommendations should always be individualized.

The Importance Of Diagnosis

An epidural injection is not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any spine procedure, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Physical examination findings
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Patient goals

The diagnosis should guide the treatment strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an epidural steroid injection?

An epidural steroid injection is a procedure performed within the epidural space of the spine using imaging guidance.

Is an epidural injection surgery?

No.

Epidural injections are minimally invasive procedures performed without surgical incisions.

What conditions are commonly evaluated for epidural injections?

Conditions may include cervical radiculopathy, lumbar radiculopathy, sciatica, disc herniation, and spinal stenosis.

Why is fluoroscopy used?

Fluoroscopy provides real-time imaging that helps guide needle placement during the procedure.

Are epidural injections performed in different parts of the spine?

Yes.

Procedures may be performed in the cervical, thoracic, or lumbar spine depending on the diagnosis and symptom location.

Do all patients with back pain need an epidural injection?

No.

Treatment recommendations depend on the diagnosis, symptoms, imaging findings, previous treatments, and individual goals.
