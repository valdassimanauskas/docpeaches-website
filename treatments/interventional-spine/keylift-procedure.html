KeyLift™ Procedure

Understanding The KeyLift™ Procedure

Lumbar spinal stenosis is a common condition that can affect walking, standing, exercise, and everyday activities.

As the spine ages, structural changes involving discs, ligaments, joints, and other spinal structures may contribute to narrowing within the spinal canal and surrounding spaces.

For selected patients, physicians may discuss advanced minimally invasive procedures designed to address specific anatomical changes associated with lumbar spinal stenosis.

One such option is the KeyLift™ Procedure.

The KeyLift™ Procedure utilizes an expandable interlaminar stabilization system that is designed to be implanted within the posterior spine as part of a surgical treatment strategy in carefully selected patients. The device is FDA-cleared and intended for use in specific spinal conditions as determined by a treating physician.

At Dr. Peaches, treatment recommendations are individualized and based on symptoms, imaging findings, functional goals, and overall patient health.

What Is Lumbar Spinal Stenosis?

Lumbar spinal stenosis refers to narrowing within the lower spine.

This narrowing may involve:

  • The spinal canal
  • Nerve passageways
  • Surrounding spinal structures

Patients may experience symptoms that affect standing, walking, physical activity, and quality of life.

Because multiple spinal conditions can produce similar symptoms, accurate diagnosis remains essential.

What Is The KeyLift™ Procedure?

The KeyLift™ Procedure utilizes an expandable interlaminar stabilization implant that is placed between specific structures of the spine during a minimally invasive surgical procedure.

The device is designed to provide supplemental stabilization and is intended for use in selected spinal conditions when determined appropriate by a treating surgeon. The implant is not intended to be used as a stand-alone device.

The specific surgical approach depends on:

  • Diagnosis
  • Imaging findings
  • Patient anatomy
  • Physician recommendations
  • Individual treatment goals

Why Might The KeyLift™ Procedure Be Discussed?

The KeyLift™ system may be discussed in selected patients being evaluated for conditions such as:

Lumbar Spinal Stenosis

Narrowing within the lower spine that may affect nearby nerves.

Degenerative Disc Disease

Age-related changes involving spinal discs.

Spondylolisthesis

A condition involving vertebral alignment changes.

Disc-Related Degeneration

Structural changes involving discs and supporting structures.

Chronic Lower Back Symptoms

Persistent symptoms that continue despite conservative treatment efforts.

Not every patient with these conditions is a candidate for the KeyLift™ Procedure.

A comprehensive evaluation is required.

Common Symptoms Associated With Lumbar Spinal Stenosis

Patients undergoing evaluation may report:

Low Back Pain

Persistent symptoms involving the lumbar spine.

Leg Pain

Pain radiating into one or both legs.

Neurogenic Claudication

Symptoms that occur during standing or walking and may improve with sitting or forward flexion.

Numbness Or Tingling

Altered sensation involving the lower extremities.

Reduced Walking Tolerance

Difficulty standing or walking for prolonged periods.

Functional Limitations

Challenges participating in work, exercise, sports, or daily activities.

How Is Lumbar Spinal Stenosis Evaluated?

Evaluation often includes:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, treatment history, and functional limitations.

Physical Examination

Assessment of movement, strength, sensation, and mobility.

MRI

Detailed imaging of spinal anatomy and nerve structures.

Additional Imaging

X-rays, CT scans, or other studies when appropriate.

The diagnosis should always guide treatment decisions.

Conservative Treatment Before Surgery

Before discussing advanced surgical procedures, many patients explore:

Physical Therapy

Exercise-based rehabilitation and mobility training.

Activity Modification

Adjustments designed to reduce symptom aggravation.

Medications

When appropriate as part of a broader treatment plan.

Epidural Steroid Injections

Image-guided procedures commonly discussed for selected patients.

Interventional Spine Care

Additional diagnostic and therapeutic procedures when appropriate.

Treatment recommendations should always be individualized.

Who May Be Evaluated For The KeyLift™ Procedure?

Selected patients may be evaluated when:

  • Lumbar spinal stenosis is identified
  • Symptoms significantly affect quality of life
  • Conservative treatment has been explored
  • Imaging findings support procedural consideration

The decision to discuss surgery should always be based on a comprehensive evaluation.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

The KeyLift™ Procedure is a treatment option—not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any surgical procedure, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Previous treatment history
  • Patient goals

Successful treatment planning begins with accurate diagnosis.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the KeyLift™ Procedure?

The KeyLift™ Procedure utilizes an expandable interlaminar stabilization system used in selected spinal procedures as determined by a treating physician.

What conditions may be evaluated for the KeyLift™ Procedure?

The system may be discussed in selected patients with lumbar spinal stenosis, degenerative disc disease, spondylolisthesis, and related spinal conditions.

Is the KeyLift™ Procedure considered surgery?

Yes.

The KeyLift™ Procedure is a surgical procedure performed by appropriately trained spine surgeons.

Does every patient with spinal stenosis need surgery?

No.

Many patients improve with conservative treatment and never require surgical intervention.

What imaging is used during evaluation?

MRI is commonly used to evaluate spinal anatomy, stenosis, nerve compression, and degenerative changes.

How do I know if I am a candidate?

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine whether surgical consultation should be considered based on symptoms, imaging findings, treatment history, and patient goals.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Lumbar spinal stenosis and degenerative spinal conditions can significantly affect mobility, physical activity, and quality of life.

Not every patient requires surgery, and not every patient with spinal stenosis is a candidate for advanced procedures.

Successful treatment begins with identifying the source of symptoms and developing an individualized treatment strategy.

For carefully selected patients, the KeyLift™ Procedure may be one of several treatment options discussed as part of a comprehensive spine care plan.

The goal is to help patients understand their diagnosis, review available options, and make informed decisions regarding their care.

Whether the objective is returning to work, staying active, participating in sports, improving walking tolerance, or maintaining independence, treatment recommendations should always be individualized.
