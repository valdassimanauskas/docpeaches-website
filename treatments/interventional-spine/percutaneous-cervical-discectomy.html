Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy

Understanding Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy

The cervical spine, commonly referred to as the neck, contains discs that act as cushions between the vertebrae. These discs help support movement, flexibility, and shock absorption.

Over time, injury, degeneration, or disc herniation may place pressure on nearby nerves and contribute to neck pain, arm pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness.

While many patients improve with conservative treatment, some individuals continue to experience symptoms that significantly affect their quality of life.

For selected patients, physicians may discuss minimally invasive procedures designed to address disc-related conditions.

One such procedure is Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy.

Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy is a minimally invasive spine procedure that utilizes specialized instruments and image guidance to access the cervical disc through a small skin puncture rather than a traditional open surgical approach.

At Dr. Peaches, treatment recommendations are individualized based on symptoms, imaging findings, functional limitations, and patient goals.

What Is The Cervical Spine?

The cervical spine consists of seven vertebrae located in the neck.

Between each vertebra is an intervertebral disc that helps:

  • Absorb forces
  • Maintain flexibility
  • Support movement
  • Protect surrounding structures

When disc abnormalities develop, nearby nerves may become irritated or compressed.

What Is A Cervical Disc Herniation?

A cervical disc herniation occurs when disc material extends beyond its normal boundaries.

This may contribute to:

  • Neck pain
  • Arm pain
  • Shoulder pain
  • Numbness
  • Tingling
  • Weakness

The severity and location of symptoms vary depending on the structures involved.

What Is Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy?

Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy is a minimally invasive procedure designed to access the cervical disc through a small percutaneous approach.

The procedure is performed using imaging guidance and specialized instrumentation.

The exact technique depends on:

  • Disc anatomy
  • Imaging findings
  • Symptom location
  • Physician recommendations
  • Individual patient circumstances

Not every patient with a cervical disc condition is a candidate for this procedure.

Why Might Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy Be Discussed?

Selected patients may be evaluated for:

Cervical Disc Herniation

Disc material affecting nearby nerves.

Cervical Radiculopathy

Nerve irritation originating from the neck.

Arm Pain

Symptoms radiating into the shoulder, arm, or hand.

Disc-Related Neck Pain

Pain associated with cervical disc abnormalities.

Persistent Symptoms

Symptoms that continue despite conservative treatment.

A comprehensive evaluation is necessary to determine whether procedural intervention should be discussed.

Common Symptoms

Patients may report:

Neck Pain

Persistent discomfort involving the cervical spine.

Arm Pain

Pain radiating into the upper extremity.

Shoulder Pain

Pain that may originate from cervical nerve irritation.

Numbness Or Tingling

Changes in sensation affecting the arm or hand.

Weakness

Reduced strength involving the shoulder, arm, or hand.

Difficulty With Daily Activities

Limitations involving work, exercise, sports, or routine activities.

How Are Cervical Disc Conditions Diagnosed?

Evaluation typically includes:

Medical History

Review of symptoms, prior treatments, and functional limitations.

Physical Examination

Assessment of:

  • Strength
  • Sensation
  • Reflexes
  • Range of motion
  • Neurological function

MRI

MRI is commonly utilized to evaluate:

  • Disc herniations
  • Nerve compression
  • Cervical anatomy

Additional Imaging

Additional studies may be obtained when appropriate.

Medications

Image-guided procedures that may be discussed in selected patients.

Other Interventional Treatments

Depending on the diagnosis and clinical presentation.

Who May Be Evaluated For Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy?

Selected patients may be evaluated when:

  • Symptoms significantly affect quality of life
  • Conservative treatment has been explored
  • Imaging findings correlate with symptoms
  • Disc-related pathology is identified

The decision to discuss procedural intervention should always be based on a comprehensive evaluation.

Why Is Diagnosis Important?

Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy is a procedure—not a diagnosis.

Before discussing any intervention, it is important to understand:

  • The source of symptoms
  • Imaging findings
  • Functional limitations
  • Previous treatment history
  • Patient goals

Successful treatment begins with accurate diagnosis.

Potential Advantages Of Minimally Invasive Spine Procedures

Minimally invasive techniques are designed to minimize disruption to surrounding tissues.

Depending on the procedure and patient circumstances, potential advantages may include:

  • Smaller incisions
  • Targeted treatment
  • Reduced tissue disruption
  • Outpatient treatment in selected cases

Treatment outcomes vary from patient to patient.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy?

Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy is a minimally invasive procedure used to access and treat selected cervical disc conditions through a small skin puncture using image guidance.

What conditions may be evaluated for Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy?

The procedure may be discussed in selected patients with cervical disc herniations, cervical radiculopathy, neck pain, and arm pain.

Is Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy considered surgery?

It is considered a minimally invasive spine procedure that differs from traditional open cervical surgery.

Does every cervical disc herniation require surgery?

No.

Many patients improve with conservative treatment and never require procedural intervention.

What imaging is used during evaluation?

MRI is commonly used to evaluate cervical discs, nerves, and surrounding spinal structures.

How do I know if I am a candidate?

A comprehensive evaluation helps determine whether procedural intervention should be discussed based on symptoms, imaging findings, treatment history, and individual goals.

The Dr. Peaches Perspective

Neck pain and cervical nerve-related symptoms can significantly affect work, exercise, sleep, and quality of life.

Not every patient requires surgery, and not every disc abnormality seen on MRI is responsible for symptoms.

Successful treatment begins with understanding the true source of pain and developing an individualized treatment strategy.

For selected patients, Percutaneous Cervical Discectomy may be one of several minimally invasive procedures discussed as part of a comprehensive cervical spine treatment plan.

The goal is to help patients understand their diagnosis, review available options, and make informed decisions regarding their care.

Whether the objective is returning to work, staying active, participating in sports, improving function, or maintaining quality of life, treatment recommendations should always be individualized.
