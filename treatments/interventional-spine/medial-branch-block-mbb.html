Medial Branch Block (MBB)

Understanding Medial Branch Blocks (MBB)

Back pain and neck pain can originate from many different structures within the spine. One potential source of pain involves the small joints located between the vertebrae, commonly known as facet joints.

When physicians suspect that facet joints may be contributing to symptoms, a procedure called a Medial Branch Block (MBB) may become part of the diagnostic evaluation process.

Understanding what an MBB is and how it fits into spine care can help patients better understand their diagnosis and treatment options.

What Is A Medial Branch Block?

A Medial Branch Block, commonly referred to as an MBB, is an image-guided procedure involving small nerves known as medial branch nerves.

These nerves help transmit signals from the facet joints of the spine.

Because facet joints can sometimes contribute to neck pain or back pain, physicians may utilize medial branch blocks as part of a comprehensive evaluation when determining the source of symptoms.

What Are Facet Joints?

Facet joints are small joints located along the back portion of the spine.

They help:

  • Guide spinal movement
  • Provide stability
  • Support flexibility
  • Assist with normal spinal function

Like other joints in the body, facet joints may undergo age-related changes, injury-related changes, or degenerative processes over time.

Why Might An MBB Be Considered?

A physician may discuss an MBB when evaluating patients with symptoms that appear consistent with facet-mediated pain.

This may include:

  • Chronic neck pain
  • Chronic back pain
  • Pain with spinal extension
  • Pain associated with facet joint degeneration
  • Certain types of mechanical spine pain

The decision to perform an MBB depends on the overall clinical picture and individual patient evaluation.

Is An MBB A Diagnostic Procedure?

One of the unique aspects of an MBB is that it is commonly used as a diagnostic tool.

In many situations, physicians use medial branch blocks to gather additional information regarding whether facet joints may be contributing to symptoms.

Because spine pain can have multiple potential causes, obtaining accurate diagnostic information is an important step in developing an individualized treatment strategy.

How Is An MBB Performed?

Medial branch blocks are typically performed using imaging guidance to assist with accurate placement.

The specifics of the procedure may vary depending on:

  • The area of the spine being evaluated
  • The patient's anatomy
  • The physician's clinical approach
  • The overall treatment plan

Patients should discuss procedural details directly with their treating physician.

Cervical, Thoracic, and Lumbar MBB

Medial branch blocks may be performed in different regions of the spine, including:

Cervical Spine

The neck region.

Thoracic Spine

The mid-back region.

Lumbar Spine

The lower back region.

The location depends on the patient's symptoms, examination findings, and imaging results.

The Importance Of Proper Diagnosis

One of the most important principles in spine care is identifying the true source of symptoms.

Back pain and neck pain can originate from:

  • Discs
  • Nerves
  • Facet joints
  • Muscles
  • Ligaments
  • Sacroiliac joints
  • Other spinal structures

Because many conditions produce similar symptoms, diagnostic procedures may play an important role in helping physicians better understand the source of pain.

Understanding Treatment Pathways

An MBB is often one component of a larger diagnostic and treatment strategy.

Depending on the diagnosis, treatment discussions may include:

  • Physical therapy
  • Exercise programs
  • Lifestyle modifications
  • Medications
  • Epidural injections
  • Facet joint procedures
  • Radiofrequency ablation (RFA)
  • Regenerative medicine discussions
  • Surgical consultation when appropriate

The goal is always to create an individualized plan based on the patient's specific needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does MBB stand for?

MBB stands for Medial Branch Block.

What is the purpose of an MBB?

An MBB is commonly used as part of the diagnostic evaluation process when physicians are investigating whether facet joints may be contributing to symptoms.

Is an MBB a treatment or a diagnostic procedure?

In many situations, an MBB is performed primarily as a diagnostic tool to gather information regarding the source of pain.

What are facet joints?

Facet joints are small joints located along the spine that help guide movement and provide stability.

Can an MBB be performed in the neck and lower back?

Yes.

MBBs may be performed in the cervical, thoracic, or lumbar regions depending on the patient's symptoms and diagnosis.

Should I bring my MRI to my consultation?

Yes.

MRI studies, imaging reports, and prior treatment records can provide valuable information during the evaluation process.
